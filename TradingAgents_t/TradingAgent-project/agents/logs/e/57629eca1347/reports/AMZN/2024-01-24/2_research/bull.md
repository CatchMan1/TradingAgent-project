
Bull Analyst: **Bull Opening — AMZN (2024-01-24)**

I’m taking the bull side on **AMZN**, and I’ll start with the punchline: this is not a “buy it because it’s up” call. It’s a **margin-and-free-cash-flow inflection story with a technical breakout attached**. The bear hasn’t spoken yet, so let me pre-but the obvious objections, because the data here is stronger than the trailing valuation headline suggests.

### 1. The fundamental core: AMZN’s profitability inflection is real and broad-based

The single most important number in the fundamental package is this: **Q3 FY2023 operating income was $11.188B, up 343% y/y, on a 7.82% operating margin versus 1.99% a year ago.** For the nine months, operating income more than doubled to **$23.643B**, and operating margin expanded from **2.61% to 5.84%**.

That is not a one-line accounting trick. The margin bridge shows up across the P&L:

- **COGS leverage:** +289 bp, as gross margin rose to **47.6% from 44.7%**.
- **Fulfillment/tech/content:** grew only **8.8%** while revenue grew **12.6%** — the largest cost pool is now growing slower than the top line.
- **Marketing:** down **4.2% y/y** while revenue rose 12.6%.
- **G&A:** down **16.3% y/y**.

That is operating leverage. That is scalability. And it is exactly what you want to see in a company with AMZN’s scale.

### 2. The FCF turn is the real bull catalyst

Bear will probably say, “Sure, margins improved, but this is still a capex machine.” Fair concern — but look at what actually happened:

- **9M FY2023 free cash flow: +$4.340B**, versus **–$29.474B** in 9M FY2022. That’s a **+$33.8B swing**.
- **Q3 alone: +$8.738B FCF**, versus **–$4.974B** a year ago.
- **TTM FCF: +$16.921B**, versus negative a year ago.

And importantly, this was not just a capex-cut story. In the nine-month FCF swing, **operating cash flow rose ~$24.9B** while capex fell ~$8.9B. OCF did most of the work. Working capital was actually a **drag**, not a tailwind — accounts payable fell $7.6B. So the FCF improvement is earnings-led and conservative, not payables-stuffing.

### 3. Balance sheet is a fortress, and AMZN is deleveraging

AMZN has roughly **$99.5B of cash and securities** against **$79.9B of debt plus finance leases** — about **$19.5B of net cash** excluding operating leases. Total debt plus finance leases fell **$6.4B** from Dec-22 to Sep-23. AMZN issued **zero long-term debt in 2023**. This is not a stretched balance sheet. This is a company self-funding its investment cycle.

Bear may complain about no buyback and no dividend. I’d argue that’s the right call at this stage: deleverage, fund the capex cycle, and preserve optionality. A buyback is not the catalyst here — **margin durability and FCF conversion** are.

### 4. Competitive advantages: the flywheel is still the flywheel

The filings confirm three operating segments: **North America retail, International retail, and AWS**. The cost structure also implies the high-margin layers — third-party seller services and advertising — are becoming more important, which is visible in the gross-margin expansion to **47.6%**.

The bear case has to explain why a company with AMZN’s scale in logistics, cloud, and marketplace services cannot keep compounding profitability. The evidence says the opposite: the cost reset is showing up, the largest cost pool is growing slower than revenue, and the high-margin mix is lifting gross profit faster than revenue — **gross profit rose 19.8% on 12.6% revenue growth in Q3**. That is the flywheel working.

And there is optionality the market may not fully price until the Q4 print: AWS/AI and advertising economics. The data package does not give us segment-level detail, so I won’t fabricate it — but the Q4/FY2023 report should be the first fuller look at segment economics after the cost reset. That’s upside optionality, not a requirement for the current thesis.

The tape is not fighting us. On 2024-01-24, **AMZN** printed a fresh multi-month high of **$158.51** and closed at **$156.87**, above the prior December high. The moving-average stack is textbook:

Bear will point to the MACD lower peak versus December as a divergence. I’ll acknowledge it — but it is **not confirmed**. The histogram is still positive and expanding, RSI is not stretched, and price is only about **1.3% below the upper Bollinger band** at **$158.98**. This is a breakout setup, not an exhaustion signal. It argues for staged entries, not for stepping aside.

### 6. Direct bear rebuttals

**Bear: “The stock is overbought near the upper Bollinger band.”**  
Bull: RSI is **63**, not 75–80. Price is **1.3% below** the upper band, not riding it. ATR is lower, not spiking. This is an orderly trend continuation, and a decisive close above **$158.51–$158.98** would confirm it.

**Bear: “Margins peaked at 7.82% and will mean-revert.”**  
Bull: The nine-month margin is **5.84%**, so AMZN doesn’t need 7.82% every quarter to justify the trajectory. The improvement is spread across COGS, fulfillment, marketing, and G&A. The Q4 comp is also easy versus Q4’22’s weak operating income base. Margin normalization is a risk to monitor — but the burden is on the bear to show why this broad-based cost reset reverses immediately.

**Bear: “FCF is just capex cuts.”**  
Bull: OCF drove roughly **74%** of the nine-month FCF swing. Capex discipline helped, but the cash engine is earnings. And working capital was a **headwind**, with AP down $7.6B. That makes the FCF turn higher quality, not lower.

**Bear: “SBC is huge — 75% of operating income.”**  
Bull: True, and I’m not ignoring it. But SBC grew **26%** while operating income grew **149%**. SBC is **4.4% of revenue**, and share count rose only **0.86% in nine months**. It’s a real cost, but it’s embedded in GAAP and does not invalidate the cash-flow inflection. Monitor it; don’t let it override the operating leverage.

**Bear: “Valuation is expensive.”**  
Bull: I can’t compute a P/E from this package, and I won’t pretend to. But the bear’s valuation argument rests on trailing EPS of roughly **$1.92**, which is exactly the wrong anchor for a margin-inflection story. Q3 operating income rose **343%**, nine-month operating income rose **149%**, and FCF swung positive. If the Q4 print validates the margin trajectory, forward earnings power moves up. That’s the debate.

**Bear: “Q4 is a binary event.”**  
Bull: Correct — and that’s why I’m not advocating a blind chase. But the setup into the print is favorable: easy y/y comp, cost reset visible in the P&L, FCF positive, balance sheet net cash, and a technical breakout. The right response is staged risk, not bearish paralysis.

### 7. My AMZN recommendation

**Bias: Bullish / Overweight AMZN.** I’d prefer staged entries:

- **Pullback entry:** **$154–$152** confluence — 10 EMA, Bollinger mid, and VWMA.
- **Momentum add:** confirmed hold above **$158.51–$158.98** with expanding volume.
- **Risk:** tight stop under **$152.28**; conservative stop under **$149.28**.
- **Targets:** **$158.5–$159.0**, then the **$160** psychological area, with measured extension if the breakout holds.

The bear has to explain why a company growing revenue **12.6%**, expanding Q3 operating margin from **1.99% to 7.82%**, swinging FCF from **–$29.5B to +$4.3B** over nine months, sitting on **net cash**, and breaking to new highs is a sell. The evidence says **AMZN** is in the early innings of a profitability and FCF re-rating. I’m on the bull side.
