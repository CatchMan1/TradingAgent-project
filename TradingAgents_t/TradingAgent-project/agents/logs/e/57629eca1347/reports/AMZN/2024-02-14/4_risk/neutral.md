Revenue growth of 11.8%, gross margin of 47.0%, operating margin of 6.41%, and operating income of $36.9B are real reported facts, not narrative.

But the aggressive analyst is too relaxed about what the current price and cash-flow quality justify. Saying AMZN is in a volume-confirmed uptrend does not automatically make a 1.25x overweight prudent. The aggressive case keeps calling valuation a sizing tool, but then supports the full 1.25x target anyway. That is the inconsistency. At roughly 59x trailing EPS, 52x headline EV/FCF, a 1.8% headline FCF yield, and a 0.46% SBC-adjusted FCF yield, AMZN is not cheap. Strong names often do not give you the perfect retest. The conservative analyst says missing upside is acceptable, and that is true, but carrying a small starter position is a reasonable way to balance that opportunity cost without embracing the aggressive overweight.

The conservative side is also a little too dismissive of the reported operating inflection. SBC is a real haircut, and the FCF peak is a real concern, but AMZN still generated $84.9B of operating cash flow and $8.2B of SBC-adjusted FCF. The right conclusion is not “avoid AMZN” or “cap at standard forever.” The right conclusion is “own AMZN, but earn the overweight.” That is the moderate path.
