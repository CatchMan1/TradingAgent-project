## 2. The FCF swing is real, but it is more levered and lower-quality than you admit

You keep citing the **$49B YoY FCF swing** — from **–$16.9B to +$32.2B**. Fair. But decompose it.

FCF = OCF of **$84.9B** minus capex of **$52.7B**. Capex fell **$10.9B**, or **17.2%**, to **9.2% of revenue**. That alone is a huge part of the swing. And capex/D&A is now **1.08x** versus **1.52x** in FY2022. That means the depreciation tailwind is fading and AMZN is spending barely above maintenance depreciation. If capex normalizes:

- At **1.3x D&A**, capex rises by roughly **$10.5B**, and FCF falls to about **$21.7B**.
- At FY22’s **1.52x D&A**, capex rises by roughly **$21.2B**, and FCF falls to about **$11B**.

So the “$32B FCF” is highly sensitive to the very capex discipline you’re assuming persists. And if AI infrastructure spending re-accelerates — which you wave away as “good” — it may be good for 2030 growth and bad for the 2024 FCF multiple the market just re-rated.

Then there’s SBC. **$24.0B**, or **4.2% of revenue** and **79% of net income**. Shares grew **1.4%**, there was **zero buyback**, and AMZN still has **$18.3B of unrecognized SBC** to expense. Post-SBC FCF is only **~$8.2B**. You call that “still positive.” I call it a thin cushion for a stock at 55x headline FCF.

You also say the cash-flow beat was broad-based. Partly true — operating income rose sharply. But OCF benefited from D&A/SBC addbacks and working capital: deferred revenue **+$4.6B**, payables **+$5.4B**.
