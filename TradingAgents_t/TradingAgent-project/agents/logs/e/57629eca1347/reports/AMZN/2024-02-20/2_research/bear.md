### 3. The fundamental story is good — and already public

Your one-sentence bull thesis is backward-looking. The market already knows FY2023 revenue grew +11.8%, operating margin hit 6.41%, and FCF hit +$32.2B. That 10-K was accepted 2024-02-02. The stock gapped on it. You cannot call that an undiscovered catalyst.

- **FCF durability is not proven.** The +$49.1B FCF swing came from OCF +81.7% **and capex −17.1%**. Capex/D&A fell to 1.08x from 1.77x. That is a completed-overbuild signature, yes — but the report’s #1 swing factor is FY2024 capex re-acceleration for AI. If capex/D&A pushes back above ~1.3x, the FCF inflection weakens materially.

- **Leases are the real leverage.** Operating + finance leases are **$87.7B**, larger than the $58.3B of long-term debt. Total obligations are **$300.4B**, with **$157.8B beyond year 5**. You can call it a laddered tail, not a cliff — fair. But “fortress balance sheet” is overstated when adjusted obligations are ~$146B gross.

But the report explicitly says segment revenue and AWS operating income were **not returned**. That is the biggest analytical gap. We cannot decompose whether FY2023 margin expansion came from AWS mix, retail cost cuts, fulfillment regionalization, or one-off cost optimization. You are asserting a durable competitive advantage from a group-level margin print while the key segment proof is missing.

That matters for **AMZN** because AWS is identified as the principal profit engine. If group margins are being driven by cost discipline in low-margin retail rather than durable AWS economics, the bull case has a shorter half-life. The retail model remains capital-intensive, with costs at **93.6% of revenue**. The moat may be real, but the datapoint you’re using doesn’t prove it.
