- Revenue: **$574.8B, +11.8% YoY**
- Gross margin: **47.0%, +320bp**

- Operating margin: **6.41%, +403bp**
- Operating cash flow: **$84.9B, +81.7%**
- Free cash flow: **+$32.2B** versus **–$16.9B** in FY2022

And this was not a back-half accounting trick. Derived from the filing, AMZN generated roughly **$42.5B of operating cash flow in Q4 alone** — about the same as the first nine months combined — and roughly **$27.9B of Q4 free cash flow**. The cash generation ramp is steep, recent, and directly in front of the market.

The bear’s best fundamental counter is: “That FCF came from capex cuts, not durable improvement.” But that misses the margin story. AMZN’s cost of sales fell to **53.0% of revenue** from **56.2%** in FY2022 and **58.0%** in FY2021. Gross margin expanded **320bp** while the combined fulfillment/technology/content bucket stayed roughly flat as a percentage of revenue. That is operating leverage from logistics regionalization, cost-of-sales efficiency, and mix — not just a capex holiday. Capex discipline helped, but the gross-margin expansion is structural and should persist.

- The fulfillment and logistics network is driving cost-of-sales leverage.

- The balance sheet now has enormous optionality: **~$144.3B in cash and marketable securities** against **$58.3B of long-term debt** and **$12.1B of finance leases** — roughly **$73.9B net cash**, with **$65.2B of securities maturing inside one year**.
- Non-current deferred revenue nearly doubled, **+96.6% to $5.7B**, and total deferred revenue rose **28% to $20.6B**. That is the closest proxy in this dataset for committed cloud/AI backlog.

So when the bear says, “Capex may have bottomed and AI spend could re-accelerate,” my response is: good. AMZN has the balance sheet to fund that investment without dilution or rating pressure. And the deferred-revenue signal suggests enterprise demand is not deteriorating. A capex re-acceleration would be a growth investment, not automatically a value destroyer — especially if it is aimed at AWS/AI capacity.

It hasn’t even been tested. AMZN has held entirely above **167.33**, the gap-day low, and above **159.76**, the gap top. But right now, the burden of proof is on the bear.

**“FCF is low quality because of capex cuts.”**  
Operating cash flow rose **81.7%** to **$84.9B**, and Q4 alone generated roughly **$42.5B** of OCF. Gross margin expanded **320bp**. This is not just a capex story. Capex/D&A is now **1.08x** versus **1.52x** in FY2022, so the depreciation tailwind is fading — that’s a real risk to monitor. But the cash-flow inflection is broad-based.

**“SBC and dilution eat the FCF.”**  
This is a legitimate concern: SBC was **$24.0B**, or **4.2% of revenue**, and shares grew **1.4%**. But FCF per share was **$3.10**, still above diluted EPS of **$2.90**, and FCF ex-SBC was still positive at roughly **$8.2B**. With **$73.9B of net cash**, AMZN has a latent buyback lever it has not pulled. No buyback in FY2023 is not bearish — it signals management still sees higher-return reinvestment opportunities.

**“Receivables are growing faster than revenue, payables are lagging, and taxes are rising.”**  
True, and worth monitoring. Receivables grew **23.4%** versus revenue **+11.8%**, payables grew only **6.8%**, and cash taxes were **$11.2B**. But the allowance ratio is stable, FY2023 pre-tax income was **98% operations-driven** versus FY2022’s mark-to-market noise, and the effective tax rate has already normalized to **19.0%**. These are headwinds, not thesis-breakers.

**“Insider data is unavailable.”**  
Correct — and I will not infer anything from it. Absence of insider data is not bearish evidence.
