The bear can point to revenue contraction, but it cannot easily explain away the structural advantages in the financials:

- **Negative cash conversion cycle of about –37.8 days.** **AAPL** collects in roughly 58 days and pays suppliers in roughly 107 days. Suppliers fund the working capital. That is a massive competitive advantage.
- **ROA of 27.5%** on a **$352.6B** asset base.
- **Interest coverage of 29.1x** and a net-cash balance sheet.
- **FCF/net income of 102.7%** in FY2023 — earnings are cash earnings. There is no accrual-quality red flag here.

That buyback cut is a real bear talking point. But let’s keep it in perspective: **$77.6B** is still enormous — about **20% of revenue** and **78% of FCF**. The combined payout was **93% of FCF**, which is near the ceiling, but the dividend alone is only about **15.5% of FCF**. There is room to flex the buyback. And because net income exceeded total capital returned in FY23, shareholders’ equity actually rebuilt **22.6% to $62.1B**. That is a healthier balance sheet, not a deteriorating one.
