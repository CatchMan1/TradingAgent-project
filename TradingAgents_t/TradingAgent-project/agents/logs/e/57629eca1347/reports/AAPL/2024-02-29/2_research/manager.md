**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**  
The bear case wins on current risk/reward for AAPL, though not by enough to justify a full Sell. The decisive issue is that the bull case is largely about quality and *potential* stabilization, while the bear case is about *confirmed* technical deterioration and a maturing fundamental growth engine.

The technical evidence favors caution: AAPL closed at 180.75 below the 10 EMA (182.63), VWMA (183.13), 200 SMA (183.90), Bollinger mid (184.78), and 50 SMA (187.72). The intermediate trend has already turned lower: AAPL fell from 195.18 on 2024-01-23 to 180.75 on 2024-02-29, with a lower-high sequence, a falling 50 SMA, and price below the 200 SMA since 2024-02-23. The 2024-02-29 high-volume down day closed weakly and printed a fresh low at 179.53; even granting the month-end flow caveat, VWMA sitting above price indicates overhead supply.

The bull’s technical counters are real but unconfirmed. The MACD histogram is improving, but the MACD line remains at its window low and below signal for 19 sessions. RSI at 38.07 is not oversold, and the earlier positive divergence had already failed once. ATR compression is directionless until resolution. AAPL is testing support, but support has not yet produced a reversal signal.

Fundamentally, AAPL is a high-quality company, but the bull overstates the growth case. Revenue grew only 2.07% in Q1 FY24; the 15.96% EPS growth was driven heavily by margin expansion, flat R&D, and buybacks. Gross margin at 45.87% leaves roughly 137 bps of cushion above the bull’s own 45% thesis-break level. Rising SBC, capex/working-capital distortions in FCF, and the lack of segment-mix data make the margin-led growth story less durable than the bull presents. The $64.6B net cash and strong FCF are genuine downside mitigants, but they are a solvency/optionality floor, not a stock-price floor. Also, no valuation data—market cap, P/E, P/FCF—was provided, so neither side can claim AAPL is cheap here.

I choose **Underweight**, not **Sell**, because AAPL’s balance sheet, cash generation, and support near 179.53 still argue against an outright exit or short. But the burden of proof has shifted to the bulls: AAPL must reclaim key levels and show confirmation before adding exposure.
