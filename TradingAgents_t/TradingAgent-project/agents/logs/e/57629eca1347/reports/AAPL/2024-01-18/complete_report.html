4. Income Statement Deep Dive
4.1 The margin engine — gross margin is the bright spot
  • Gross margin rose 41.8% → 43.3% → 44.1% across FY21–FY23, i.e. +231 bp over two years.
  • In FY2023, gross profit fell only 1.0% while revenue fell 2.8%: COGS fell 4.2% ($223.5B → $214.1B), faster than revenue. That pattern is consistent with (a) an increasing revenue mix from higher-margin Services and (b) favorable product cost/component pricing and mix, partially offset by FX.
  • Actionable read: gross margin, not unit growth, is currently the swing factor for AAPL earnings. A 100 bp change in gross margin at FY23 revenue scale is worth ~$3.8B of gross profit (~$0.24 of EPS at a 14.7% tax rate), versus roughly $3.8B of revenue growth at constant margin — i.e. margin durability matters as much as revenue reacceleration.
4.2 Opex is the offsetting pressure
  • Opex grew +17.0% in FY22 and +6.8% in FY23, reaching 14.31% of revenue (vs. 13.02% and 12.00%).
  • Consequently, operating margin has been flat-to-down: 29.79% → 30.29% → 29.82%.
  • SBC is the fastest-rising cost line: $7.9B → $9.0B → $10.8B (+19.9% in FY23), or 2.83% of revenue (vs. 2.29% in FY22). Unrecognized (unvested) SBC cost at 2023-09-30 was $18.6B — a visible, non-cash drag on reported EPS for future years, and a genuine dilution offset to buybacks.
  • 18.6B of unrecognized SBC ≈ $1.18/share of future expense still to be recognized.
4.3 Below the operating line
  • Interest expense jumped +34.2% ($2.93B → $3.93B) as the debt stack repriced into a higher-rate world; interest & dividend income rose +32.7% ($2.83B → $3.75B) on higher yields on the securities portfolio. Net non-operating result was −$565M (vs. −$334M and +$258M).
  • Interest coverage remains enormous: EBIT/interest = 29.1x.
  • Tax: effective rate 14.7% (13.3% FY21, 16.2% FY22). Cash taxes paid ($18.68B) exceeded book tax expense ($16.74B).
    • The FY2023 FDII deduction benefit dropped to $0 (vs. $296M in FY22, $1.37B in FY21) — a discrete headwind that was more than offset by a larger foreign-rate differential (−$5.74B vs −$4.37B) and research credits ($1.21B).
    • Excess SBC tax benefits are shrinking ($2.14B → $1.87B → $1.12B), meaning less future EPS support from the tax line.
  • Geographic profit concentration is a real risk: foreign pre-tax income of $72.9B = 64.1% of $113.7B total (FY22: 59.9%; FY21: 62.9%). Any change in international tax regimes (Pillar Two, EU state-aid/tax disputes) hits roughly two-thirds of the profit base. The 10-K also flags that a decrease in unrecognized tax benefits of up to $4.5B is "reasonably possible" within 12 months — a discrete downside risk not in consensus EPS at face value.
