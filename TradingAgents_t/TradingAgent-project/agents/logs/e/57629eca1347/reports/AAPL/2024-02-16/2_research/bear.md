**3) “The margin inflection is real growth.”**

It is real, but it is not the kind of growth that deserves the benefit of the doubt. Q1 FY2024 revenue rose **2.1%** to $119.575B. Gross margin expanded **291 bps** to 45.87%, and operating income rose **12.1%**. That is operating leverage — but it came with **COGS falling 3.1% in absolute dollars** on higher revenue. That is cost relief and mix, not volume growth. It cuts both ways. If input costs normalize or mix reverts, the operating leverage reverses quickly. A 2% top line gives no buffer.

And the prior year was not a soft patch — FY2023 was an outright down year: gross profit **−1.0%**, net income **−2.8%**, and EPS rose only **0.3%** because of buybacks. One strong margin quarter does not erase a full year of decline. The burden of proof is on Q2 FY2024 to show gross margin holds above 45% and revenue re-accelerates. You’re asking investors to pay today for proof that arrives next quarter.

**4) “R&D flat is fine; SBC is a dilution offset; the tax rate is just a watch item.”**

Those are not thesis killers individually, but together they chip away at the quality argument. R&D fell **0.2% y/y in nominal dollars** while SG&A rose 2.7%. For a company whose bull case depends on the next product cycle, flat R&D is a yellow flag, not a positive. SBC is **11.2% of FY2023 net income** and grew **19.9%** in a down-earnings year. You call it a dilution offset; I call it a real compensation cost that makes the buyback less powerful. And with **64.1% of FY2023 pretax income foreign-sourced** and an effective tax rate of **14.7%**, the tax line is the weakest part of the model. OECD minimum-tax or US international tax changes hit EPS directly with no operating offset. A fortress balance sheet does not protect the P&L from a higher tax rate.

**5) “Net cash gives optionality.”**

It is nice, but it is not going to stop a de-rating if revenue growth stays at 2%. Meanwhile management is parking cash — $13.0B of securities maturities versus $9.8B of purchases — and the dividend is only **11.3% of net income**. That is conservative, but it is not incremental growth. The balance sheet tells me AAPL won’t blow up. It does not tell me the stock is cheap.
