### 1) The growth engine reaccelerated, and the quality is better than the headline bears will admit

The most important datapoint in the fundamental file is Q3 FY2023: **GOOGL** posted revenue of **$76.693B, +11.0% YoY**, while operating income rose **+24.6% to $21.343B** and operating margin expanded **300bp to 27.8%**. Gross margin also improved to **56.7%** from 54.9%, and diluted EPS rose **+46% to $1.55**.

Yes, the tax rate helped EPS—7.1% versus 14.3%—and I’ll own that. But the cleaner measure is pre-tax income, which still grew **+30.6%** in Q3 and **+13.1%** over nine months. Revenue accelerated to +11% while margins expanded. That combination—growth and efficiency at the same time—is exactly what you want in a mega-cap.

For the nine months, **GOOGL** generated **$61.6B of free cash flow, +40% YoY**, with a **27.9% FCF margin**. It has **$119.9B in cash plus short-term investments**, interest expense of only **$239M** against interest income of **$2.755B**, and it repurchased **$45.3B** of stock while reducing shares outstanding by **2.4%**. That is a persistent bid under the stock and a real EPS tailwind.

And the optionality is not priced aggressively: Google Cloud is inflecting toward profitability, Vertex AI sits inside a massive distribution ecosystem, and Other Bets—Waymo, Verily, Wing—are essentially free call options inside the current numbers.

### 2) The competitive moat is still underappreciated

**GOOGL** owns the world’s largest search and digital advertising franchise, with Google Services producing the vast majority of revenue at very high operating margins. YouTube, Android, Chrome, and Play give **GOOGL** unmatched distribution. That distribution is the reason AI is an opportunity for **GOOGL**, not just a threat.

The bear will say “search is maturing” or “AI could disrupt the core.” Fair debate—but **GOOGL** is not a passive incumbent. It has the data, infrastructure, cloud platform, and consumer touchpoints to monetize AI across search, YouTube, Workspace, and enterprise cloud. Cloud profitability is already improving, and the balance sheet lets **GOOGL** fund the AI capex cycle without straining liquidity.

That is a competitive advantage: **GOOGL** can invest through a capex cycle while still buying back tens of billions of stock. Most companies cannot do both.
