## 5. Indicator selection and rationale

I selected eight complementary constructs rather than stacking correlated oscillators:

1. **close_200_sma** — establishes the dominant long-term uptrend and the strategic bias.
2. **close_50_sma** — my range-defining line; its two-month flatness is the single most informative fact.
3. **close_10_ema** — the trigger line; price below it defines the active pullback.
4. **macd** (with macds/macdh read as one construct) — the fresh crossover on 01-04 is the key momentum-change event.
5. **rsi** — measures how much froth was removed (65.7 → 49.4) and shows there is now room in *both* directions, unlike 12-26 when it was near overbought.
6. **boll / boll_ub / boll_lb** — used as one envelope to normalize price within volatility and to detect the failed upper-band test and the 30% bandwidth expansion.
7. **atr** — pure risk management: stop placement and position sizing at 2.0% daily range.
8. **vwma** — the only volume-integrated trend measure, and the source of the most concrete bearish evidence (price below volume-weighted average).

Deliberately excluded: `macds` and `macdh` as standalone entries (redundant with `macd`), any stochastics (redundant with RSI), and `mfi` — the snapshot reports MFI = 0.638, which is inconsistent with a 0–100 oscillator scale and looks like a data-quality artifact, so I have not used it.

## 6. Actionable framework

**Base case (highest probability): continued mean reversion toward 134–133.7, then stabilization.** The 50 SMA is a natural magnet precisely because it has been the two-month equilibrium and price is only 1 ATR above it. Nothing in the data says the long-term uptrend is broken.

- **Preferred entry zone: 134.5 → 133.7.** Look for the pullback to stall here with RSI in the 40–45 area and a narrowing daily range / declining volume. This zone aligns the 50 SMA with prior consolidation and is where risk/reward is best.
- **Stop: daily close below 133.0** (approx. 1.5×ATR below the entry midpoint of ~134.1). Invalidation is structural, not noise-based.
- **Upside targets: 137.4 (VWMA) → 138.3 (10 EMA) → 139.6 → 141.5–142.7.** A reclaim of 137.4 on volume >30M is the trigger for the first target; the 141.5–142.7 area is the prior ceiling and should be treated as a trim zone the first time it is revisited.
- **Risk math:** at ATR 2.727, use 1.5–2.0 ATR stops (4.1–5.5 points). Size positions as *(risk budget) ÷ (2 × 2.727)*, i.e. roughly 5.5 points of risk per share. This makes the reward-to-risk to 141.5 approximately 3:1 from a 134.1 entry.

**Bull acceleration case (secondary):** A single close above 138.29 (10 EMA) with the MACD histogram turning positive again would invalidate the short-term downtrend and set up a retest of 141.5–142.7, with 143.73 (upper band) as the stretch target. Do not pre-position for this; require the close.

**Bear case (monitor, don't front-run):** A **daily close below 133.5 on volume above ~35M** would be the first objective evidence of trend damage — it would flatten the 50 SMA relationship and open 129.55–129.69 (Bollinger lower band / 12-14 low). Note the current decline is on below-average volume (0.9× the 20-day mean), which argues *against* assigning this high probability today.

**Key nuance for position management:** the bullish and bearish evidence here lives on different timeframes. Long-term structure (price 8.7% above a rising 200 SMA, 50 > 200) is bullish; short-term mechanics (price below 10 EMA and VWMA, fresh MACD bearish cross, RSI fading from 65.7 to 49.4, lower highs and lower lows for six sessions) are bearish. The correct posture is **reduce gross exposure and wait for the 133.7–137.4 range to resolve**, rather than adding at 136.4 where price sits in the middle of the envelope with no edge.
