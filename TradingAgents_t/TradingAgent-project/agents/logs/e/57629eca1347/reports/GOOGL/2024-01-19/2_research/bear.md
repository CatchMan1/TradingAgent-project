## 4) Capex and accruals weaken the cash-engine argument

This is where your “cash machine” case is most vulnerable in 2024.

You cite 9M free cash flow of **~$61.6B, +40% YoY**, driven by OCF of **$82.8B** and capex of **$21.2B, down 11.1%**. But the nine-month capex decline is a comparison artifact. The run-rate is inflecting up:

- Q1 2023 capex: **$6.289B**
- Q2 2023 capex: **$6.888B**
- Q3 2023 capex: **$8.055B**, up **+17% q/q**

Annualized Q3 capex is roughly **$32.2B**. If that run-rate persists or rises for AI/data-center buildout, the capex tailwind behind the +40% FCF growth disappears. You say your bull case does not require FCF margin expansion. But if FCF growth decelerates, the buyback — **$45.3B in 9M 2023, or ~85% of FCF** — becomes less of a mechanical floor. Buybacks are discretionary. They can be slowed. They are not a contractual bid under the stock.

Then there is the balance-sheet noise:

- Accrued liabilities rose **+$17.7B, or +46.8%**, in nine months on ~7% revenue growth.
- Accrued income taxes rose **+$11.9B, or +729%**.
- Cash taxes already swung **+$10.4B** in 9M 2023.

You say the balance sheet can handle a charge or outflow. Sure. But that does not make the equity attractive at $146.38. It means solvency is not the issue; **earnings quality and free-cash-flow trajectory** are. A fortress balance sheet protects the company, not the buyer at an extended price.
