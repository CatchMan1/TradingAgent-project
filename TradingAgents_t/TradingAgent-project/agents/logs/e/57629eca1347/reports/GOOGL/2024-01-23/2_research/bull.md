### Growth: the Q3 data shows a real acceleration, not a hope story

The key fundamental fact is that **GOOGL’s revenue growth more than doubled between H1 2023 and Q3 2023**: H1 revenue grew +4.9%, but Q3 revenue grew **+11.0% to $76.69B**. That is the highest-quality bullish datapoint in the dataset.

And it was not low-quality growth. Q3 operating income rose **+24.6% to $21.34B**, operating margin expanded **+300 bp to 27.8%**, gross margin rose to **56.7% from 54.9%**, and cost of revenue grew only **+6.6%** while revenue grew +11.0%. That is operating leverage. R&D plus sales/marketing grew just +5.5% in Q3 versus +10.1% for the nine-month period, meaning cost discipline was accelerating into the quarter.

The bear will say: “Yes, but Q3 EPS of $1.55 was tax-inflated.” Fair. The 7.1% Q3 tax rate versus a 13.4% 9M rate is not a run-rate. Normalize Q3 EPS to roughly **$1.40–$1.42**, and it is still up roughly **32–34% year over year** against $1.06. More importantly, the tax rate does not create the **+24.6% operating income growth** or the **+300 bp operating margin expansion**. Those are pre-tax operational results. I am not underwriting GOOGL on a $6.20 annualized EPS fantasy. I am underwriting it on the operating inflection.

Cash flow supports the same conclusion. 9M operating cash flow was **$82.8B**, free cash flow **$61.6B**, and FCF margin **27.9%**. Yes, roughly $10.4B of OCF was helped by accrued-but-unpaid taxes; strip that out and underlying FCF growth is high-teens rather than +40%. High-teens is still excellent for a company this size. And that cash funded **$45.3B of buybacks** in nine months, reducing diluted shares by **3.6%** year over year. GOOGL is retiring roughly 2–2.5% of shares annually while compounding earnings. That is a mechanical tailwind under EPS.
