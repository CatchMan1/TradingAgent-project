
Bear Analyst: **Bear, GOOGL — January 5, 2024**

Bull, I’ll concede the easy part: GOOGL is not a broken business. But your case asks investors to buy at **$135.73** because the 200-day is rising and Q3 was strong. That’s exactly where I push back. The long-term trend may be intact, but the tactical tape has already rolled over, the near-term risk/reward is poor, and the fundamental “inflection” is younger and lower-quality than you’re presenting.

**On the technicals: you’re calling the 50-day a launchpad. I call it a tripwire.**

GOOGL has now printed **seven consecutive sessions of lower highs and lower lows**. Today’s close of **$135.73** was in the lower third of the daily range, below both the **10 EMA at $137.82** and the **VWMA at $137.85**. That VWMA matters: price is **1.54% below** the volume-weighted average, and that average has been rising all month. In plain English, the recent volume-weighted buyer is underwater. Rallies into **$137.8–$137.9** are likely to meet supply from people trying to get flat, not fresh demand.

You point to the **MACD still being positive at 1.105**. True—but the MACD crossed below signal on **January 4**, and the histogram widened from **–0.070 to –0.289** in one session. That is a fresh, accelerating momentum warning, not a stale one. You point to **RSI 47.62** as “room for a bounce.” I’d frame it differently: there is **no oversold cushion**. The prior RSI trough was **39.73** on December 4. GOOGL can fall another meaningful leg before mean-reversion buyers have a statistical reason to step in.

And the 50-day at **$133.61**? You call it a launchpad because it’s been flat for two months. I call it untested support sitting just **1.6% below spot**. A close below it would end a four-month pattern of not closing below the 50-day. That’s not a trivial line—it’s the pivot. If it breaks on above-average volume, **$130.5** comes into play quickly, with the Bollinger lower band at **$130.54** and the 2-ATR stop near **$130.4** lining up almost perfectly.

**On risk/reward: buying here is the worst of both worlds.**

At **$135.73**, upside to the 10 EMA/VWMA confluence at **$137.85** is only about **+1.6%**. Downside to the structural stop near **$130.40** is about **–3.9%**. That’s less than **0.4:1** reward/risk at spot. Even the report’s preferred 50-SMA entry only gets you to roughly **1.3:1**—mediocre, not compelling. Your own playbook says either wait for **$137.9 reclaimed** or wait for **$133.6 tested with RSI in the high-30s**. I agree with the levels. I disagree with paying in the middle of the channel.

And don’t oversell the ATR contraction. Yes, **ATR is 2.676**, down from **3.25** in early December, so this has been an orderly decline. But compressed volatility cuts both ways. With Bollinger bandwidth around **9.3%**, a normal range expansion can produce a **$6–7 daily move without any news**. Contracting ATR is not a buy signal; it’s a warning not to confuse a quiet tape with a safe entry.

**On fundamentals: Q3 was good, but the bull case is extrapolating two quarters and ignoring quality.**

You’re right that Q3 revenue grew **+11.0% y/y** and operating income grew **+24.6%**. That is real. But the nine-month picture is much less exciting: revenue **+6.9%**, and the **9M operating margin was flat at 27.4%**. The margin inflection is a Q3 phenomenon, not yet a full-year trend. Q4 FY2023 had not even been reported as of this data set. So your “re-acceleration” thesis is essentially two quarters of evidence plus a narrative.

And the earnings quality flags are not minor. Q3 net income grew **+41.5%**, but the effective tax rate was **7.1% versus 14.3%** a year ago—roughly a **$1.5B** benefit that does not repeat. The nine-month non-operating line swung from **–$2,501M** to **+$709M**, a ~$3.2B tailwind driven by mark-to-market noise. Operating cash flow got a **+$10.4B** boost from accrued-but-unpaid taxes. Strip that out and cash conversion is closer to **96%**, not the headline **116%**. Still healthy—but not the “fortress” optics you’re selling.

There’s also a future cash drain hiding in the balance sheet: **accrued liabilities jumped +$17.7B** since year-end, and **accrued income taxes rose +$11.9B**. Those are not permanent sources of cash. They reverse. Meanwhile, **SBC rose +14.6% y/y to $16.5B in nine months**, faster than revenue—the real cost of retaining AI talent. And **capex fell 11% in 9M**. You treat that as a bullish sign, but the report explicitly flags it: a capex rebound is the single biggest forward risk to the FCF margin that currently looks best-in-class. You can’t simultaneously celebrate 9M FCF +40% and assume the AI buildout will never pressure it.

**On the “moat”: wide moats don’t prevent bad entries.**

You argue GOOGL’s ecosystem, data, and AI research give it a durable edge. Maybe. But the data in front of us doesn’t quantify Cloud profitability, doesn’t show segment-level AI monetization, and doesn’t prove the Q3 margin expansion is sustainable. The nine-month revenue growth of **+6.9%** is the more honest read of a maturing core business. AI may expand the TAM, but at this point it’s also an expensive arms race—visible in rising SBC and the forward capex risk. “Wide moat” is not a near-term catalyst. It’s a reason to own the stock at the right price, not to ignore a fresh momentum breakdown.

**Directly to your bull counterpoints:**

- You say the MACD cross is “only two days old.” That makes it fresher, not less relevant. The histogram is widening negatively.
- You say selling volume isn’t expanding. True—but the VWMA above price shows supply overhead. Orderly distribution is still distribution.
- You say the heaviest volume days were up days. That’s backward-looking. The market is telling you recent buyers are trapped now.
- You say the tax benefit is a quirk because operating income is clean. Fine—but then lean on operating income, and admit the 9M operating margin is flat. You can’t book the Q3 operating leverage as durable while dismissing the 9M average.
- You say “I’m not alone in seeing the value.” There’s no sentiment report in this run, so that’s rhetoric, not evidence. Neither of us gets to claim the crowd.

**Bottom line:** GOOGL may be a great franchise, but at **$135.73** the stock is in a young, unexhausted tactical rollover, with trapped volume overhead, no oversold cushion, and a fundamental story that still needs Q4 and FY2024 capex guidance to confirm. The bull case is treating a two-quarter margin inflection and a fortress balance sheet as if they eliminate near-term downside. They don’t.

I’m bearish on adding GOOGL here. I’d rather miss a bounce than underwrite a 50-day break with poor asymmetry and earnings-quality caveats. A decisive close above **$137.9** on volume above the ~22M mean would flip me. A controlled test of **$133.6** with RSI in the high-30s would make me reconsider. But until then, the burden of proof is on the bull—and the tape says he’s early.
