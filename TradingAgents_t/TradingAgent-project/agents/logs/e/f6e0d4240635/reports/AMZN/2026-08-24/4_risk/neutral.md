
Neutral Analyst: I’m in the neutral seat, and my first reaction is that both of you are using the same facts to justify different risk appetites rather than different forecasts. I agree with the SELL direction in the trader’s plan, but I do not agree with the aggressive analyst’s comfort holding 60–70% residual into NVDA and Jackson Hole, and I do not agree with the conservative analyst’s blanket push to cap AMZN at 30–35% before the event. AMZN at 262.07 is not a broken chart. It is a post-gap digestion sitting on a knife-edge, with strong operating momentum, negative post-acquisition free cash flow, and a binary catalyst directly ahead. That deserves a staged trim and a real hedge discussion, not maximum exposure and not a near-exit.

The aggressive analyst is right that the 21.3x forward P/E story does not survive the adjusted-EPS arithmetic. On roughly $7.3–7.8 annualized operating EPS, AMZN is closer to 33–36x, and negative free cash flow after acquisitions means there is no buyback or cash-return cushion underneath the multiple. That is a legitimate reason not to pay up. But he overstates the certainty of the bearish asymmetry. The pullback in AMZN has come on contracting volume, roughly 28M shares on 08-24 versus a 21-session average near 47M, and the MACD line is still positive at +3.16 even with the histogram negative. RSI at 52.48 never flushed, which he reads as complacency, but it can also mean the overhead supply has not been aggressively distributed. The VWMA at 267.41 is real resistance, but the first downside trigger is still a daily close below 257.04, not today’s price. Keeping 60–70% residual and calling it a cheap call only works if the hedge is fully sized and the caller can tolerate gap risk. Otherwise it is not cheap optionality; it is uncompensated event exposure.

The conservative analyst is right to focus on the gap risk. A 257 stop is only about 0.7 ATR below 262.07 with ATR at 7.28, and a two-day event can jump it. AMZN has negative TTM free cash flow of $11.6B, free cash flow after acquisitions near −$53.6B, debt up from $68.8B to $133.0B in six months, and $650B of contractual obligations. That is not a margin-of-safety setup. But the conservative response overcorrects. AMZN still has revenue growth of 19.6%, operating income growth of 43.2%, a 13.7% operating margin, flat inventory while revenue grew 18%, and SBC intensity falling. The 50-SMA at 250.14 and 200-SMA at 238.39 are still rising, and the golden cross has held since May 6. Selling half of full weight now and cutting the residual to 30–35% before the event may be the right choice for a very low-risk mandate, but as a blanket neutral call it risks selling at the roof of the unfilled gap right before a possible mean-reversion reclaim. The conservative case also treats the 33–36x adjusted multiple as if it forbids holding any residual, but a 23% operating-EPS growth rate and dominant AWS/custom-silicon position still have value. The issue is sizing and hedging, not zero exposure.

To the conservative point that the neutral range plan pays nothing for event risk, I’d say the answer is not to abandon the range plan entirely. It is to make the residual conditional. If the caller actually puts on a properly sized 30–45 day 250/240 put spread against the residual, then keeping roughly 50–55% of standard weight through the NVDA/Jackson Hole window is defensible. If the caller will not hedge, the residual should be closer to 40–45% of standard, not 60–70%. That is the middle ground between the aggressive and conservative camps. It respects the aggressive analyst’s valuation and event-risk warnings without pretending the primary trend and operating engine do not matter, and it respects the conservative analyst’s balance-sheet and gap-risk warnings without dumping AMZN at the gap roof before the 257.04 trigger is actually hit.

So my neutral adjustment to the trader’s plan is this: keep the SELL bias, but treat it as a staged trim, not a directional short. Sell the first tranche now or near 262.07, but split the proposed one-third so that half of that tranche goes now and the balance goes either into 266.40–267.41 if AMZN rallies into the VWMA supply or on a daily close below 257.04 with volume above 45M, whichever happens first. Keep the residual at roughly 45–55% of standard weight if hedged, 40–45% if unhedged, and lower only if the caller is already overweight AMZN. Do not add above 266.40; only re-add on a daily close above 267.41 with volume above 45M. Hard de-risk below 257.04 on a daily close with volume, and take the residual to minimum on a break of the rising 50-SMA at 250.14. If the caller wants to keep the higher end of the residual range, the put spread is not optional; it should be sized to the residual, not a token overlay.

That is the moderate path. The aggressive analyst is too confident that trimming a third and holding most of the position is cheap asymmetry into a binary event. The conservative analyst is too quick to cut the residual to 30–35% before the event even though AMZN is still above rising structural supports and the operating engine is genuinely strong. The balanced answer is to sell into the gap roof, keep a reduced and preferably hedged residual, and wait for 257.04 or 267.41 to resolve the trade. So I would keep the final transaction proposal as SELL, but as a staged, hedged trim rather than either a full de-risk or a comfortable hold.
