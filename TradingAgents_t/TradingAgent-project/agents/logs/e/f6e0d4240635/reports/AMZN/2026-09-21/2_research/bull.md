
Bull Analyst: **Bull opening statement — AMZN**

Bear, I’ll save you the trouble of opening with the negative-FCF slide: yes, AMZN is spending heavily, yes, reported EPS is flattered by a non-cash mark, and yes, the stock has been in a corrective range since August. None of that changes the core bull case. ### 1. Growth potential: this is not a slowing mega-cap

AMZN’s Q2 FY2026 numbers are the strongest part of the bull case:

- **Revenue: $200.6B, +19.6% YoY** — and that is *acceleration* from Q1’s +16.6%. A ~$800B annualized revenue base growing 19.6% is extraordinary.
- **H1 revenue: $382.1B, +18.2% YoY.**
- **Q2 operating income: $27.5B, +43.2% YoY.**
- **Q2 operating margin: 13.69%, +226 bps YoY.**
- **H1 operating income: +36.6% YoY.**
- **Gross margin: 52.26% in Q2, +45 bps YoY; H1 52.05%, +84 bps.**

Bear, you’ll say the GAAP EPS number is low quality. I agree. **Reported TTM EPS of $12.42 includes about $4.44/share from a private-equity revaluation**; normalized TTM EPS is closer to **$7.98**. But strip that out and what do you get? **Q2 core EPS of roughly $2.16, +28.6% YoY; H1 core EPS of roughly $4.09, +25.1% YoY.** That is still elite growth at AMZN’s scale. The bear case needs the *core* business to be deteriorating. It isn’t. It is accelerating.

The forward opportunity is in the mix: AWS, advertising, third-party marketplace, and AI infrastructure. The $650B contractual-obligation book — with **$78.1B due within 12 months** — is the clearest quantitative fingerprint of multi-year AI compute, chip, and content commitments. Bear will call that a liability. I call it **committed demand and a visible buildout runway**. AMZN is not spending into a void; it is spending against contracted capacity and a recovering revenue trajectory.

### 2. Competitive advantages: scale, working capital, and funding access

AMZN’s moat is not just “it is big.” It is the cash-conversion machine underneath the retail business:

- **Cash conversion cycle: approximately −64 days** — DSO ~40 days, DPO ~140 days, DIO ~36 days.
- That negative working-capital cycle lets AMZN fund inventory with suppliers’ money and reinvest in AWS, logistics, and AI.
- **Cash + marketable securities: $176.1B**; even after the debt increase, AMZN still carries **net cash of roughly $29.7B**.
- The new **£4.25B sterling bond was ~2.5x covered**. Bear will point out demand “cooled” versus a peer. Fine — it was still a highly oversubscribed, investment-grade, long-tenor deal. That is funding strength, not distress.

And the operating leverage is real: Q2 marketing spend rose only **2.5%** while revenue rose **19.6%**; G&A fell **6.0%**. That is demand-pull, not spend-pull. AMZN is monetizing Prime, ads, third-party services, and AWS faster than it needs to add marketing and overhead. That is exactly what a high-quality compounder looks like during an investment cycle.

Yes, capex is **27% of revenue** and roughly **101% of adjusted EBITDA**. That is the bear’s best point. But capex is the *input* to the AWS/AI franchise, not a leak in the boat. The correct debate is whether AMZN earns a return on that capex. Q2’s **+43% operating income growth** alongside a **226 bp margin expansion** says the core business is already covering the investment while still growing. If capex plateaus and revenue keeps compounding near 19%, the operating leverage from here is enormous.

### 4. Bear counterpoints — direct responses

**Bear: “Free cash flow is negative — $11.6B TTM, adjusted even worse.”**  
Guilty. This is a deliberate investment cycle, not a broken business. AMZN generated **$161.4B TTM operating cash flow**, and even after removing the **$41.4B deferred-tax add-back**, adjusted OCF is still around **$120B**. The negative FCF is capex-driven. The bull trigger is not “FCF turns positive tomorrow”; it is **capex plateauing while revenue growth stays near 19% and operating income keeps compounding**. Q2 already showed +43% operating income. The bear needs margin compression, not just high capex.

**Bear: “Debt has nearly doubled to $133B.”**  
Yes — and AMZN still has **$176B of cash and securities** and roughly **net cash**. It just raised long-tenor sterling debt at ~2.5x coverage. This is a company pre-funding an AI buildout while it has balance-sheet capacity, not a leveraged retailer in distress. Interest expense is rising, but it is still only about **5% of Q2 operating income**. The risk is real; it is not thesis-breaking.

**Bear: “The EPS beat is fake.”**  
Partly. I will not defend the $12.42 reported TTM EPS. But I will defend the **$7.98 normalized TTM EPS** and the **+25–29% core EPS growth**. The private-stake mark is non-cash, and the underlying $122.3B equity portfolio is optionality — volatile optionality, but optionality. A markdown would be a headline-EPS air pocket, not a cash-flow event. I’d rather own AMZN’s stake portfolio than not have it.

**Bear: “D&A is accelerating and margins are peaking.”**  
D&A rose ~31% in Q2, and that is a real headwind. But AMZN still expanded operating margin by **226 bps** in the same quarter. Gross margin expansion plus marketing/G&A leverage absorbed the D&A ramp and then some. If D&A outpaces gross profit for two consecutive quarters, the bear wins that point. We are not there yet.

**Bear: “The news flow is negative.”**  
The fresh news cluster is mostly stale, unquantified, or non-fundamental: a republished bond recap, a pregnancy-accommodation lawsuit with no damages or certification odds disclosed, a cargo-crash probe with no cause determined, a minimum-wage teaser with no numbers, and an unverified commentator claim about AI liabilities. The only clearly in-window AMZN-specific event in the supplied sample is a streaming lobbying coalition — neutral near-term, mildly constructive long-term. There is **no hard in-window fundamental catalyst** that changes the earnings or AWS trajectory. The bond is evidence of funding access, not a red flag.
