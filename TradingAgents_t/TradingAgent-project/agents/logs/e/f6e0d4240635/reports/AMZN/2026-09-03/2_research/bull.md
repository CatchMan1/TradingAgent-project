- H1 2026 revenue: **~$382.1B, +18.2% y/y**
- Q2 operating income: **$27.46B, +43.2% y/y**
- Q2 operating margin: **13.7%, +226 bps y/y**
- H1 operating income: **$51.31B, +36.6% y/y**
- H1 operating margin: **13.4%, +181 bps y/y**
- Q2 gross margin: **52.3%, +45 bps y/y**
- H1 gross margin: **52.1%, +84 bps y/y**

That is not a company struggling to scale. That is a company whose **retail logistics, advertising, and AWS mix are all feeding operating leverage**.

And look at cost discipline, which is where the bear argument usually goes quiet:

- Marketing expense: **+2.5% y/y** against revenue **+19.6%**
- G&A: **–6.0% y/y in absolute dollars**
- SBC: **–7.6% y/y in Q2**, falling from 3.90% to 3.01% of revenue
- Unrecognized SBC: **$24.8B**, already known and declining as a percentage of revenue

AMZN is not buying growth with runaway expenses. It is squeezing controllable costs while growing revenue high-teens. That is the definition of operating leverage.

## 2. The “bad earnings quality” bear point actually supports the bull case—if you adjust correctly

Yes, the bear is right that reported EPS is heavily inflated by non-cash marks:

- Q2 2026 non-operating income: **$53.4B**
- Of that, **$50.5B** was a non-cash upward price adjustment on non-marketable equity securities
- H1 2026 equity mark-ups: **$62.8B**, about **52% of pre-tax income**
- Reported Q2 diluted EPS: **$5.75**
- My adjusted “core” Q2 EPS: **~$2.1**
- Reported H1 diluted EPS: **$8.53**
- Core H1 EPS: **~$4.0**

So I accept the criticism: **GAAP EPS is optically inflated.** But that cuts against the bear, not for it. The bear wants to say the earnings aren’t real. Fine—underwrite the core. Core EPS was still up roughly **+24% y/y in Q2 and +22% in H1**. The operating business is growing even after removing the mark-to-market candy.

And critically, those equity marks are **non-cash and do not flow into operating cash flow**. They can reverse, but they do not impair AMZN’s ability to generate cash from selling goods, ads, cloud capacity, and Prime subscriptions.

Bear, if your short thesis is “EPS quality is poor,” I agree at the accounting level—but that is not a reason to bet against AMZN’s operating trajectory. It is a reason to use core EPS instead of headline EPS. The market may misprice the GAAP number, but the business is still compounding.

## 3. Competitive advantages: the moat is widening, not narrowing

- **Prime and the marketplace** create customer frequency and seller dependency.
- **Logistics** is a structural cost and service advantage.
- **Custom silicon and AI infrastructure** are increasingly vertical advantages inside AWS.
- **Negative cash conversion cycle of roughly –64 days** means working capital is a source of cash, not a drag. Payables of $147.4B and DPO near 140 days fund the flywheel.

The news flow adds optionality, not just noise:

And the DOJ beef-pricing item? It is a **data request** to retailers including AMZN, not an enforcement action or fine. AMZN’s exposure runs through Whole Foods, which is immaterial at AMZN’s scale. That is headline risk, not thesis risk.

## 4. The capex/FCF bear case is real—but it is an investment cycle, not a distress signal

This is the strongest bear argument, so let me take it head-on.

AMZN’s free cash flow is negative:

- H1 2026 FCF: **–$27.0B**
- TTM FCF: **–$11.6B**
- TTM capex: **$173.0B**, about **22% of revenue**
- H1 2026 capex: **$98.4B, +72% y/y**

That sounds scary until you look at the other side of the ledger:

- H1 2026 operating cash flow: **$71.4B, +44.2% y/y**
- TTM operating cash flow: **$161.4B**
- Cash + marketable securities: **$176.1B**
- Long-term debt: **$133.0B**
- Net cash on a debt-only basis: **~+$29.7B**
- Q2 interest coverage: **20.9x**
- H1 interest coverage: **24.3x**
- Gross debt / annualized EBITDA: **~0.77x**; ~**1.28x** including leases

So no, AMZN is not levering up because it cannot fund itself. It is levering up because it is deliberately building AI/cloud/logistics capacity while still generating **$161B of TTM operating cash flow**.

The bear wants to treat capex as a permanent cash burn. I treat it as **pre-built capacity**. The moment capex growth decelerates, FCF inflects hard. The bear’s case requires capex to keep accelerating while revenue and AWS demand fail to materialize. The data shows the opposite: revenue is accelerating, margins are expanding, and operating cash flow is growing faster than revenue.

And the balance sheet gives AMZN room to run this play. A company with net cash, 20x+ interest coverage, and $176B of liquidity is not a financial distress story.
