
Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for AMZN — Opening Statement

Let me start with the single number the bear will try hardest to bury, because it's the one that actually matters: **Q2-2026 operating income grew 43.2% year-over-year to $27.46 billion on revenue growth of 19.6%.** Operating margin expanded 226 basis points to 13.69%. On a business doing roughly $800B annualized, that is not a rounding error — that is a structural re-rating of earning power, and it happened while marketing spend grew 2.5%, G&A *fell* 6%, and share-based comp *declined* 1.5%.

That combination has a name: **demand-pull operating leverage.** You don't get that by buying growth. You get it when your infrastructure is already built and incremental revenue drops through at high margin. Every bear thesis on AMZN has to explain why that math reverses. None of them do.

---

## The bear will say "the EPS is fake." They're half right — and it doesn't matter.

Yes: $53.4B of Q2's $80.9B pre-tax income came from non-operating fair-value marks on non-marketable equity stakes. Reported EPS of $5.75 is roughly 2.9x core EPS of ~$1.95. I'm not going to pretend otherwise — the fundamentals report is right that anyone modeling off GAAP EPS is mis-specified.

But here's the counter the bear never makes: **strip out every penny of those marks and AMZN still grew core EPS ~25.6% in H1-2026** ($3.63 vs ~$2.89). Twenty-six percent core earnings growth at this scale is elite. And if the bear wants to argue those marks are "paper," I'll point them at the balance sheet: non-marketable equity went from $16.2B to $122.3B in six months. That's a $106B step-up in *actual asset value* from AMZN's AI and venture stakes. It's Level 3 and it's volatile — fair — but "paper" is a strange word for a portfolio that has appreciated sixfold.

**So I'll make the bear a deal:** you keep the GAAP EPS skepticism, and I'll trade the operating line. Operating income is the cleanest, least-gameable metric in this filing, and it just printed +43%.

---

## The "capex is out of control" argument is backwards — it's the moat being poured

Bear case: LTM capex $173.0B, +60.7% y/y, **107% of operating cash flow**, LTM FCF −$11.6B. Therefore the equity is a debt-market story.

My response: **capex is 2.3x depreciation.** That ratio is the whole argument. Maintenance capex roughly tracks D&A. When you're spending 230% of depreciation, you are not propping up a business — you are *building a second one*. Capex/revenue went from 17.7% to 25.8%. That capital is going into AI datacenters in West Texas ($1B, construction already begun this month) and North Carolina ($10B), against demand characterized in the news flow as AWS "growing at a historic pace."

And the demand is *contracted*, not hoped for. **Total contractual obligations: $650.0B — 4.0x LTM operating cash flow**, with $78.1B due in the next twelve months. The bear reads that as "fixed costs." I read it as **$650 billion of commitments Amazon signed because customers asked for the capacity first.** You don't sign multi-year AI compute and logistics capacity contracts on speculation.

Meanwhile the cash engine the bear glosses over: **LTM operating cash flow $161.4B, +33.2% y/y.** H1 core net income converted to CFO at **1.81x**. This isn't a company that can't generate cash — it's a company choosing to deploy it at what it believes are AI-cycle returns.

**And the funding is not stressed.** Net cash ex-leases is **+$29.6B**. Interest coverage is **20.9x** in Q2. The cash conversion cycle is **−64 days** — suppliers fund the working capital. You can lever a business like that up considerably before it becomes a credit problem, and at 20.9x coverage we are nowhere near that conversation.

## The tape already voted — and it voted on volume

Now the technicals, because the bear will want to talk about the −4.14% pullback from the $284.02 record close.

Look at what actually happened on 2026-07-31: **+15.32% in one session on 129.05M shares — 2.9x the prior day's volume** — leaving an unfilled gap from $239.82 to $262.01. Three sessions later AMZN printed **$284.02, the highest close in the entire dataset**, then $287.20 intraday.

Now look at the retreat. **129.05M → 90.83M → 69.75M → 45.12M → 30.67M.** Volume collapsed 76% from gap day. Price gave back 4.14% on *shrinking* volume. That is the textbook signature of profit-taking into strength with **no institutional supply.** If the gap were a trap, the red candles would carry the volume. They don't.

**VWMA confirms it:** it has risen every single session since the gap — 240.82 → 245.72 → 250.17 → 252.45 → 253.80 → 254.95. The volume-weighted cost basis is climbing, which is the fingerprint of accumulation, not distribution.

And the moving-average stack is flawless: **272.26 > 10-EMA 260.87 > VWMA 254.95 > Bollinger mid 250.14 > 50-SMA 247.45 > 200-SMA 236.25.** Price is 15.24% above a *rising* 200-SMA. The 50-SMA just inflected upward (246.38 → 247.45) after declining since June. The MACD flipped from −3.75 to +6.83 in five sessions. These are slow, hard-to-fake signals.

## The noise the bear will dress up as signal

- **Bezos sold ~$350M.** It was his *first sale of 2026* — ending a seven-month absence of insider supply, not escalating it. It's de minimis against market cap, almost certainly 10b5-1, and — critically — **he sold into a record.** The useful datum in that headline is the record, not the sale.
- **NYC cease-and-desist over e-bikes.** A marketplace-listing compliance letter. Financially immaterial. This is headline noise from an already-extended tape.
- **NC diesel-generator permits contested.** Genuinely worth monitoring as a permitting-friction pattern — but a single state regulatory proceeding on backup power at one of dozens of sites is basis points of enterprise value. Meanwhile NiSource secured **$1.4B in customer bill relief from data-center agreements** — the sector is proactively de-risking the political backlash. That cuts *against* the bear's permitting story.
- **Nasdaq −0.8% on Aug 5 while the Dow hit its third straight record.** That's rotation, not risk-off. AMZN got sold as megacap tech on a day the tape rotated. It says nothing about AWS demand or the 13.7% operating margin.

The honest near-term read is exactly what the news report says: mildly negative skew for a few sessions on positioning and rotation. **That is a timing headwind, not a thesis threat.** Don't let a one-evening headline sample get confused with a fundamental argument.

## Bottom line

The bear's strongest cards are earnings-quality optics and a negative FCF print. The market confirmed it with a +15.3% gap on 2.9x volume, a record close at $284.02, a perfectly stacked moving-average structure, a rising VWMA, and a pullback on 76% less volume than the breakout. That is not distribution. That is consolidation.
