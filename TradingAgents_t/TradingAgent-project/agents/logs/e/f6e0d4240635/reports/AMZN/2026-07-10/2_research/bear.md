## 2. Negative FCF Is Not Automatically a “Feature” — It’s a Risk Until ROIC Is Proven

Bull, you said the FCF compression is the growth story. I understand the argument. But the burden of proof is on you.

Look at the actual trajectory:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Mar-2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Operating cash flow | $84.95B | $115.88B | $139.51B | $148.53B |
| Capex | $52.73B | $83.00B | $131.82B | **$151.00B** |
| Free cash flow | $32.22B | $32.88B | $7.70B | **−$2.47B** |

FCF went from **+$32B** to **−$2.5B** in two years. Capex nearly tripled. In Q1 FY2026 alone, capex was **$44.20B** versus operating income of **$23.85B** — AMZN spent nearly **1.85x its quarterly operating income** on capex.

You call that “high-ROIC AI infrastructure.” Where is the ROIC proof? We don’t have segment-level ROIC in the data. We have a capex pipeline that is still filling: capex incurred but not yet paid rose from **$16.8B in 2024** to **$27.0B in 2025**. That means more cash out the door ahead.

And D&A is already surging: **$18.95B in Q1**, **$70.44B TTM**, up from $55.4B in the prior TTM. As that depreciation catches up, the margin expansion the bull is celebrating could face pressure.

Yes, OCF is compounding. But OCF is not cash available to shareholders. The bull keeps saying “record operating cash flow” — but AMZN is spending more than that on capex. That is the whole debate. I’m not penalizing AMZN for investing. I’m saying the investment case requires proof that this capex earns attractive returns before investors pay up.

## 3. The Debt Story Is Not a Rounding Error — the Pace Is the Warning

Bull, you said debt-to-assets is only **~13.4%** and liquidity is **~$144B**. Fine. But you’re ignoring the speed of the change and the lease-adjusted obligations.

- Total debt moved from roughly **$68.4B** at Dec-2025 to **~$121.9B** at Mar-2026 — a **~$53.5B increase**.
- Long-term debt issued in Q1 FY2026 was **$53.4B** — a record.
- Interest expense rose **48% YoY** to **$0.80B**.
- Operating lease liabilities are **$91.62B**; finance lease liabilities are **$13.33B**. Lease-adjusted obligations are closer to **~$227B**, not a rounding error against a $916.6B asset base.

And this is a regime change. AMZN issued **$0** of long-term debt in FY2023 and FY2024. Then **$15.67B in FY2025**, then **$53.4B in Q1 FY2026**. The company is now debt-funding the AI build-out. That can be rational — but it also means less flexibility if AI returns disappoint, rates move, or FCF stays negative.

The current ratio is only **1.18**. Positive working capital of ~$38B sounds okay until you remember the capex pipeline. The bull calls the balance sheet “AAA-adjacent.” There is no rating evidence in the data for that. I’d call it a strong balance sheet in transition — and transitions carry risk.

Q1 reported EPS was **$2.78**. But roughly 40% of that was non-core. Core EPS was closer to **$1.7–$1.8**. Annualize that and AMZN at **$245.34** is trading around **34–36x core Q1 EPS**, not the ~22x headline annualized number. That is not a cheap stock.

And the mark-up is not just an accounting footnote. Private-equity holdings jumped from **$16.2B to $48.1B** in one quarter. That is a volatile asset whose value can reverse. If it does, reported earnings and book value take a hit. The bull says “ignore it.” Fine — but then AMZN looks more expensive on core earnings while FCF is negative. That’s not a comfortable combination.

## 5. The News Flow Is Long-Dated, Competitive, and Already Re-Run

Bull, you’re excited about Leo, Alexa+ ads, AWS AI, and sovereign cloud. I get it. But let’s be precise:

- **Leo**: 396 satellites deployed, “sufficient to begin an initial rollout.” But there are no subscriber or ARPU economics. It is well behind incumbent scale, and satellite-to-cell competition is heating up. That is long-dated optionality, not near-term earnings.
- **Alexa+ Agentic Ads**: interesting, but unproven. Privacy, regulatory, and adoption risks are real. Turning Alexa into an ad surface could also invite backlash.
- **AWS AI**: positive, but incremental. AWS faces intense competition from other hyperscalers, and custom silicon is expensive. “Loom” and sovereign cloud deepen the moat, but they don’t change the near-term FCF picture.
- **BofA target**: the report itself flags an internal inconsistency — a **$165 target** against a **$247** stock. Treat the direction as valid if you want, but the level is unreliable. That’s not a strong anchor for a bull case.
- **Mag 7 rotation**: small-cap ETFs beating the S&P as the Mag 7 stumble is a real headwind for mega-cap multiples. AMZN’s one-day **+1.4%** outperformance on July 9 was followed by a July 10 close of **$245.34**, near the low. One day is not relative strength.
- **Instacart “insulated” from AMZN’s grocery push**: the bull calls it immaterial. Maybe. But it’s a data point that AMZN’s grocery offensive is not denting incumbents. That chips away at the “new growth vector” narrative.
- **Agentic AI disruption**: the bull says AMZN is the disrupter. Maybe. But agentic commerce could also disintermediate traditional retail search. AMZN’s retail margins are thin; any share shift matters.
- **Bezos/Blue Origin stock sales**: minor, yes. But combined with unavailable insider data, there is no insider-confidence offset.
- **AI power shortfall**: data-center power constraints and electricity costs are a medium-term risk to the capex build-out. That is not in the bull’s model.

The bull says optionality is “priced at roughly zero.” That is an assertion, not evidence. At $245, AMZN is not a free call option. It is a mega-cap stock with a lot of capex, debt, and AI hope embedded in the price.
