## 2. The "3:1 payoff" is a probability error. Let's do the actual math.

This is the bull's centerpiece, and it's the weakest thing in his argument. He divides **+14.0% to the prior high** by **−4.9% to the 200-day** and calls it 3:1. That's not a payoff ratio. That's two price targets with **no weights attached**.

| Scenario | Prob. | Move (to technical targets) | Weighted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Down (246.56 → 239.6–241.0) | 45% | ~−4.5% | **−2.0%** |
| Chop (249.6–259.4) | 35% | ~0% | **0.0%** |
| Up (267.5 first, 280 secondary) | 20% | ~+7% | **+1.4%** |

Even if you grant him the *maximum* up target of 287.20 (+14%) on the 20% branch: EV ≈ **+0.8%**. That is not "decisively bullish." That's a coin flip with a **negative modal outcome** — 45% of the probability mass sits on a 4–6% decline.

And here's the trap: his preferred entry is a **close above 259.40**, which is 3% higher. The technical report itself puts that R:R at **1:1 to 2.5:1**, not 3:1. So he's blending the **unconfirmed band-touch entry at 251.89** (which the report explicitly says *not* to buy) with the **best-case target at 287.20**. You can't have the best entry and the best exit in the same trade. That's not a skew — that's a spreadsheet error dressed up as conviction.

## 3. "Capex is a choice" has a ceiling — and the depreciation wave is still ahead

I'll give the bull the operating momentum. Revenue +19.6%, operating income +43.2%, margins +230bp — those are real, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But his two key defenses have holes.

**On "contracted forward demand":** The bull calls **$650B of contractual obligations** a backlog. It isn't. It's a commitment to *buy* capacity, content, energy, and logistics — **the cost side, not the revenue side.** Yes, some may be take-or-pay capacity with implied demand visibility. But the disclosure is a *purchase obligation*. He's spinning a fixed-cost commitment as a customer backlog. That's a material misread, and it's the load-bearing beam of his risk dismissal.

**On "margins expanded despite D&A":** This is the bull's best point, and it's backward-looking. Q2 D&A was **$20.0B — but D&A is a lagging indicator.** It reflects capex from 2021–2024. Current capex is **$54.2B per quarter — roughly 2.7x the depreciation run-rate.** If capex holds near $200B/year and assets depreciate over ~6 years, **steady-state D&A trends toward ~$50B/quarter — more than double today's.** Even on a blended 10-year life, it's ~1.7x. **The depreciation bill from *this* capex is mostly ahead of us, not behind us.** The bull is extrapolating margin expansion before the invoice arrives.

**On "net cash":** He cites $176.1B cash + AFS against $146.4B debt + finance leases for **+$29.7B net cash**. That's the most generous definition available — it **excludes $96.3B of operating lease liabilities**. Include those and the picture is roughly **net debt of ~$67B**, not net cash. Reasonable people debate the treatment, but calling a company with $96B of lease liabilities "net cash" is at minimum incomplete, and at worst misleading.

**And "capex can flex down" is not a free option.** Cut it and you signal demand weakness — which de-rates the stock. Keep it and FCF stays negative and debt keeps climbing. With **$78.1B contractually due within 12 months**, a meaningful chunk isn't discretionary at all. That's a lose-lose, not a lever.

28x is not a "5% premium to the 200-day" bargain. It's a full multiple that requires everything to go right. And with **no dividend and no buyback**, there is **no capital-return floor** underneath the stock when the multiple compresses. The bull's entire case rests on the multiple holding while he waits for the trend to reassert. That's the assumption he never defends.
