## 1. The growth is real, and it's re-accelerating — that's the whole ballgame

Start here, because everything else is commentary:

| Metric | Q1-2026 | Q2-2026 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $181.5B | **$200.6B** | +10.5% QoQ |
| Revenue YoY | +16.6% | **+19.6%** | **Accelerating** |
| Operating income | $23.7B | **$27.5B** | +16.1% QoQ |
| Operating income YoY | — | **+43.2%** | — |
| Operating margin | 13.03% | **13.69%** | +226bp YoY |

On a **$800B+ annualized revenue base**, AMZN accelerated from 16.6% to 19.6% growth and expanded operating margin by 226 basis points *in the same quarter*. Operating income grew 43%. That is not a company running out of runway — that's a company whose mix has rotated into AWS and advertising and is now converting revenue into profit at a visibly higher rate.

And note the cost discipline underneath it, because this is where the bear's "spending is out of control" line falls apart:

- **SBC: 3.01% of revenue, down from 3.90%** — a 7.6% *decline* in absolute dollars against 19.6% revenue growth.
- **Marketing: 5.83% of revenue, down from 6.81%.**
- **G&A: 1.39%, down from 1.77%.**
- **Inventory flat at $38.2B** (−0.4% YTD) while quarterly revenue rose 19.6% — that's roughly 10x annualized turns. You do not get that from a bloated retailer.

Gross margin expanded 43bp to 52.26%. Every single discretionary line contracted as a percentage of revenue. This is operating leverage, and it's showing up in the cleanest line on the statement.

## 3. "Negative free cash flow" — this is the single most misread number in the market

TTM FCF is **−$11.6B**. H1-2026 FCF is **−$27.0B**. The bear will wave this around like a distress signal.

Here's what's actually happening:

- **TTM operating cash flow: $161.4B, +33.2%.** H1 OCF: $71.4B, **+44.2%**.
- **TTM capex: $173.0B, +60.7%.** H1 capex $98.4B, **+72%**, or 25.8% of revenue (27.0% in Q2 alone).

The business is generating *more* cash than ever. It is choosing to spend even more than that on capacity. That's the entire gap.

Now, why is that bullish rather than reckless? Because the spend is **contracted**, not speculative. Total contractual obligations stand at **$650.0B**, of which roughly **$387B sits in the "thereafter" bucket**. That is not a retail purchase-order profile — that's multi-year compute, power, and content commitments. You don't sign a $387B tail on a hunch. You sign it because customers have committed to buying.

The corroborating news flow from this past weekend points the same direction: AMZN reportedly **tripled its Nvidia GPU orders** and is expanding custom-silicon activity alongside its own in-house chips (with Marvell named as a key AI-cloud supplier partner). Treat the "tripled" figure with the skepticism it deserves — it's a secondary aggregation, not a filing — but the direction is consistent with everything else: **demand is outrunning supply, and Amazon is racing to build the capacity to serve it.**

Meanwhile, Microsoft just disclosed Azure crossing **$100B**. The bear calls that a negative AWS comparison. I call it *third-party validation that AI cloud is a real, enormous, monetizing market* — and AWS is the largest incumbent in it. If Azure is a $100B business, the addressable pie is bigger than anyone modeled two years ago.

**The right lens on AMZN's FCF is not "it's negative," it's "when does capex cross back under OCF?"** H1 capex was 1.38x OCF. Every quarter that ratio moves toward 1.0x, this stock re-rates. That convergence is the trade. The bear is anchoring on a trough in the ratio and calling it a trend.

## 4. "The balance sheet is levering up" — at 12% debt-to-assets with 21x coverage

Total debt went from $68.4B to $132.2B in six months. Sounds scary. Here's the context the bear omits:

| Metric | 2026-06-30 | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Cash + marketable securities | **$176.1B** (+32.8% YTD) | Fortress |
| Net cash ex-leases | **+$43.9B** | Still net cash |
| Debt / total assets | **12.1%** | From 8.4% — modest |
| Interest coverage (Q2 annualized) | **~21x** | No stress |
| Current ratio | 1.03 | Stable |

AMZN **still holds net cash of $43.9B excluding leases**, has $176B of liquid assets, and covers interest expense 21 times over. Yes, interest expense rose 155% — from a very small number to a still-small number ($1.31B in Q2 against ~$27.5B of quarterly operating income). This is a company funding a generational buildout at investment-grade rates while sitting on more cash than most sovereign funds. Calling that a solvency concern requires ignoring the balance sheet.

The one bear point I'll genuinely concede: **capex is 2.71x D&A**, so a depreciation headwind is coming regardless of how the AI bet resolves. But that cuts the other way too — depreciation lagging capex by that much means the *earnings power* of this buildout hasn't even begun to be reflected yet. The bear wants to charge AMZN today for costs that arrive tomorrow while ignoring revenue that arrives on the same schedule.
