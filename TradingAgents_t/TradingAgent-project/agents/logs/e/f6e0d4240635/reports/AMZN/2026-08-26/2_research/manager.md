**Rationale**: Let me be straight about how I scored this, because both analysts showed up with real evidence and I don't want to hide behind "it's complicated."

The bull genuinely wins one big argument, and it's the most important one over a multi-year horizon: the operating business is inflecting. Operating margin 11.8% → 11.4% → 13.1% → 13.7%, Q2 revenue +19.6% with operating income +43.2%, SBC down 7.6% in dollars, marketing up only 2.5%, G&A −6.0%, gross margin +45bp, inventory flat against 19.6% revenue growth. And they absorbed a 42% PP&E depreciation increase while still expanding margin 2.3 points. TTM OCF of $161.4B, +33.2%, growing faster than revenue and exceeding D&A plus SBC, is a real number and it undercuts any "the engine is broken" framing. I'm not going to pretend that's noise. It isn't.

But the bear won the four arguments that actually determine what we do with the position at $260.28, and three of them the bull never touched.

First, the depreciation curve. The bull's best card is the margin stair-step; the bear attached a bill to it that the bull's own numbers make inevitable. TTM capex $173.0B annualizing above $215B, on a 5–6 year life, implies roughly $29–35B/year of incremental depreciation landing over the next several quarters against a current D&A base near $78B — a +37% to +45% increase in that line, or ~1.7 to 2.3 points of revenue as a margin headwind. Against a 13.7% operating margin, that is not a rounding error. And the bear landed the decisive blow here: the bull set his own tripwire at "operating margin below ~12% while capex stays above $50B/quarter," and his own capex thesis makes that tripwire likely to trigger for mechanical reasons that have nothing to do with AI demand failing. When your bear signal doubles as the expected output of your bull thesis, you don't have a risk framework — you have a coin flip. The bull's counter, that they already absorbed the front edge, is fair but incomplete: he's extrapolating operating leverage forward while ignoring the lagged cost of the capex that is generating it.

Second, the balance sheet. The "fortress with an option attached" claim does not survive lease adjustment. Operating lease cost of $4,118M/quarter (~$16.5B/yr) and $96.3B of operating lease liabilities push fixed-charge coverage from the bull's headline 21× to roughly 5×, and turn his +$29.7B net cash into roughly −$66.7B of net debt. Long-term debt up 93% in six months, net cash deteriorating $21.8B, interest expense +155% to $1,314M, net interest swinging from +$569M to −$19M, a 1.03× current ratio, and $650B of contractual obligations against $215B+ of annual capex. The bear is right that AMZN is now the most rate-sensitive it's been in a decade, and the bull's "issued into strength" line is a description of every late-cycle capital program, not a rebuttal.

Third — and this is the one that decided my vote — the $39.8B of acquisitions. H1 acquisitions went from $1,652M to $39,767M, a 24× increase, Q2 alone $24,359M versus $1,700M, funded while free cash flow is −$11.6B and debt is doubling. The bull did not address this in a single sentence, and his valuation does not price a dollar of it. Buying unknown assets at peak-cycle prices with borrowed money, with unquantified goodwill and integration risk, is a capital-allocation governance question, not a sentiment question. "FCF is a choice" cuts both ways: a management team that chooses to lever up and buy $39.8B of unexplained assets while burning cash has demonstrated how it uses the throttle. The bear's demand — "name the quarter they'll choose to stop" — went unanswered.

Fourth, earnings and cash quality has a second leg the bull stripped out. TTM deferred tax of $41.4B against cash taxes paid of only $6.6B, and an H1 current tax position that was a net benefit of −$2,730M on $120.7B of pretax income, means cash taxes are running near negative. That flatters both EPS and the OCF number the bull leans on. Effective rate already moved 12.8% → 22.5% year over year. Layer on receivables +30.1% versus revenue +19.6% and allowance coverage slipping 3.42% → 3.08% — two of the bull's own watch items are already moving the wrong way, at the margin, right now. Also worth noting the mark is two-sided, not a ratchet: warrant fair-value losses of −$1,449M in Q2 / −$1,051M in H1. And the bull's claim that the $122.3B stake is a "hedge" is simply wrong on mechanics — a hedge pays when the other leg loses. If the AI complex de-rates, the mark, AWS expectations, and the operating multiple fall together. That's three units of the same beta, not a hedge.

On the tape, the bear's read is more faithful to the data the bull himself supplied. The bull quoted nine consecutive lower highs — 287.20 → 282.79 → 280.14 → 278.84 → 273.46 → 269.58 → 266.40 → 263.89 → 262.05 — against a flat floor at 257–259. He called that a coil; lower highs against a repeated flat floor is the textbook descending-triangle structure, and it usually resolves down. Price is below the 10 EMA ($261.97), the 20 SMA ($266.10), and VWMA ($265.53), so the marginal buyer is underwater and there's overhead supply at 265–266. The unfilled gap at 239.82–265.00 sits below price with roughly 40% already filled. But a rising 200 SMA is not a near-term catalyst, and "record-low 21.5M volume" reads at least as easily as paralysis ahead of two binary macro/semis events as it does as seller exhaustion.

Here's the clincher for me, and it's the bull's own trade construction. His trigger is a close above $266.10 — 2.2% above spot — and his invalidation is $257.04, 1.2% below spot. That means his own actionable conclusion is "do not own this here." The bear is asking for 3.6% to 8.4% of downside into a gap that's already half-filled, with NVDA earnings and inflation data on the calendar and no AMZN-specific catalyst until late October. The bull admits he has no offsetting catalyst for eight weeks. So both analysts, reading the same file, agree the current price is unattractive; they disagree only about which direction the resolution runs. On a mechanical-cost argument (depreciation, cash taxes), a leverage argument the bull's lease adjustment fails, an unaddressed $39.8B of borrowed-money M&A, and a near-term asymmetry with binary event risk and no idiosyncratic upside event — the bear's side is the stronger one, and it's stronger by enough to act, not enough to abandon the name.

I want to be explicit about what I do not have, because it bounds my confidence. There is no Q3 revenue or operating income guidance in the record, no updated capex guidance, no disclosure of what the $39.8B of H1 acquisitions bought, no counterparty breakdown on the $122.3B stake, no options-implied volatility or skew to price the event risk, and no positioning or short-interest data. The "AWS 37% growth" figure is an unverified author's assertion that the bull himself declined to lean on, so it carries zero weight for me. That missing guidance layer is precisely why this is Underweight (trim into a full price with a rising cost curve) and not Sell (exit a franchise whose operating margin, cost leverage and cash generation are demonstrably improving and whose long-term trend structure is unbroken).
