
Neutral Analyst: Let me take the neutral risk seat. I agree with the direction of the trader’s SELL/Underweight on AMZN, but I think both the aggressive and conservative versions are overfitted to one side of the evidence. The aggressive analyst is right that AMZN is in a corrective range inside a long-term uptrend, that the lower-high sequence matters, that MACD is still below zero and below signal, and that roughly 32x trailing normalized EPS plus negative free cash flow and a releveraging balance sheet justify reducing full-weight exposure. Where I think he is too optimistic is in assuming he can use 265.9–267.6 as a reliable residual exit and in dismissing the 9/21 tape improvement too quickly. The first close above the 50-SMA and mid-band in eight sessions, RSI back to 52.7, above-average volume, and price 1.85% above VWMA are not a confirmed reversal, but they are enough to make an aggressive bearish press risky. Selling at 258.45 is sensible de-risking; calling it a high-reward risk decision overstates the edge. Also, a 25% tactical long alongside an underweight is not a clean hedge; it is a second position with its own gap risk.

The conservative analyst is right that stops can be skipped and that AMZN’s July 31 gap and February 6 gap prove overnight risk is real. But cutting to 25–30% of standard benchmark now goes too far for a standard risk book. The evidence does not show distress. Revenue grew 19.6%, operating income grew 43.2%, operating margin was 13.69%, liquidity is $176B, and net cash is still around $30B. The negative free cash flow, $133B debt, $650B of contractual obligations, flat deferred revenue, and the mark-to-model EPS lift are real reasons to be underweight, but they are not reasons to treat AMZN as a broken company. The unknown warehouse wage headline is also being treated as a reason to cut harder, but the data gives no wage level, scope, or cost. An unknown binary is a reason to size moderately and avoid full weight, not to assume the worst outcome. Conservative sizing at 25–30% is appropriate for a caller with a very concentrated AMZN position or a low risk budget, but it is not the right default for a standard benchmark allocation.

The middle path is to keep the SELL/Underweight direction but make it staged and less dependent on a single exit. If the caller is at a full standard weight in AMZN, I would reduce to roughly 55–60% of standard weight now around 258.45, which is a 40–45% trim. If AMZN is already underweight, apply that proportionally rather than assuming a flat book. Use strength into 265.9–267.6 to reduce toward 40–45% of standard weight, but put in scaled limit orders instead of waiting for the whole exit there. If AMZN closes below 253.60, take the underweight toward 35–40%; below 250.5–251.9, toward 30%; below 244.30, toward 20–25%; and go flat or near flat on a close below 240.4. Treat 267.6 as the level where the intermediate bearish thesis is invalidated: if AMZN closes above that with a MACD crossover and a higher low, stop adding bearish exposure and reassess. Do not rely on the stop as a guarantee, because the earnings gaps show it can be skipped; size for the gap.

On the tactical long, I would cap it much lower than the aggressive analyst does. If the caller wants to trade the range, keep it to 10–15% of standard weight at most, only near the 253.6–256.1 support cluster, with a hard stop at 253.60, and label it separate from the core underweight. I would not use it as a hedge. The cleaner moderate path is simply to trim now, sell residual strength, and let a close above 259.77 with volume and a MACD cross turn the tactical bias neutral-to-slightly constructive before considering any add-back. That way we participate in AMZN’s operating strength if the repair continues, but we do not pay full weight for negative free cash flow, rising debt, $650B of commitments, and a compressed volatility regime that can resolve either way. The balanced answer is not to press an outright short and not to hide at 25% weight; it is to be meaningfully underweight, staged, and responsive to the 253.60 and 267.6 lines.

So my adjusted proposal remains SELL/Underweight on AMZN, with the staged trim and sizing above rather than the aggressive or conservative extremes.
