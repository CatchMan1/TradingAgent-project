## 4. Bear Counterpoints — Let's Have the Real Debate

### Bear point 1: "Free cash flow is negative — TTM FCF -$2.5B. That's a broken business model."

**My response:** FCF is negative *because capex is deliberately front-loaded against a demand curve management just said is booming*. Look at the actual sequence:

| Period | OCF | Capex | FCF |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $84.9B | $52.7B | +$32.2B |
| FY2024 | $115.9B | $83.0B | +$32.9B |
| FY2025 | $139.5B | $131.8B | +$7.7B |
| TTM Q1'26 | $148.5B | $151.0B | **-$2.5B** |

Operating cash flow grew **+$8.9B TTM**. Capex grew **+$57.9B TTM**. So the FCF compression is entirely a capex decision, not an OCF deterioration. OCF is still compounding. The moment capex growth decelerates relative to OCF — which is exactly what happens as AI infrastructure matures — this snaps back hard. You don't get to call a company "broken" when its operating cash generation is at an all-time high. You call it *investing*.

### Bear point 2: "Debt nearly doubled — LT debt $68.8B → $122.6B. That's reckless."

**My response:** It's a *balance-sheet choice*, and the liquidity makes it entirely manageable. `AMZN` holds **~$144B in cash and marketable securities** against $216.8B current liabilities — working capital swung to **+$27.3B**. Leverage on debt alone is roughly **0.29x debt/equity** against ~$438B equity. This is not a leverage crisis; this is a AAA-adjacent balance sheet terming out a once-in-a-generation infrastructure build. Yes, interest expense is up 48% YoY — at $800M a quarter against $23.9B quarterly operating income, that's a rounding error on coverage. The bear is describing a company that borrowed to survive. `AMZN` borrowed to *build*.
