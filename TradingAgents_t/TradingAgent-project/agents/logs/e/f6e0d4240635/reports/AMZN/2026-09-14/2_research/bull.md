## Growth: this is acceleration, not deceleration

Let's put the scale in perspective. A $200B *quarter*. And it grew **19.6% year over year**. That is a company adding roughly **$33B of quarterly revenue** — more than most S&P 500 companies generate in a year — and doing it while expanding gross margin by 45bp to 52.3%.

The bears love to talk about margin "peaking." Wrong direction. Incremental operating margin in H1 was **23.4%** — Q2 alone was over **25%**. For every incremental dollar of revenue, AMZN keeps a quarter of it as operating profit. That is high-quality, operating-leverage-driven growth, not growth bought with discounting.

And look at the cost line discipline, because this is where the "Amazon spends indiscriminately" narrative dies:
- Marketing grew **2.5%** against 19.6% revenue growth (6.8% → 5.8% of revenue)
- G&A **declined 6.0%**
- SBC **declined 7.6%**, now just 2.6% of revenue vs 3.2%
- Share count up only **0.5%** in six months

That is a management team running the P&L hard while simultaneously funding the biggest capex cycle in corporate history. You don't get to do both by accident.

## The financial "red flags," answered directly

**"Free cash flow is negative."** Yes — because **capex is $173B TTM, up 60.7%**, and Q2 capex was 27% of revenue. But look at what funds it: **TTM operating cash flow of $161.4B, up 33.3%.** OCF is growing *faster* than earnings, and cash conversion is roughly **1.9× core net income**. This isn't a business that can't generate cash — it's a business electing to convert cash into AI capacity. Those are very different things. And the "pre-funding" is backed by contract: the **$650B of obligations** the bear will call a risk is largely take-or-pay demand *commitments*. That's a backlog signal disguised as a liability.

**"Debt is up 93% to $133B."** From a low base, into a rate environment where AMZN can borrow at investment-grade spreads, with **interest coverage of ~24×** and **$176B of cash plus short-dated securities**, of which **$96.5B matures within a year**. Gross debt is ~1.4× annualized H1 EBIT. On a total-liquidity basis the company is still **net cash (–$29.7B)**. This is not a leverage problem; it's a balance sheet being optimized for a once-in-a-generation buildout.

**"EPS is distorted."** The bear wants to throw out $3.59/share of equity marks — fine, use **core EPS of ~$1.95 in Q2 (+26% YoY)** and **~$3.63 in H1 (+25.6%)**. Those are the numbers I'll underwrite, and they're excellent. But the bear can't have it both ways: if you're going to exclude the mark-up from earnings, you *must* add the **$122.3B asset (~$11.3/share pre-tax)** to the balance sheet. The bear wants to ignore the gain *and* ignore the asset. That's not conservatism; that's selective accounting.

**"Receivables are growing faster than revenue."** +30.1% vs +18.2% — worth monitoring, agreed. But the allowance rose 16.7% alongside it, inventory is *flat* with reserves *down*, and there's a plausible mix shift toward advertising and AWS receivables. This is a watch item, not a thesis-breaker.
