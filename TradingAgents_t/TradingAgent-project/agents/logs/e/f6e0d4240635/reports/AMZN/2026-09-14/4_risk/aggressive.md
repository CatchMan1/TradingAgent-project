The conservative analyst will almost certainly point to negative free cash flow, the 93% jump in long-term debt, and the $650B contractual obligation stack and say AMZN is too risky to hold. My response is that this confuses a capex supercycle with a solvency problem. AMZN just printed Q2 revenue of $200.6B, up 19.6%, operating income of $27.5B, up 43.2%, and a 13.7% operating margin, up 226 basis points despite depreciation rising 31%. Incremental operating margin was roughly 23% to 25%. That is not a deteriorating business. TTM operating cash flow is $161.4B, up 33.3%, and AMZN holds $78.2B of cash plus $97.9B of available-for-sale securities, with interest coverage around 24 times. Gross debt including finance leases is about $146.4B, roughly 1.4 times annualized first-half operating income. Yes, debt rose fast, and yes, TTM free cash flow is negative $11.6B because capex is running at $173B, or 27% of Q2 revenue. But that is discretionary growth investment in AI and cloud capacity, not a liquidity crisis. The conservative mistake is treating the capex bill as if it has no return path. The trader’s plan handles that risk properly: do not exceed standard allocation until capex converts to cash and debt and contractual obligations stabilize. That is not a reason to underweight the core today.

The conservative analyst will also say headline EPS is distorted. They’re right, but they’re drawing the wrong conclusion. Q2 EPS of $5.75 included roughly $3.59 per share of non-cash equity marks. Core EPS was about $1.95, up 26%. H1 core EPS was about $3.63 versus $2.89. So if you strip out the mark-to-model noise, AMZN still grew core earnings 25% to 26% on a massive revenue base. And the very item that distorts GAAP earnings is also a hidden asset: equity securities without a readily determinable fair value ballooned from $16.2B to $122.3B, roughly $11.3 per share pre-tax. The Anthropic news matters here. Anthropic has reportedly selected Nasdaq for an IPO, and Nvidia may invest up to $10B at a roughly $2T valuation. That is not confirmed and the valuation can move, but it is exactly the kind of asymmetric optionality the conservative view keeps ignoring. AMZN’s stake could get a public mark, and the $517B compute commitment reinforces AWS demand. The conservative analyst will say it’s unproven. Fine — that’s why we don’t lever up on it. But it is a reason to keep the core and be ready to add, not a reason to sell.
