You’re going to say AMZN is technically soft: **$248.23 close, below the 50-day SMA at $256.19, below the 10-day EMA at $249.66, below VWMA at $250.77, MACD negative, RSI only 44.20**. You’re going to say free cash flow is negative, debt has risen sharply, and headline EPS is distorted by a non-cash mark. All true. None of that makes AMZN a sell.

My call is straightforward: **AMZN is a Buy/Accumulate on this correction**, with the long-term thesis anchored in the operating inflection, AWS/AI competitive advantages, and a still-strong liquidity position. For traders, I’d respect the range—**above $250.77–$251.83 is confirmation; below $244.30 is near-term damage; below $241.22 would invalidate the strategic bull case**. ### 1. The bear is looking at a short-term chart; the fundamentals are accelerating

AMZN’s Q2 2026 revenue was **$200.6B, up 19.6% year over year**—an acceleration from +16.6% in Q1. H1 revenue was **$382.1B, +18.2%**. That is not a company losing momentum.

More important, the profit engine is inflecting:

- Q2 operating income: **$27.5B, +43.2% YoY**
- Q2 operating margin: **13.69%, +226 bp YoY**
- H1 operating margin: **13.43%, +181 bp**
- Q2 core EPS excluding the non-cash private-equity mark: **≈$2.16, +29% YoY**
- H1 core EPS: **≈$4.09, +25% YoY**

So yes, bear, the headline EPS of **$5.75 in Q2** is inflated by a **$50.5B non-cash mark**. I’m not going to defend that as cash earnings. But even after stripping it out, AMZN grew core EPS roughly **29%** on **43% operating-income growth**. That is real operating leverage, not accounting magic.

And the margin gains came from the right places: COGS down as a percentage of revenue, fulfillment/tech/content down, marketing down, G&A down, partly offset by depreciation. SBC was flat at about **3.0% of revenue**, and share count rose only **0.5% YTD**. That means the margin story is not being bought with dilution.

### 2. AWS and AI are not hype—AMZN is building the infrastructure positions

The bear will say “AI capex bubble.” I’d counter that the news flow shows AMZN is monetizing the buildout on multiple fronts.

First, AMZN signed a **multi-year deal worth more than $1B with Synopsys** for AWS custom silicon—directly supporting alternatives to Nvidia and improving AWS cost/performance differentiation. That is a competitive moat story, not just a spending story.

Second, the Anthropic signal is large: Anthropic reportedly has **more than $100B promised to AMZN**, and its preliminary Q2 revenue was **$11.6B, up roughly 14x YoY**. Is there counterparty and affordability risk? Yes. But the demand read-through for AWS is real, and it supports the idea that AMZN’s AI capacity is being pre-sold into a growing ecosystem.

Third, the **20-year nuclear PPA with Constellation** supports more than **$3B** of Maryland infrastructure investment, including about **190 MW of new capacity expected online between 2030 and 2032**. This is long-dated, but it addresses one of the biggest bottlenecks in AI/data centers: reliable power. That is a strategic advantage that many competitors cannot replicate quickly.

The bear case says AI spending is outrunning cash flow. Fair. But AMZN is not a one-product AI story. It has AWS, advertising, marketplace, Prime, logistics, and custom silicon. It can spread the investment across the largest cloud franchise and one of the strongest consumer platforms in the world. The **$650B contractual obligation book** is a risk if demand rolls over—but it is also evidence that AMZN is contracting future capacity against expected demand.

### 3. Negative FCF is an investment cycle, not a broken business

Bear, your strongest point is free cash flow. H1 2026 FCF was **−$27.0B**, TTM FCF was **−$11.6B**, and Q2 capex was **$54.2B, or 27.0% of revenue**. I’m not going to wave that away.

But look at the cash engine underneath it:

- Q2 operating cash flow: **$45.4B, +40% YoY**
- Q2 OCF margin: **22.6%**
- TTM OCF: **$161.4B**
- OCF/NI: **1.19x**
- Cash + AFS securities: **$176.1B**
- AFS maturities within one year: **$96.5B**
- Net cash excluding leases: **+$29.7B**
- Q2 interest coverage: **20.9x**

Long-term debt nearly doubled, yes—but interest coverage is still roughly **21x**, and AMZN has **$176B** of liquid assets. This is a company funding a generational AI/data-center build with internally generated cash and low-cost debt while preserving substantial liquidity. That is not distress; that is scale advantage.

And the balance sheet contains **$122.3B** of equity securities without readily determinable fair value—about **$11.22 per share pre-tax**—which gives AMZN optionality. The mark can reverse, but it is also a meaningful embedded asset.

That is a contracting range with a rising long-term trend underneath it. The bear wants to call that breakdown risk; I call it a setup where the long-term buyer gets paid if the operating thesis keeps improving.

The technical plan is clear: I would not chase the exact middle of the range. But for a bull, the right approach is to **accumulate on weakness toward $244.30–$245.60** or add on a confirmed close above **$250.77–$251.83** with volume. AWS, advertising, marketplace, and Prime economics provide a cushion. The news report itself notes consumer stocks were “barely flinching,” so the market is not treating this as an AMZN-specific shock.

The Oracle AI-capex concern is worth watching, but it is Oracle-specific. Meanwhile, Micron’s AI-driven earnings beat is a positive read-through for AI infrastructure demand. The AI-bubble commentary is speculative and political; I won’t build a thesis on it.
