
Conservative Analyst: I’ll take the aggressive case head-on, because it is well argued but it keeps slipping from “AMZN’s operating business is improving” to “therefore AMZN is safe to buy here.” Those are not the same statement. I agree with the aggressive analyst on the core operating fact: AMZN’s Q2-2026 operating income of $27.46B, up 43.2%, margin at 13.69%, and H1 core EPS growth of roughly 25.6% are real and important. Revenue grew 19.6% while marketing grew 2.5%, G&A fell 6%, and SBC fell 1.5%. That is genuine operating leverage, and I would not dismiss it. But the trader’s decision is not a claim on operating income; it is a purchase of AMZN equity at a specific price, after a 15.3% event gap, with ATR at 9.59 and 3.52% of price, and with the stock back inside the old $274.99–$278.56 ceiling. My job is to ask what can go wrong, not just what can go right. AMZN’s earnings quality is still a real risk. The aggressive analyst calls the $50.5B Q2 mark-up a “free option.” I don’t. It is 66% of pre-tax income, it is non-cash Level 3 valuation, and AMZN now carries $122.3B of non-marketable equity, roughly 20% of total assets. That can flatter GAAP EPS, it can reverse, and it makes the Q3 comparison mechanically ugly because the $50.5B lap will not repeat. The aggressive says the market re-rated AMZN on operating income, not marks. Fine. Then AMZN should be able to hold its value without the marks, and we should demand that rather than underwrite them as upside.

On free cash flow, the aggressive reframes negative FCF as AI capex. I accept that capex is strategic. But LTM FCF is still negative $11.6B, H1 FCF is negative $27.0B, capex is 107% of operating cash flow, gross long-term debt rose 93% to $133.0B, interest expense rose 155% year over year, and contractual obligations are $650B, about four times LTM CFO. Interest coverage at 20.9x is comfortable today, but the trend is not conservative. A conservative risk manager does not call $82B of H1 debt issuance “the cheapest capital in corporate history” without pricing the fixed-cost conversion, the depreciation wave that is already up 44% year over year, and the current ratio thinning to 1.03. That is exactly why the trader’s own cap at 1.0 unit until FCF stops deteriorating is right—and why I would go further and keep the cap lower until the cash-flow trend actually turns.

The biggest problem is the entry logic. The aggressive analyst says buying AMZN at $258 inside the $247–$262 confluence is disciplined. But the technical report itself says a close below $262.01 structurally invalidates the 2026-07-31 gap. A bid at $258 is below that invalidation line. That means the first tranche is not buying support; it is buying a partial gap fill after the breakout has already failed on a closing basis. That can be a mean-reversion trade, but it is not the low-risk version of this setup. If AMZN trades to $258, the burden should be on the bulls to show a reclaim of $262.01, not on us to catch the knife. I would rather bid the deeper confluence at $250–$255 near the VWMA and Bollinger mid, or wait for a close back above $262 after a test, or wait for the $278.50 volume-confirmed breakout. And if we do touch $258, the first tranche should be smaller than the plan’s 0.40 unit because the stop at $245 is only about 1.36 ATR from entry in a 3.52% ATR tape. A close-based stop is not a guarantee against an event gap; AMZN printed a roughly 9.7% two-session drop in February 2026. That is the tail we must size for.

The volume argument is constructive, but it is not proof. A decay from 129M to 30.7M on a 4.14% pullback can be profit-taking into a vacuum, or it can be absence of demand. MFI at 47.2 is not confirming. The MACD histogram has already rolled from 4.62 to 4.36. Price closed back inside the old ceiling. Bollinger width has doubled and ATR is up 39% in six sessions. The aggressive says the slow lines don’t lie. True, but they also don’t protect you from a full gap fill toward $239.82. The 50-SMA inflection from 246.38 to 247.45 is real, but it is small and slow, and price is still 10% above the 50-SMA and 15% above the 200-SMA. That is not a low-risk entry by conservative standards.

Since the neutral analyst has not spoken, I’ll say this to the likely neutral view: “constructive but not chase-able” is a fine observation, but it needs teeth. Waiting for a close above $278.50 on more than 60M shares is a valid trigger, but it is not sufficient by itself because AMZN just failed to hold that zone. I would want that breakout to hold for more than one session, and I would still keep size below normal because volatility is expanded. The neutral camp should not simply split the difference between buy and wait; it should insist on a smaller maximum position and a clearer invalidation point.

That is how we protect assets, minimize volatility, and still participate if the operating leverage story is real. I can support a conditional BUY on AMZN, but not an outright buy at the current price.
