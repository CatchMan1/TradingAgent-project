Investment Thesis: The debate splits cleanly along two questions and each side wins one of them, which is what makes this an Underweight trim rather than a Sell or a Hold.

The bull wins the ownership question, and all three risk analysts effectively concede it. Q1 FY2026 revenue rose 16.6% to $181.52B, operating income rose 29.6% to $23.85B, operating margin expanded ~130bps to 13.1% and gross margin ~120bps to 51.8%, on TTM operating cash flow of $148.5B, ~$144B of cash and securities, a current ratio near 1.18, and roughly $0.80B of quarterly interest expense against $23.85B of quarterly operating income. Price sits ~5% above a still-rising 200-SMA at $233.33, has reclaimed the 10-EMA at $243.05, and the MACD histogram has turned positive. That rules out Sell and rules out the aggressive analyst's framing of this as a high-conviction bearish bet: negative FCF at Amazon is a capex choice, not a solvency event, and the conservative analyst is right that a caller cannot assume a better opportunity exists to redeploy into when no portfolio context was supplied.

The bear wins the "at this price" question, which is the operative one. (1) Technical structure is unconfirmed, not repaired — price closed at $245.34 below a declining 50-SMA at $254.03, tagged the upper Bollinger band at $251.43 (intraday high $251.03) and closed near the session low on only 31.65M shares versus 248M on the 6/26 reversal (itself plausibly quad-witching/index flow, a distortion the neutral analyst correctly discounts). The MACD line at −1.523 remains below zero; a crossover inside a negative regime is a weak signal in either direction, so it does not license adding. (2) The asymmetry is poor: ~7.5% to the 227–229 shelf versus ~3.5% to the 50-SMA, with ATR at ~7.72 meaning one ordinary session can cover a third of the gap to support. (3) Fundamentals have a genuine quality problem — TTM FCF of −$2.47B against $151B TTM capex, quarterly capex at 1.85x quarterly operating income, long-term debt stepping from ~$68.4B to ~$121.9B in a single quarter with a record $53.4B issuance and interest expense +48% YoY, plus $91.62B of operating and $13.33B of finance lease liabilities. Add the February 2026 gap (222.69 prior close to 202.70 open on 179M shares) as demonstrated catalyst risk.

On sizing and execution the analysts genuinely disagree, and I side with the neutral analyst's middle path against both extremes. The aggressive analyst wants the entire trim worked into the 251–254 band, which assumes a bounce arrives before risk can be cut; the conservative analyst wants to lean to the lower half of the residual immediately, which risks an over-trim while the 200-SMA and support shelf are still intact. The defensible compromise is a residual of ~50–60% of standard, trimmed in stages: into strength at $248–251 and $251–254, remainder on a loss of $243 or a daily close below $237. All three analysts agree on the guardrails — no new money at $245.34, halve the residual below $237, exit below $227.01, re-add in thirds only after a high-volume close above $254 — and on rejecting $254.5 as a tight short stop, since at ~7.72 ATR it is inside normal noise.

Prior-analysis lessons are applied directly: the last three AMZN calls (Overweight, then two Holds) all produced thin or negative five-day alpha because the actionable level sat ~5% away and the position was left at full weight against an unconfirmed tape. The fix here is to act on the asymmetry rather than wait for a trigger 3.5% lower, and to attach dated re-scoring checkpoints — next earnings print — plus an index-relative opportunity-cost check, rather than letting a trim drift.

What would change it: two consecutive high-volume daily closes above $254 with MACD above zero and RSI >55 convert this toward Overweight (staged re-add in thirds); TTM FCF back above zero or capex/operating-cash-flow trending down with D&A growth not outrunning operating income growth validates further add-back; any segment-level ROIC or AWS margin disclosure tying the $151B capex to identified returns would justify a full weight. Explicit evidence gaps that must not be priced in either direction: no segment-level ROIC or AWS/retail profitability split, no rating-agency data to support the "AAA-adjacent" claim, no insider-transaction data, a partial news sample, and a BofA target that is internally inconsistent ($165 against a ~$247 stock) and usable only as direction.
