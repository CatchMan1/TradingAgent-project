**Investment Thesis**: Direction goes with the operating evidence; size, entry geometry and add discipline go with the neutral analyst, whose corrections no one rebutted.

What the aggressive analyst won, unrefuted by either colleague: AMZN grew Q2 revenue +19.6% (accelerating from +16.6%), operating income +43.2% to $27.5B, operating margin +230bp to 13.7%, and TTM operating cash flow +33.2% to $161.4B — with inventory flat at $38.2B, marketing +2.5% against +19.6% revenue, and SBC down to 3.0% of revenue. Margins expanded while D&A was already running +31% y/y and capex was at peak intensity, which is operating leverage outrunning depreciation, and it is the hardest fact in the debate to explain away. On valuation, ~32x core EPS against ~29% core EPS growth is roughly a 1.1x PEG on an accelerating, margin-expanding franchise — a full multiple, not an indefensible one, and the conservative seat never produced a peer or historical anchor to make "priced for perfection" bite.

The conservative and neutral seats nonetheless won the risk composition of the trade, and I adopt it. First, negative free cash flow is not a sentiment issue: TTM FCF is −$11.6B because capex reached $173.0B (+60.7% y/y), Q2 capex was $54.2B (~$217B annualized) and 27% of revenue, and — decisively — capex rose sequentially from $44.2B, which punctures the bull's "if capex merely flattens, FCF inflects" conditional and is why the RM's own catalyst list puts Q3 capex first. Capex is ~2.3x TTM D&A, so the depreciation bill is still ahead. Second, leverage is a trajectory problem: gross debt +92% to $132.2B in six months, interest expense +155% to $1.314B and a flip to net interest expense, quick ratio 0.875, $650B of contractual obligations with $78.1B due inside a year. It is not a solvency problem — +$30.4B net cash ex-leases, net debt including leases under five months of operating cash flow, and Q2 interest at roughly 5% of $27.5B operating income — but the conservative is right that we underwrite the direction, not the snapshot. Third, earnings quality: $62.8B of H1 mark-up takes GAAP EPS to $5.75 versus ~$2.16 core, and the $122.3B non-marketable equity book is ~11.2% of assets, self-marked with no disclosed inputs. The aggressive's "latent strategic value" framing cannot be counted as a free option while the reversal risk is discounted; the correct treatment is the RM's — underwrite core EPS ex-mark-up, which still grew ~29%, and treat any downward mark as a size-reduction event, not a dip to buy.
