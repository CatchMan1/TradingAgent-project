
Bull Analyst: **Bull opening for AMZN — I’ll take the other side of the “broken momentum” trade**

But here’s my challenge to the bear: **that is a timing problem, not a thesis problem.** The bear needs AMZN’s operating momentum to be rolling over. The data says the opposite.

### 1) The growth engine is accelerating, not maturing

AMZN just printed **Q2 2026 revenue of $200.61B, +19.6% YoY** — the first $200B quarter — and that was an *acceleration* from Q1’s **+16.6%**. At roughly an $800B annualized revenue base, accelerating growth is rare and powerful.

Even better, it came with real operating leverage:

- **Q2 operating income: $27.46B, +43.2% YoY**
- **Operating margin: 13.69% vs 11.43%** a year ago, a **+226bp** improvement
- **Gross margin: 52.26%, +45bp YoY**
- Marketing grew only **+2.5%**, G&A fell **−6.0%**, SBC fell **−7.6%**

That is not a company losing control of costs. That is a scaled flywheel converting revenue growth into profit growth at roughly **2.2x** the rate of sales.

The bear will say, “Yes, but free cash flow is negative.” I’ll get to that. But first, the competitive advantages are doing exactly what they should: AWS is the cloud/AI backbone, advertising is high-margin, the marketplace and Prime create logistics and demand density, and the custom-silicon/AI infrastructure build is widening the moat. The news flow also gave us **Best Buy/Fire TV advertising expansion** and an **international seller inbound program** — not needle-movers alone, but exactly the kind of ecosystem monetization that supports the long-term margin story.

### 2) The bear’s FCF argument is real — but it’s an investment-cycle argument, not a demand argument

Yes, AMZN’s TTM free cash flow is **−$11.6B**, versus **+$13.5B** a year ago. The bear will wave that around. But look underneath:

- TTM operating cash flow: **$161.4B, +33.2%**
- H1 2026 OCF: **$71.4B, +44.2%**
- TTM capex: **$173.0B, +61%**
- Q2 capex: **$54.2B, +68% YoY**, equal to **27% of revenue**

AMZN is not failing to generate cash. It is deliberately converting enormous cash generation into AI/cloud/logistics capacity. That is a choice — and a strategically rational one given AWS demand commentary and the reported **~$500B contracted backlog**.

And the balance sheet can carry it:

- Cash + available-for-sale securities: **$176.1B**
- Total debt incl. finance leases: **$146.4B**
- Still **net cash on a narrow basis**
- Total debt / TTM OCF: **0.91x**
- H1 interest coverage: **24.3x**

The bear says debt nearly doubled. True — long-term debt rose to **$133.0B**, total debt **+80.5%**. But this is not a leveraged distress story. AMZN has **$161B of operating cash flow**, **$176B of liquidity**, and interest coverage above **24x**. If capex merely stops accelerating, FCF snaps positive quickly. The FCF bear case requires capex to keep compounding forever without revenue catching up. The operating data does not support that.

### 3) The GAAP EPS scare is noise; core operating earnings are strong

The bear may also point to earnings quality. Fair point: reported Q2 EPS of **$5.75** included a huge non-cash equity remeasurement — roughly **$50.5B** in Q2 and **$62.8B** in H1. Core EPS was closer to **$2.15** in Q2.

But that cuts *in favor* of the bull case when you frame it correctly: even after stripping out the accounting windfall, AMZN’s core EPS grew about **+28% YoY** in Q2 and **+25%** in H1. Meanwhile operating income grew **+43.2%**. The non-cash mark is headline noise. The operating leverage is the signal.
