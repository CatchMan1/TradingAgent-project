**Investment Thesis**: All three risk seats converge on lower exposure; the dispute is only degree, and the degree is settled by which evidence is load-bearing. The Aggressive seat's best contribution is not its directional argument but its procedural one — a pre-committed, trigger-driven trim beats a static full position into a binary print — and on that point he is right, and it is why this is a reduction rather than a hold. But his two load-bearing bullish pillars fail on the merits, and both failures are conceded or unrefuted.

First, the "$569.3B contracted backlog." The fundamentals are explicit that the $569.3B (with $57.1B due inside twelve months) is predominantly AWS compute/power/chip purchase commitments — money Amazon owes on a fixed schedule, not money customers owe Amazon. Relabeling the single largest fixed-cost liability on the balance sheet as demand visibility inverts its sign, and the FCF/leverage argument built on it collapses with it.

Second, "the depreciation wave already arrived because margins expanded anyway." TTM D&A of ~$70.4B against a ~$151.0B TTM capex run-rate means depreciation is running at less than half of capex; Q1 2026 capex of $44.2B (+76.7% y/y) enters the depreciation schedule only when placed in service, quarters from now. D&A +32.8% against revenue +16.6% is therefore a forward squeeze into 2027, not a headwind already absorbed — and it sits inside a stock already being re-rated for spending too much.

What decides the direction is the balance-sheet turn, because it is the actual driver of the multiple: net cash fell from +$63.8B to +$22.1B in a single quarter, long-term debt went $68.8B → $121.9B (nearly doubled), interest expense +47.9% y/y, and capex incurred but not yet paid rose to $27.0B from $16.8B — meaning reported FCF of −$2.5B understates the real drain. The trajectory matters more than the level: FCF +$32.9B → +$7.7B → −$2.5B while capex grows +82% versus OCF +28%. The Conservative's framing — current ratio is fine, the trajectory is not — is the correct one; the Aggressive's "+$22.1B net cash, 30x coverage, 21.4% ROE" rebuts solvency, which nobody contested, and never rebuts direction of travel. The Conservative's mark-to-model point also stands unanswered: ~31% of Q1 net income came from private-AI-stake revaluations, a two-sided mark that can produce an optical EPS miss while the operating story is intact — precisely the kind of item that gaps rather than drifts.
