
Bear Analyst: **Bear Analyst — Rebuttal on AMZN**

You asked me to show a broken thesis. I don’t need one. A stock can underperform without the company being broken. My case is simpler: **at 253.54, AMZN is a great operating business priced for a capex cycle to work, with negative free cash flow, a fast-rising debt load, and a technical picture that puts supply directly overhead.** That is not a coiled spring I want to buy. It is a coin flip with a poor margin of safety.

Let me take your points directly.

---

## 1) “Negative FCF is a choice” — agreed. But it’s a 6.5x capex-to-incremental-profit choice.

You’re right that TTM operating cash flow of **$161.4B, +33.3%**, is real and strong. But the filing also shows **TTM capex of $173.0B**, producing **TTM FCF of –$11.6B**. That is not a rounding error. It is a fundamental shift in the financial profile.

And here is the number that matters most: **Q2 capex was $54.2B against Q2 incremental operating income of roughly $8.3B.** That is about **6.5 dollars of capex for every 1 dollar of incremental quarterly operating income.** You call that investment. I call it a bet that has not yet been proven by cash returns.

You say the spending is backed by contracts. Let’s examine that.

## 2) The $650B “backlog” is not a backlog — it’s a spending commitment.

This is the weakest part of your argument. The filing data show **$650B of contractual obligations**, with **$78.1B due within 12 months** — roughly **48% of TTM operating cash flow**. The fundamental report describes these as largely **purchase commitments for cloud capacity, energy, content, and logistics**.

Those are **AMZN’s obligations to pay**, not necessarily customers’ take-or-pay commitments to buy. You are reframing a liability as a backlog without the footnote that proves it. Unless you can show these obligations are matched by binding customer contracts at attractive margins, you cannot treat them as demand. You should treat them as **inflexible spending** in a negative-FCF period.

That is the core bear point: the capex cycle is becoming contractual just as free cash flow has turned negative.

## 3) The balance sheet is liquid, but the trajectory is the risk.

Yes, AMZN has **$176B of cash plus short-dated securities**, **24x interest coverage**, and gross debt at only about **1.4x annualized H1 EBIT**. I’m not calling this a solvency crisis.

But look at the direction: **long-term debt went from $68.8B to $133.0B in six months — +93%.** Interest expense doubled year over year. The **current ratio is 1.03x**. Accounts receivable grew **+30.1%** against revenue growth of **+18.2%**. Deferred revenue is flat at about **$25B**, so it provides no evidence of accelerating committed demand.

The “net cash on a total-liquidity basis” label is a snapshot. The bear case is about the trend: negative FCF plus rising debt plus $78.1B of near-term obligations. That is less flexibility than the headline liquidity number implies.

## 4) The Anthropic asset is real — but you’re capitalizing a headline.

I agree with you on one thing: if we exclude the **$3.59/share** of non-cash equity marks from Q2 EPS, we should use **core EPS of ~$1.95 in Q2 and ~$3.63 in H1**. Those are the right earnings numbers. They grew ~26% and ~25.6%, respectively. That is genuinely good.

But your treatment of the **$122.3B Anthropic/equity stake** is too generous. The filing says it is **equity securities without a readily determinable fair value**. It ballooned from **$16.2B to $122.3B in six months — +655%** — and now equals about **11.2% of total assets**. It is mark-to-model, non-cash, and can reverse with equal speed. There is an associated deferred tax liability on eventual disposal.

You want to add **~$11.3/share pre-tax** to the valuation. Fine — but only after a deferred-tax and liquidity haircut. And you cannot use an unconfirmed IPO headline and a reported but unconfirmed Nvidia anchor talk as hard value. The news report itself says the evidence is **headline/summary only, 20 of 188 records supplied, with recycled items and no company confirmation**. A ~$2T pre-IPO valuation is exactly the kind of number that can reset lower.

I’m not ignoring the asset. I’m refusing to pay full price for a mark-to-model stake while the core business is burning free cash flow.

## 5) The competitive “moat” evidence is thinner than you suggest.

You cite Anthropic’s **$517B compute commitment** and the UPS volume cut as proof the moat is widening. But the fundamental data package explicitly says **segment revenue, AWS growth, and segment operating income are not exposed**. So your moat claim is not coming from the financials. It is coming from headlines.

And those headlines cut both ways. The Anthropic compute commitment may validate demand, but it also raises **counterparty and financing sustainability** questions. If Anthropic is committing $517B to compute, AMZN may have to spend even more capex before it sees the cash. The UPS story is mixed — less third-party parcel capacity can create fulfillment friction — not a clean moat proof.

## 6) The technicals: the coil is real, but the upper stack is against you.

You’re right that the **200-day SMA at 239.93 is rising** and price is **5.67% above it**. That is why I’m not calling for a collapse. The primary trend is up.

But at **253.54**, AMZN is below the **50 SMA at 255.47**, below the **10 EMA at 256.04**, below the **VWMA at 257.37**, and below the **Bollinger mid at 258.50**. That is a four-layer overhead supply stack. The stock lost the 50 SMA on **2026-09-09** and has not reclaimed it.

Momentum is not washed out. **RSI is 45.84** — not oversold, and with no bullish divergence. The July bottom had RSI at **33.3**. There is room for another leg lower without any technical violation. **MACD is –1.05**, below zero, after a bearish cross on **2026-08-17** and a zero-line cross on **2026-09-09**. The histogram narrowing from –1.68 to –1.06 is mostly the signal line collapsing toward zero, not the MACD line turning up.

And the volume signal you call “controlled consolidation” includes the most important recent event: **2026-08-28 closed at 266.43 on +3.97% and 49.55M shares, then immediately reversed and never traded back above it.** That is a high-volume failed breakout — supply overhead, not noise.

The **2026-09-14 higher low at 250.74 versus 249.58** is a 1.16-point difference, about **0.19 ATR**. That is noise, not stabilization.

It doesn’t. I’m not predicting default downside. I’m saying the burden of proof is on the buyer at **253.54**, and the evidence does not give you a margin of safety.

If AMZN reclaims **258.50** on volume above **35M** and holds **257.37**, I’ll respect the bull trigger. But buying before that is not an edge. It is buying into resistance because the coil is tight. The coil can resolve up — but then you buy the confirmation, not the middle.
