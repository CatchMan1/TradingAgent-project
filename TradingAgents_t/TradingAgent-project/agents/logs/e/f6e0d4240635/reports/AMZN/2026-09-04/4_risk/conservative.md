First, the aggressive analyst keeps saying negative free cash flow is a deliberate capex choice. I agree that capex is a choice. But the consequences of that choice are not optional. AMZN generated TTM operating cash flow of 161.4 billion, yet TTM free cash flow was negative 11.6 billion because capex was 173.0 billion, up 60.7 percent year over year. Q2 capex alone was 54.2 billion, roughly a 217 billion annualized run-rate, and capex was 27 percent of Q2 revenue. That is not a small investment nuance. That is a capital-intensity regime change. The aggressive analyst says waiting for clean free cash flow means missing the 253.50 support entry. My response is that buying support in a negative-FCF, debt-funded capex cycle is not automatically asymmetric. Sometimes support breaks precisely because the financing math gets less comfortable.

The leverage objection is also being minimized too quickly. Net cash ex-leases of 30.4 billion sounds reassuring until you include operating leases, which puts AMZN at roughly 65.9 billion of net debt. Gross debt excluding finance leases rose from 68.8 billion to 132.2 billion in six months, up 92 percent. Interest expense rose 155 percent year over year in Q2, and AMZN actually flipped to a small net interest expense in Q2 for the first time in the modern era. The aggressive analyst says interest is only about 5 percent of operating income. That is true today, but the trajectory is what matters. Debt nearly doubled in six months, and the company has 650 billion of contractual obligations, with 78.1 billion due within twelve months. A conservative analyst does not ignore that because operating income is currently strong. We underwrite the downside, not the current snapshot.

The mark-to-model issue is another place where the aggressive case leans too heavily on the upside. The 122.3 billion of non-marketable equity securities is now about 11.2 percent of total assets, and 62.8 billion of H1 pre-tax income came from upward revaluations of those assets. The trader’s plan correctly says to value AMZN on roughly 32x core EPS, not reported EPS. Good. But 32x core EPS against roughly 29 percent core EPS growth is still a full multiple for a company with negative free cash flow, rising debt, and a depreciation curve that is compounding at over 30 percent. The aggressive analyst calls the 122.3 billion portfolio latent strategic value. I call it an illiquid, reversal-prone mark that a conservative risk process should discount, not count as a free option. And the plan itself admits AWS segment revenue and margin and Q3 guidance are not verifiable from the provided record. That is a reason to keep size small, not to build to 1.25x.
