## 1. The FCF "Capex Choice" Argument Is the Bull's Biggest Self-Inflicted Wound

| Period | OCF | Capex | FCF |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $84.9B | $52.7B | +$32.2B |
| FY2024 | $115.9B | $83.0B | +$32.9B |
| FY2025 | $139.5B | $131.8B | +$7.7B |
| TTM Q1'26 | $148.5B | $151.0B | **−$2.5B** |

The bull reads this as "OCF grew +$8.9B, capex grew +$57.9B, therefore it's just a capex decision." **That's exactly the problem.** Look at the *rate of change*: OCF adds roughly $9B a year. Capex added $58B in a year. The gap is widening **faster than OCF can close it.** The bull's entire "it snaps back hard the moment capex decelerates" thesis requires the *deceleration to actually show up.* It hasn't. Q1 2026 capex was **+77% YoY** — the single steepest jump in the series. And the 10-K disclosed **$27.0B of capex incurred but not yet paid** (up from $16.8B), which means the cash out the door in the *next* few quarters is already committed and rising.

So the bull isn't describing a mature investment that's about to normalize. He's describing an investment that is still **accelerating into a hole that debt is filling.** That's not "investing" — that's a cash-flow profile with no visible inflection point in the evidence we have. Call me when the bull can point to a quarter where capex growth decelerated relative to OCF. There isn't one in this dataset.

**And here's the tell the bull never addresses:** if this were as clean and self-funded as he claims, why is AMZN borrowing hand-over-fist to do it? Q1 2026: **$53.4B of new long-term debt** and $6.0B short-term. TTM LT debt proceeds $68.4B versus roughly zero in 2023–2024. You fund a "moat" with operating cash when the moat is real and cash-generative. You fund it with debt when the spend is outrunning the cash. The financing flow of **+$52.8B in a single quarter** is the loudest signal in this entire fundamental package, and the bull spends his whole answer explaining it away.

It's a *rising* cost against earnings that are already weaker than headline on a normalized basis (point 3).

The bull says "AMZN borrowed to build, not to survive." I agree with the motive. **Motives don't pay coupons.** The balance sheet is absolutely still liquid — I'm not calling solvency risk. I'm calling *narrative* risk: the "FCF-compounding, buyback-funding, low-leverage" story that has justified AMZN's premium multiple for a decade is now a *negative-FCF, re-leveraging, capex-debt* story. Meanwhile operating income grew **+29.6%** and revenue **+16.6%.** Sooo... operating income +29.6% but normalized EPS only +6.3%? **Where did the operating beat go?** The bull skips right past this. The answer is: D&A exploded to **$18.9B (+33% YoY)** as capex hits the P&L, interest expense rose 48%, and the effective tax rate jumped 300bps (24.0% vs 21.0%). 

**That D&A point is the bearish time bomb nobody is pricing.** The capex ramp doesn't just consume cash — it front-loads depreciation that *compresses operating margin going forward*. The bull is celebrating 130bps of margin expansion in the very quarter *before* the depreciation wave fully lands. The $27B of incurred-but-unpaid capex will convert into future D&A. If operating margin expansion is already being partly offset by rising D&A and it's only going to intensify, the "operating leverage" story gets a lot thinner in the coming quarters.

And the $12.3B mark-up? The bull calls it "real embedded optionality." Let's call it what it is: **a non-cash, mark-to-model revaluation of illiquid private stakes** sitting in a **$48.1B** bucket that grew by $31.9B in a single quarter. In a risk-off tape or an AI-valuation reset — which is precisely the scenario the IBM headline just invoked — these marks reverse and hit reported net income. That is *earnings volatility dressed up as strength*, and it's a live risk given how much of the "cheap on earnings" framing depends on headline EPS.

The bull asked me to show that "+29.6% operating income is a deteriorating business." I won't — that's a straw man. I'll show you that **a +29.6% operating-income line buying only +6.3% normalized EPS growth is a business where the capital intensity is eating the earnings**, and where the headline is flattered by a mark-to-model gain that can vaporize. That's not a deteriorating business. It's a business whose *reported profitability no longer flows cleanly to shareholders.*
