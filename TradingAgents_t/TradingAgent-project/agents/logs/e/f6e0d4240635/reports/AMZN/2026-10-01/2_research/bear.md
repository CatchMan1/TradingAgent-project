### 2. The FCF and balance-sheet story is worse than “investment cycle”

You’re right that Q2 operating cash flow was strong: **$45.4B, +40% YoY**, with OCF/NI at **1.19x**. But OCF is not FCF. AMZN’s H1 2026 FCF was **-$27.0B**, and TTM FCF was **-$11.6B**. Q2 capex alone was **$54.2B, or 27.0% of revenue**. Annualizing that run-rate implies roughly **$217B** of capex against TTM OCF of **$161.4B**. That is not a small timing issue; that is a structural cash deficit funded by debt and balance-sheet capacity.

And the balance sheet is not the “fortress” you’re describing. Long-term debt rose from **$68.8B to $133.0B**, up **93.2%** in six months. Total debt plus finance leases rose from **$81.1B to $146.4B**, up **81%**. Net cash excluding leases is only **$29.7B**, down from **$51.4B** at year-end. Including operating leases, AMZN is in **net debt of $66.7B**, versus **$37.9B** at year-end. The current ratio is **1.03x**. Interest expense rose **155% YoY** to **$1.31B**, and interest income of **$1.30B** only just offsets it. Interest coverage is still **20.9x**, but the direction is wrong.

Then there’s the **$650B contractual obligation book**. You frame it as evidence of demand. I frame it as a massive forward commitment against **$176B** of liquid assets and negative FCF. If AI demand meets expectations, fine. If it doesn’t, those obligations become a cost problem, not a demand signal.

### 3. Earnings quality is still a serious issue

You correctly conceded that headline EPS is distorted. But I don’t think you’ve priced the risk properly. H1 2026 pre-tax income of **$120.7B** included **$62.8B** of non-cash upward marks on private equity securities—over **half** of pre-tax income. Q2 headline EPS was **$5.75**, but core EPS was roughly **$2.16**. H1 headline EPS was **$8.53**, but core was roughly **$4.09**.

Yes, core EPS still grew about **25–29%**, and that is genuine. But the market is not paying for core EPS—it’s paying for a story that includes a **$122.3B** private-equity stake, equal to about **11.2% of assets**, whose value is model-based and can reverse. Every **10% markdown** is roughly **$12.2B pre-tax**, or about **$1.12/share**. And the **$38.1B AOCI increase** is another non-cash book-value boost that the data doesn’t cleanly explain. That doesn’t scream “high-quality earnings.”

On valuation, using the report’s core TTM EPS of roughly **$7.9–8.0**, AMZN at **$248.23** is about **31x core earnings**, not the ~20x headline multiple. For a company with negative FCF and rising debt, that is not cheap. The headline P/E flatters the stock by roughly **55%** on the report’s own math. That is a bear point, not a bull point.

### 4. The AI news is long-dated and double-edged

You’re leaning hard on the Constellation PPA, the Synopsys deal, and Anthropic. Let’s be precise.

The **Constellation PPA** is strategically sensible, but it is a **20-year power purchase agreement**, not an AMZN equity investment. The **$3B** is infrastructure investment supported by the deal, not necessarily AMZN’s payment. The **190 MW** of new capacity comes online between **2030 and 2032**. Near-term earnings impact: negligible. It is a long-dated option, not a catalyst.

The **Synopsys deal** is worth more than **$1B** over multiple years. That’s positive for AWS custom silicon, but it is small relative to AWS scale and the summary is truncated. It does not change the near-term FCF problem.

Anthropic is the biggest potential positive—but also the biggest red flag. The **>$100B promised to AMZN** is from a **draft prospectus**, with affordability explicitly questioned. It is not confirmed backlog. It creates **customer concentration and financing risk**. If AI funding tightens, that commitment becomes a headline risk, not a moat. The Oracle read-across matters: Oracle is down **60% from its high** on AI-capex-versus-cash-flow concerns. AMZN is not immune to that repricing logic.

The bull case treats announcements as backlog. The evidence doesn’t support that yet.

### 5. Competitive and macro risks are not “second-order”

You say AMZN is not a pure retailer. True. But retail still matters. Consumer confidence is at its **lowest since 2014**, and Target is cutting prices on nearly **2,000 items**—building on more than **10,000 reductions**—explicitly to compete with Walmart and AMZN. Power constraints are real, and AMZN’s solution is mostly **2030+**. The market is being asked to pay today for capacity that may or may not earn its cost of capital.
