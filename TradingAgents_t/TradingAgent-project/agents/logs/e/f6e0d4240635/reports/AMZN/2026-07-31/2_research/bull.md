That is the empirical proof that this capex cycle is **productive capital**, not a hole. The bear wants you to model $220B of capex as future FCF destruction. But the depreciation from the *previous* $173B of capex is already flowing through, and margins went **up**, not down.

## Now let me take the bear's four best shots.

### Bear shot #1: *"Earnings quality is terrible — core EPS is $2.16, not $5.75."*

**Concede the fact, destroy the conclusion.** Yes, $50.5B of the $62.6B Q2 net income is a non-cash revaluation. I'm not going to defend the headline GAAP number — I'll value the business on operating income, full stop.

But here's what the bear's own adjustment reveals: **even fully stripped of every non-operating item, core EPS is up ~26–40% year-over-year** (Q2 core ≈$2.16 vs ~$1.54 prior year; H1 core $3.63–$4.06 vs ~$2.89). The *operating* engine grew operating income +43%. Strip the marks and the growth is still spectacular.

And the mark-up isn't fake, it's *early*. Amazon's private equity book is a real portfolio of AI-era stakes whose value the public market is now acknowledging. When those positions IPO or mark up further, you'll get the same "bad quality" criticism — while the book value compounds. The accounting treatment is a presentation issue, not a value issue.

**Bottom line: the bear's best data point is an argument for using EV/EBIT, which makes AMZN look *better*, not worse.**

### Bear shot #2: *"Free cash flow is negative — TTM −$11.6B."*

Operating cash flow grew **+33% TTM to $161.4B**, and **+44% y/y in H1**. The cash engine is roaring. FCF is negative for exactly one reason: **capex went to $173B TTM, +61%.** That's a discretionary allocation decision made *because* the demand is there.

And the tax line is a genuine cash tailwind the bear won't mention: **cash taxes paid were $4.0B on $120.7B of pretax income in H1** (vs $5.6B last year). Deferred tax of $30.5B on the marks means real cash is staying inside the business. That's ~$26B of tax deferral assist to fund the build.

Would I prefer positive FCF? Obviously. But I'll take a negative-FCF Amazon spending into 37%-growth cloud demand over a positive-FCF Amazon that *isn't* building. The 2022 analogue the bear wants to invoke was a company with decelerating growth and an over-built logistics network. **This is a company with accelerating growth and a capacity shortage.** Not the same trade.

### Bear shot #3: *"Debt nearly doubled — $132.2B, interest expense +155%."*

Net cash is **still +$30.4B** after the entire debt raise. Cash plus securities is **$176.1B, up 32.8%.** Interest coverage is **20.9x**. The bond market absorbed **$82B of new paper in twelve months** into a company with a 13.7% operating margin expanding at +43% — that's not distress, that's the cheapest financing in the world being used to build the highest-return asset in the world.

And against those obligations sits **$650B of contractual commitments** — which the bear frames as a liability. I frame it as the mirror image of the demand backlog. You don't sign $650B of AI/cloud capacity and content commitments if you think you can't sell it. That's a *revenue* pipeline with a payable attached.

At the current burn, yes, the net-cash cushion could flip in 2–3 quarters *if* nothing changes. But operating income is compounding at 43%. That's the offset the static model misses.
