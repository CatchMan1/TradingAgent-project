11. Indicator selection rationale

I used 8 indicators across four non-redundant dimensions:

  • close_50_sma + close_200_sma — separate medium- from long-term trend and expose the flattening 50-SMA against a still-rising 200-SMA. This is the core of the "three-speed market" thesis.
  • close_10_ema — the only fast trend measure used; it dates the failed bounce precisely (09-23) rather than leaving it to eyeballing. I deliberately did not add a second short average, to avoid redundancy.
  • macd + macds/macdh — the zero-line, signal-line and histogram together distinguish "bearish but decelerating" from "bearish and re-accelerating." The 09-22 histogram near-zero then re-widening is the key non-obvious finding.
  • rsi — independent, bounded momentum oscillator to test whether the decline is oversold (it is not) and to check for divergence.
  • boll_ub + boll_lb (with the snapshot's mid-line) — gives both envelope position and band width, which is what actually quantifies the volatility compression.
  • atr — converts every level into a risk unit and shows the 42% volatility collapse.
  • vwma — the only volume-integrated price measure; it confirms the decline is volume-endorsed (price below VWMA) even though absolute volume is light.

Deliberately excluded: boll (the mid-line is already in the verified snapshot and is functionally the 20-SMA), and any second oscillator — adding one would duplicate RSI's information while inviting conflicting signals.

6. Bull Case vs Bear Case (evidence-based)
Bull case
  1. Demand re-acceleration at scale: revenue growth accelerated from +16.6% (Q1) to +19.6% (Q2), with the first $200B quarter. This is the opposite of what a maturing mega-cap usually does.
  2. Operating leverage is real and broad-based: Q2 operating income +43% on +19.6% revenue; marketing intensity down 98bp, G&A down, SBC down, gross margin up 45bp.
  3. Cash generation is intact: TTM OCF +33% to $161.4B; H1 OCF +44%. The FCF deficit is a choice (capex), not a demand problem.
  4. Balance sheet can fund it: $176B cash+securities, net cash on a narrow basis, debt/OCF <1x, interest coverage >20x.
  5. Hidden asset value: $122.3B of non-marketable equity stakes (up from $16.2B) is not reflected in operating earnings but is real optionality.
  6. Minimal dilution: share count +0.5% in six months.
Bear case
  1. Free cash flow has turned negative: TTM −$11.6B vs +$13.5B a year ago; H1 deficit of −$27.0B. FCF-based valuation models break down.
  2. Capex is still accelerating into an unproven demand curve: $54.2B in a single quarter (+68% YoY) at 27% of revenue, with capex/D&A at 2.30x. If AI demand normalizes, depreciation hits 2027–2028 P&L with no offsetting revenue.
  3. Leverage is rising quickly: long-term debt nearly doubled (+93%) to $133.0B in six months; total debt +80.5%; Q2 interest expense +155%. A further ~$64B of debt at ~4.5–5% implies $3–3.5B of incremental annual interest (~$0.25–0.30/share) versus the H1 run-rate.
  4. Earnings quality is poor at the headline: over half of H1 pre-tax income is a non-cash private-mark. That mark can reverse.
  5. Tax rate normalization: 23.0% vs 17.0% is a multi-billion-dollar headwind; cash taxes at 6.9% of core pre-tax are unsustainably low.
  6. Shareholder returns have paused: no meaningful buyback visible; capital is being redirected to capex, M&A ($39.8B in H1), and debt service.
  7. Returns are diluting: ROIC down from ~15% to ~12%.
7. Actionable Conclusions for Traders
  1. Re-base every earnings model. Use Q2 core EPS of ≈$2.15 and H1 core of ≈$4.09 — not $5.75/$8.53. On a ~$8.60 annualized core run-rate, fundamental momentum is ~+28%, not +242%. Consensus numbers based on GAAP will be noisy in both directions every quarter.
  2. Trade the operating inflection, not the GAAP print. The genuinely bullish signal is revenue acceleration + margin expansion simultaneously (16.6% → 19.6%; 13.1% → 13.7%). This is the metric to monitor in the Q3 print.
  3. Watch three specific numbers in Q3 (due late October 2026):
    • Capex: does it exceed ~$55–60B/quarter? Sequential deceleration would be a major positive for FCF sentiment.
    • D&A as % of revenue: currently 10.0% and rising ~90bp/yr. A break above ~11% without revenue acceleration is a margin warning.
    • Non-operating line: confirms whether the private-mark regime is one-off or recurring, and in which direction.
  4. FCF is the swing factor. A move back toward positive TTM FCF (requiring capex to flatten below ~$150B annualized) would likely re-rate the stock; continued acceleration above $200B annualized keeps the FCF bear case alive. Current TTM: −$11.6B.
  5. Monitor the debt trajectory. $64B of new long-term debt in six months is a step-change. Watch for further issuance, credit-rating commentary, and interest expense (Q2 annualized ~$5.3B vs $2.1B a year earlier).
  6. Position sizing around event risk: the report is based on June 30 data; Q3 results in roughly four to five weeks will reset the narrative. The information edge here is the core-EPS adjustment and the capex/FCF math, not stale headline numbers.
  7. Do not rely on insider signals — no authorized insider data exists for AMZN in this dataset. Any claim of insider buying/selling would be fabricated.

Net fundamental stance: Constructive on the operating business, cautious on the equity's near-term earnings optics and cash-flow profile. The operating momentum (+19.6% revenue, +43% operating income, 13.7% margin) is the best in years and argues for upside; the negative FCF, doubling debt, accelerating depreciation, and a headline EPS number that is 62% non-cash argue for treating reported results with heavy skepticism.
