It is to keep the staged-build idea but shrink the aggressive sizing, tighten the conditioning, and avoid the conservative mistake of turning a normal post-gap shakeout into a permanent sideline.

The aggressive analyst is right that the Q2-26 operating evidence is not noise. AMZN did $200.6B of revenue, up 19.6%; operating income of $27.46B, up 43.2%; operating margin of 13.69%, up 226bp; AWS up 37%; and the 129.05M-share session was real participation. I am not going to pretend that is a one-day headline. But the aggressive analyst keeps saying “the catalyst is confirmed” as if that confirms the entry. It does not. AMZN closed at 271.58, only 1.24% below the 274.99 range-top close and 2.51% below the 278.56 intraday high. The first tranche has roughly 1.2% to 2.5% of clear upside into supply and a 9.24% gap to the 246.50 stop. The staged plan improves that, but a 40% starter still puts a lot of capital at the worst point in the range. The aggressive analyst also wants to lean toward 125% of a standard AMZN allocation, which is about 6.5% to 7.5% of portfolio if standard is 5% to 6%. That directly conflicts with the trader’s own instruction to halve size versus the pre-gap ATR regime. ATR is 9.51, or 3.5% of price. You cannot say “halve normal size” and then target 125% of standard without explaining what standard means. I would not.

The aggressive analyst’s “no data is not a negative signal” is also too loose for a risk role. No sentiment report, no insider data, and an unverified AOCI composition are not reasons to sell AMZN, but they are reasons to size at or below standard, not above it. The $50.49B private-equity mark-up in Q2, core EPS near $2.16 versus $5.75 reported, negative TTM free cash flow of about $11.6B, debt up 93% to $132.2B, and $650B of contractual obligations do not kill the operating thesis. But they do mean the market can reprice AMZN sharply if AWS growth or margin commentary slips. The aggressive case treats those as accounting noise; I treat them as volatility inputs. That matters for sizing.

The conservative analyst is right about asymmetry, gap risk, and stop slippage. A close below 262.01 would invalidate the one-session gap-hold and open the 239.82-to-262.01 air pocket. The 246.50 stop sits near the 10 EMA at 242.70, the 50 SMA at 246.63, the VWMA at 245.72, and the mid-band cluster, so if that zone breaks, four supports break at once and the fill could be worse than 246.50. That is exactly why I would not size AMZN at the aggressive 125% target. But the conservative proposal has its own flaw: making 262.01 the hard stop for tranche one is too tight in a 3.5%-ATR regime. One ATR from 271.58 is about 262.07. That is noise-level, not structural. And the conservative plan then wants to buy 258–264 for tranche two, which is partly below the 262.01 “hard stop.” That is internally inconsistent. If 262.01 is a hard stop, you cannot also have a plan to add at 258–264 unless you first require a reclaim of 262.01 or a new base. I would rather use 262.01 as a de-risk trigger and 246.50 as the core structural stop, with a rule that you do not add below 262.01 until AMZN reclaims it.

The conservative analyst is also too back-loaded. A 20% starter with 40% waiting for 258–264 assumes the pullback comes. If AMZN closes above 278.56 on 60M shares and MACD crosses zero, the conservative plan will be underinvested in the exact scenario the trader is trying to capture. The conservative analyst says missing upside is acceptable. Fine, but the goal is not to optimize for missing upside; it is to optimize for reliable risk-adjusted participation. A 30% starter is a better compromise than 20% or 40%.

So here is my moderate adjustment. Keep the BUY on AMZN, but target 100% to at most 110% of a standard AMZN allocation, not 125%. If standard is 5% to 6% of portfolio, that means roughly 5% to 6.5%, and I would stay at the low end if the caller already owns MSFT, GOOGL, NVDA, or other AI-cloud/capex exposure. Portfolio context is unknown, so express this as a percentage of the intended AMZN position, not as an assumption about the current book. If already long AMZN, add only toward standard; do not add to an overweight. If starting from zero, take a 30% starter at or below 271.58, not 40%.

Then use roughly 30/35/25 with 10% held in reserve. Tranche one is 30% at or below 271.58. Tranche two is 35% into 258–264, but only if 246.50 holds and only after stabilization, meaning a close back above 262.01 or declining-volume retest, not averaging down into the air pocket. Tranche three is 25% only on a close above 278.56 with at least 60M shares and the MACD line above zero. Keep 10% for a post-breakout retest or a new base. Use 262.01 as a de-risk trigger: a close below it cuts tranche one and pauses tranche two. Use 246.50 as the hard structural stop for the core. Size the whole position assuming the stop could slip toward 240–242 in a fast tape, not just 246.50. At a 5% to 6.5% portfolio target, a 12% adverse move to the low 240s is about 0.6% to 0.78% of portfolio, which is close enough to the trader’s 0.5% to 0.75% risk budget; at 7.5%, it breaches it.

That is the balance. The aggressive analyst is right that AMZN’s operating inflection is real and that being underexposed to a genuine re-rating has a cost. The conservative analyst is right that the entry is at range-top resistance, the gap is real, the stop can slip, and the balance sheet carries more risk than the headline EPS suggests. The trader’s staged framework already bridges those views. My adjustment is to make the first tranche smaller, the target allocation smaller, the 262.01 level a real de-risk trigger rather than a casual trim, and the 278.56 breakout the only place where AMZN earns the final tranche. That keeps the BUY on AMZN, but it makes the plan sustainable rather than either a chase or a permanente wait.
