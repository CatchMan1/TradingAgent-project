### 2. The fundamentals are good, but the bull is extrapolating a cyclical upcycle

Yes, AAPL grew revenue **~16%** in Q2 and H1. For a company this size, that is impressive. But the bull’s mistake is assuming that automatically means the stock is attractive at the current price. We do not have valuation/consensus multiple data in this packet. That cuts against the bull, not the bear. Without valuation, you cannot claim a margin of safety. A great company at a full price is still a risky investment.

There are also real fundamental headwinds the bull glosses over:

- **Memory costs are a forward margin risk.** UBS reportedly raised its Q3 DDR contract-pricing forecast to **+32% QoQ** from +17%. The bull points to AAPL’s **49.3%** Q2 gross margin, but that is backward-looking—the quarter ended March 28. Rising memory costs hit future COGS with a lag. A 49.3% gross margin may be closer to a peak than a floor.
- **R&D is ramping hard at +32.6% YoY** to **$22.3B** in H1. That is the right strategic move for AI and custom silicon, but it is also an operating-margin headwind. The bull assumes the payoff is certain; the evidence is not there yet.
- **The effective tax rate rose to ~17.5% from 15.0%**, a direct EPS drag.
- **Buybacks slowed sharply**: H1 FY2026 buybacks were **$37.0B** versus **$49.5B** in H1 FY2025. Since buybacks have been a major EPS tailwind, a slower pace reduces the per-share compounding the bull is relying on.
- **The $12.8B increase in gross intangibles is unexplained** in the provided data. The bull calls it possible growth capital. It could also be M&A integration risk, content deals, or a lower-return use of cash. Until verified, it is an uncertainty—not a bullish data point.
- The balance sheet is strong, but “fortress” is overstated on liquidity: the **current ratio is only ~1.07**, with **$134.6B** of current liabilities against **$144.1B** of current assets. Net cash of **~$61.9B** is real, but it does not eliminate the risk that growth decelerates and margins compress.

The bull says: “Explain why a company growing 16% with 49% gross margins should be sold.” My response: I’m not arguing AAPL is a bad business. I’m arguing the market already knows AAPL is a good business, and the stock’s sharp bounce into resistance leaves little room for disappointment. If growth normalizes or memory costs bite, the multiple and the price will do the adjusting.

### 4. Competitive weaknesses are still real

The bull says AAPL is not “just hardware” because of the ecosystem. Fair. But the ecosystem does not immunize AAPL from mature-market saturation, China competition, or AI execution risk.

AAPL remains heavily exposed to iPhone and geopolitics. The AI/Siri relaunch is still unproven. Custom silicon helps, but AAPL is not the leader in AI compute—it is trying to integrate AI into a hardware ecosystem while competitors move faster at the model and data-center layer. A $2,500 foldable is a niche product, not a mass-volume growth engine. And China local-supply substitution is a structural headwind to both demand and supply positioning, even if India diversification helps over time. Diversification costs money and takes years; it is not a free option.
