### 3. The fundamentals are good, but the quality is overstated

This is where I think the bull case is most vulnerable.

Yes, **9M FY2026 revenue grew 16.1%** and **Q3 revenue grew 16.4%**. That’s real. But the margin story is doing almost all the work. Q3 operating expenses grew **22.9%**, faster than revenue. R&D rose **32.3%**. Operating margin expanded **263 bps** only because gross margin expanded **357 bps** to **50.1%**. That is not operating leverage. That is gross-margin leverage.

You call the **72% incremental gross margin** in Q3 a sign of pricing power. I call it a sign of mix, pricing, and cost tailwinds that are unlikely to be permanent. If gross margin normalizes toward the high-40s, EPS growth compresses quickly because opex is already growing faster than revenue. The bull case says “watch Q4.” I say: at roughly **38x TTM EPS** and a **~2.8% FCF yield** on my derived market cap, you don’t get paid to wait for a binary margin print.

The cash flow is also less pristine than you suggest. **9M operating cash flow grew 43.1%**, but that was helped by a **$10.8B decline in cash taxes paid**. FY2025 was a heavy cash-tax year. The underlying FCF growth is still strong — $110.2B in nine months is excellent — but the growth rate is partly timing-driven. Don’t extrapolate it.

And the capital-return support is slowing: total capital returns fell **10.1%** year over year, buybacks fell **12.0%**, and net cash of **$62.2B** is only about **1.3%** of the approximate $4.87T market cap. It’s a fortress balance sheet, but it’s not a valuation floor.

The inventory build is the one you’re hand-waving. Total inventory is up **94%**; raw materials are up **260%**. You say it’s a pre-build for a product ramp. Maybe. But that’s an assumption. Finished goods are down 4%, which is good today, but the absolute build is large. If the product cycle slips or demand disappoints, write-down risk is real. A bull case shouldn’t require the best-case interpretation of a 94% inventory increase.

There are also two items you didn’t address: **net intangibles rose 91% to $25.4B** with no explanation of counterparties or terms, and **deferred revenue grew only 8.8%** versus revenue growth of 16.1%. The services/backlog flywheel is not accelerating faster than the total business. Add in the effective tax rate rising to **17.6%** from **15.4%**, and the EPS tailwind is less durable than the headline suggests.
