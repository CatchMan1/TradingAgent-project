## 1. "Accelerating fundamentals" — the acceleration is borrowed, and part of the growth is unexplained

The bull waves 52.5% FCF growth and 16.2% revenue growth like a flag. But the fundamentals report — the bull's own source — states plainly that roughly **$28B of the $35B operating-cash-flow increase came from working-capital normalization**, reversing prior-year distortions. FCF growth is **not repeatable**; normalized FCF tracks net income, closer to 20%. The bull is extrapolating a one-time cash swing into a valuation case.

Worse, the bull calls it "base business growth" and never accounts for the **$13.27B increase in gross intangibles** that appeared alongside a **$13.53B rise in non-current liabilities** in the same nine months. That near-perfect offset is the signature of an acquisition or large IP/licensing transaction — and the report explicitly says it is *not itemized*. So when the bull says "the base business is growing 16% before any Duo revenue," I ask: how much of that 16% is organic, and how much is a purchased asset the bull hasn't even mentioned? Neither of us can answer — the data doesn't. That's an unquantified risk being sold as clean growth.

## 2. "Margins are expanding" — at the operating line, the engine is sputtering

The bull points to 50.06% Q3 gross margin. Fine. Now look *below* the gross line: **R&D jumped 32.3% to 10.72% of revenue**, up from 9.43%. The trend across periods is **8.19% → 9.34% → 10.72%** — an accelerating staircase, not a blip. The bull's defense is "if R&D intensity stabilizes near 10%." That's an assumption, and the data shows the opposite. And operating margin actually **fell sequentially from 34.02% in H1 to 32.62% in Q3**. The "simultaneous expansion" the bull celebrates is a gross-margin story being eaten at the operating line.

Then layer on the tax line: effective rate **15.39% → 17.58%**, a **~$2.7B** hit that isn't going away. And the bull's beloved EPS tailwind — buybacks — **decelerated 12% to $62.1B**. The bull spins that as "higher quality." It's also arithmetic: slower buybacks means slower EPS growth for the same net income. The EPS engine is losing cylinders right as the bull asks you to pay up.
