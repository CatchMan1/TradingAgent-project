
Bull Analyst: Let me open the debate by being clear about what I’m arguing and what I’m not arguing. I’m not saying the 2026-09-04 candle in AAPL was pretty — it wasn’t. A failed poke above the $328 upper Bollinger band and a close near the session low is a warning. But the bear case cannot be “one rejection candle means the trend is over,” because the weight of evidence still says the opposite: AAPL is in a confirmed intermediate uptrend, the fundamental engine is accelerating, and the stock just gave bulls a defined entry instead of forcing them to chase a broken breakout.

**The fundamental case is the core of this. AAPL is not growing in line with a mature hardware company.** Through nine months of FY2026, revenue grew 16.15% to $364.4B, gross profit rose 21.7%, operating income rose 21.7%, net income rose 20.0%, and diluted EPS rose 22.4% to $6.88. In the June quarter alone, revenue grew 16.4%, gross margin hit a record 50.05%, and operating income grew 26.6%. The incremental operating margin was 48.7% — roughly half of every additional revenue dollar fell through to operating profit. That is elite operating leverage, and it is happening while R&D is up 32.5%. A company that can invest that aggressively and still expand margins is not a company losing pricing power.

**The competitive advantages show up in the numbers.** AAPL’s ecosystem is delivering pricing and mix strength: COGS grew only 8.6% against 16.4% revenue growth in the June quarter. That is the signature of a business with strong brand, product differentiation, and services mix — not a commodity hardware vendor. The cash conversion cycle is roughly negative 63 days, meaning AAPL is funded by its supply chain rather than its own capital. Capex is only 1.87% of revenue, so the model is capital-light. Free cash flow was $110.2B in nine months, up 52.5%, with FCF/net income at 108.6%. And net cash nearly doubled to roughly $62.2B, with zero new long-term debt issued. That is a fortress balance sheet with optionality for larger capital returns, not a stretched one.

**On growth opportunities:** I don’t need to underwrite a speculative foldable iPhone or an AI miracle to make the bull case. The existing run-rate already annualizes the nine-month revenue base to roughly $486B. The foldable cycle and a reimagined Siri are call options on top of that. The news review itself describes the foldable cycle as a positive product narrative and the AI commentary as sentiment support — not core fundamentals. So the bear can call those speculative, and I’ll agree; they are not why I own AAPL. The core case is 16% revenue growth, 50% gross margin, 48% incremental operating margins, and $110B of free cash flow.

**Now the technicals.** The bear will point to the $328–$333 rejection and say the breakout failed. Fair. But that is a short-term supply shelf, not a broken primary trend. AAPL is still 12.7% above a rising 200-day SMA at $283.87. The 50-day SMA flattened through August and has now turned up to $315.15. The 10 EMA crossed back above the 50 SMA on 2026-08-19, and price closed above the 50 SMA on 2026-08-26 — both signals have held. MACD crossed above its signal on 2026-08-26 and above zero on 2026-08-31. The VWMA has risen from $307.76 on 2026-08-19 to $316.37, meaning the volume-weighted buyer during the base is now in profit. That is accumulation, not distribution.

The 2026-09-04 session was a rejection at the $328 upper band and the underside of the July gap, but look at the volume: the down-day was 39.6M shares versus 53.2M on the 2026-09-01 up-day. That is not classic distribution. RSI reset from 63.38 to 53.89 and never reached 70 on the advance, so there is no overbought extreme being unwound. MACD is still positive at +2.59 with a signal at +1.03; the histogram decelerated from +2.028 to +1.558, but that is a first down-tick after eight sessions, not a bearish cross. The uptrend is intact; the breakout attempt failed. Those are different things.

**So let me engage the bear’s likely arguments directly.**

*“The breakout failed at $328–$333.”* True — and that is exactly why the bull plan is not to chase $320+. The better entry is the $315–$317.50 confluence: the 50 SMA at $315.15, the VWMA at $316.37, and the 2026-08-28 low at $315.45. From $316 with a stop below $307.01, the risk is about $9; to the $333.43–$334.75 gap shelf, the reward is about $18. That is roughly 2:1. A failed breakout into support is often a better risk/reward than a successful breakout chased at the top.

*“MACD is decelerating.”* It is decelerating, not deteriorating. The MACD line is still above zero, still above its signal, and the histogram is still positive. A bearish cross would require several more sessions of weakness. The data say late-phase impulse, not trend reversal.

*“The UK £2bn lawsuit and BASF patent suit are negative.”* They are headline risks, not cash-flow events. The UK claim is an allegation at the Competition Appeal Tribunal, not a judgment; the claim size is not expected liability. The BASF suit summary provides no patent numbers, venue, damages, or relief. AAPL has $62.2B of net cash and $110B of nine-month free cash flow. These are monitorables, not thesis-breakers. If the bear wants to argue material liability, they need a ruling, a provision, or a quantified request — none of which is in the evidence.

*“Jefferies cut its target on a setback.”* The evidence gives no new target, no rating, no numbers, and no named strategy. Evercore ISI reportedly reiterated Outperform on 2026-09-02. I am not going to trade an opaque headline against a 50.05% gross margin and 22.4% EPS growth.

*“App Store weakness and foldable uncertainty.”* Those are real watch items, but they are not showing up in the margin line yet. Q3 gross margin hit a record. If services were collapsing, I would expect mix and margin pressure — not a 357bp year-over-year expansion. The foldable cycle is optionality, not the base case.

*“Gross margin at 50% is a peak.”* This is the bear’s strongest fundamental point. But “peak” is not “collapse.” The sequential march from roughly 48% to 49% to 50.05% was driven by revenue growing twice as fast as COGS, and even a normalization to 49.5% would leave AAPL with exceptional operating margins. The checkpoint is Q4 FY2026: gross margin guidance at or above 49.5% keeps the thesis intact. A guide below 49% would be a genuine warning. We are not there.

*“The tax rate is a headwind.”* Correct — 17.58% versus 15.39% in the nine-month period is a 219bp drag, and it cost real EPS. But AAPL still grew EPS 22.4% with that headwind already in the numbers. If the tax rate stabilizes, that becomes a tailwind to forward comparisons, not a reason to sell.

*“Inventory is up 94%.”* The detail matters: raw materials rose 260%, while finished goods fell 4.1%. That looks like component pre-positioning or a product-ramp build, not channel stuffing. The Q4 balance sheet is the checkpoint. If inventory keeps climbing while revenue growth decelerates below 12%, I’ll take the bear seriously. Until then, the cash flow statement — $110B of FCF — says this is not a working-capital crisis.

*“Buybacks are doing the work.”* No. Net income grew 20.0% and EPS grew 22.4%; only about 2.4 percentage points came from share count reduction. The other ~20 points were operational. Buybacks are optionality, not the thesis. And with an 11.6% dividend payout ratio and $62.2B of net cash, AAPL has room to increase capital returns — that is upside, not dependency.
