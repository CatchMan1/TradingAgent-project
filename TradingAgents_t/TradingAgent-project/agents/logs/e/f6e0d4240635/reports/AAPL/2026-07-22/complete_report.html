Bull Analyst: ## Opening Statement: AAPL — The Market Is Underwriting a Slowdown That the Filings Say Isn't Happening

Since the bear hasn't taken the floor yet, let me frame exactly where I think this debate is going to be fought — and why the bear argument, in whatever form it arrives, is going to be built on a timing mismatch between a stale narrative and fresh data.

The bear's case, almost certainly, will hang on three planks: (1) momentum is visibly decelerating — MACD histogram down 52% in four sessions, RSI off 9 points from 71.65; (2) the stock is stretched at 18.4% above its 200-SMA after a +21.3% V-shaped rip in 15 sessions; and (3) the capital-return engine is sputtering — buybacks cut 25%, tax rate up 2.5 points, cash flow flattered by non-repeatable items. Add a dusting of Mag-7-in-a-rut sector malaise and an unresolved DMA overhang, and you've got a tidy bear note.

It's a good note. It's also looking at the wrong four sessions through the wrong lens. Let me take each plank apart.

Plank 1: "Momentum is rolling over." No — momentum is breathing, which is what strong uptrends do.

The bear will point at the MACD histogram falling from 3.337 on 2026-07-17 to 1.599 on 2026-07-22 and call it distribution. But look at what price actually did over that window: -2.35% off a record close. You cannot have institutional distribution on that little price damage. And crucially, look at the volume: 63.41M → 53.47M → 41.34M → 38.76M, three consecutive declines into the pullback. The -2.14% reversal day on 07-20 came on lower volume than the prior up-day's 63.41M. Real tops show rising volume on down days. This shows supply stepping away, not stepping up.

Then there's the tripwire that actually matters: the 10-EMA at $322.88. The 07-21 low was $322.22 against a 10-EMA of $322.21 — a one-cent hold. The 07-22 low was $323.34, also above it. Three consecutive closes above a rising 10-EMA (it's climbed 12 straight sessions from 289.34 to 322.88). That's not a broken trend; that's a textbook bull-flag consolidation. RSI cooling from 71.65 to 62.54 without price breaking down is the healthiest possible resolution of an overbought condition — relief through time, not price. The bear is reading a coiling spring as a snapped one.

And the volume-weighted confirmation the bear will skip entirely: VWMA jumped from 300.72 to 310.27 in a single session on 07-17 — a 3.2% one-day move in a volume-weighted average is only possible if enormous size traded at much higher prices. It's kept stepping up since (314.02 → 317.27 → 319.48). Price sits just 2.0% above it. Buyers own the tape.

Plank 2: "It's extended at 18.4% above the 200-SMA." That's what the start of a durable trend looks like — and the 200-SMA was tested and held.

Here's the detail the bear narrative needs to confront: the June 25 low close of 275.15 came within 0.4% of today's 200-SMA of 275.28 — and held. That's the long-term mean being stress-tested and defended. Since then, AAPL has put on 21.3% in 15 sessions, and the 50-SMA has risen monotonically from 282.21 (06-08) to 304.89, now 10.8% above the 200-SMA. The stack is 10-EMA (322.88) > 50-SMA (304.89) > 200-SMA (275.28), all three rising. There is no death-cross risk anywhere on the horizon.

Yes, the Bollinger upper band is expanding fast — 312.31 on 07-02 to 345.83 on 07-22, +10.7% in three weeks — while the lower band is roughly flat (277.29 → 274.30). That asymmetry is the signature of an upside volatility breakout, not a squeeze about to reverse. %B at 72% leaves the stock in the upper third of the envelope but comfortably below the upper band. This is not a blow-off; it's a stock with room.

Plank 3: "The capital-return engine is sputtering." Read the other half of that balance sheet.

This is where I think the bear is most vulnerable, because the buyback narrative is a half-quote. Buybacks fell 25.3% to $36.99B in H1 — true. But look at what Apple did with the other half of its FCF:

  • Net cash rebuilt to ~$61.9B, up +$28.2B in six months.
  • Gross debt cut 14% ($98.68B → $84.70B).
  • Total equity up 44% ($73.7B → $106.5B).
  • Total returns cut to 57% of FCF from 107% in FY2025.

That's not a company running out of capital-return capacity. That's a company choosing to de-lever and re-arm into an investment cycle, while still returning $44.7B and still shrinking the float by 105.6M shares. That's optionality, and optionality is worth more than a mechanical 2% annual EPS boost — especially when the buyback can re-accelerate the moment the R&D cycle starts printing revenue.

And the "flattered cash flow" charge? I'll concede it — and then normalize it. Strip out the $11.3B lower cash-tax payment, and H1 CFO growth is still ≈+27%, FCF growth ≈+33%. The bear is attacking the size of a surplus, not its existence. Meanwhile capex is 1.7% of revenue (down 27.7% YoY) while R&D is +32.6% — Apple is funding an AI/product build entirely through the P&L, which is precisely why FCF conversion is structurally elite.
