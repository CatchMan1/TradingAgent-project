## 3. Your "Margins Expanded Anyway" Argument Is Backward-Looking — And That's the Trap

You said: *"Operating margin expanded +100bps while R&D surged 33% — the AI spend is being absorbed."* That's true **for H1 FY2026.** But you're extrapolating a backward-looking fact into a forward-looking guarantee, and that's exactly where the setup breaks.

Look at the mechanics underneath:
- **R&D intensity rose from 7.66% to 8.75% of revenue.** That's a structural step-up, not a one-quarter blip.
- Revenue grew +16% — that's the *only* reason margin held. Apple is relying on **operating leverage from rapid growth** to absorb the spend.

Now ask the bear's question: what happens when that +16% growth normalizes — as it must against these comps? The R&D ramp doesn't stop; it's a multiyear AI build. Rising opex + decelerating revenue = **margin compression in FY2027.** You're celebrating the last quarter of operating leverage as if it's permanent. It isn't.

And you skipped a real headwind sitting right there in the numbers: **the effective tax rate jumped from ~15.5% to 17.5%.** That already dragged net income growth (+19.4%) below operating income growth (+21.3%). That's a persistent, structural EPS drag — not a footnote.
