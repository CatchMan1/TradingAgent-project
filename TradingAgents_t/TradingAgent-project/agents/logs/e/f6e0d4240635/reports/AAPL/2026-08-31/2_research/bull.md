## 5. The gap argument — this is the bear's best point, and it's backwards

There are **no trapped buyers** at 315, 320, or 330 from that session — nobody bought there, because the stock never printed there. What the gap actually represents is a **vacuum**, and vacuums get traversed quickly once the initial panic is absorbed.

## 6. The inventory build — the bear's "overbuild" call is contradicted by the mix

The bear will hammer inventory: **+94% to $11.1 billion, raw materials +260% to $7.6 billion, 61.6 days of COGS vs 34.0.**

Let's actually read the mix, because the mix is the tell:

- **Raw materials and purchased parts: +$5,521M (+260%)**
- **Finished goods: −$147M (down)**

A demand miss produces **finished goods** bloat — you build product, nobody buys it, it sits in the channel. An overbuild thesis is a *finished-goods* thesis. That is not what this is. This is **components bought ahead of a build.** You buy silicon, memory, and displays before you assemble. Raw-materials-heavy inventory with finished goods flat or down is the textbook pre-launch signature.

And it lines up with everything else in the news flow: CNBC reported Apple pushing **early Mac updates** and preparing **"biggest product launches in years"** ahead of the CEO handoff. You don't pre-buy $5.5 billion of raw materials for a demand collapse.

Now — am I saying there's zero risk? No. The honest discriminator is **FQ4 gross margin and revenue**. If GM holds ≥49% with double-digit revenue growth, the build was demand-driven and the setup is a coiled product cycle. If GM compresses >150 bps on rising inventory, the bear was right. I'll take that trade, because **gross margin has been expanding sequentially** (50.06% in FQ3 vs 49.07% 9M average), the demand data (IDC record share) supports the ramp, and the stock is being bought back by the company at roughly these levels.
