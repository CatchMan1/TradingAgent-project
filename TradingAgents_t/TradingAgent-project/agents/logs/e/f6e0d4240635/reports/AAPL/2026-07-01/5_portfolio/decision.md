**Rating**: Underweight

**Executive Summary**: Trim AAPL into the $294–$295 resistance zone; this is a reduction of a long, not a short and not an exit. Take net exposure toward roughly 50–70% of normal target weight (lower half of that band if currently at or above target), keep a residual core sized to survive a 6–8% drawdown toward $277–$270, and cap any new starter exposure at ~25% of intended allocation. Use $288.94 as a warning level, $282 as the tactical cut, and $270.33 (rising 200-SMA) as the long-term trend line forcing a full review. Re-risk only in stages after a daily close above $295 on above-average volume with MACD crossed above signal and RSI >55, confirmed by two to three sessions holding above that zone.

**Investment Thesis**: All three analysts converge on the same operative conclusion even though they frame it differently: AAPL should be de-risked into strength at resistance, and none of them supports shorting or abandoning the name. That convergence, not the disagreements, is what sets the rating.

The deciding evidence is asymmetric and short-dated. AAPL at $294.38 is pinned at the Bollinger mid-band ($294.88), just above the 50-SMA ($292.67), with the 10-EMA lagging beneath both at $290.25. Momentum has not confirmed: MACD is −2.26 versus a −1.01 signal (no cross), RSI is dead neutral at 50.97, and the bull case's own trigger — a decisive close above ~$295 on above-average volume with MACD crossing up and RSI >55 — has not fired. Upside to the $302 target is about $7.60 against an $8.20 ATR; we are being asked to risk more than a full average day's range for less than one day's range of reward. That is uncompensated exposure at full weight, and it is the single strongest point in the debate; the aggressive and neutral analysts both concede it.

Layered on top is a live, unquantified catalyst: management has called the memory-chip shortage "extreme," price increases are already passing through everything but iPhones, and the CXMT sourcing fix sits on a Pentagon blacklist requiring government clearance. The conservative analyst is right that this is a binary regulatory and margin risk we cannot underwrite at full size; the neutral analyst is right that it is not a clean known-directional edge — only 20 of 184 news records, all June 30, is a thin sample. Either way it argues for smaller tactical size, not for a full-weight hold through the chop.

The counterweight — and the reason this is Underweight rather than Sell — is that nothing in the evidence says the franchise is broken. Revenue +16.6%, gross margin 49.3%, EPS +21.8%, H1 free cash flow $78.3B, net cash ~$61.9B, and a rising 200-SMA at $270.33 (price also above the $288.94 VWMA, with the 6/26 reversal on 261.8M shares lending the bounce credibility). Those justify keeping a core. They are also trailing and known: a fortress balance sheet protects solvency, not the quarterly risk budget, and a 6% move to $277 or 8% to $270.33 is entirely ordinary at an $8.20 ATR. Secondary flags — a roughly $12.5–12.8B net increase in intangibles implying an unexplained acquisition, R&D up 33.6%, and an effective tax rate rising to ~17.5% from ~15% — further reduce confidence in adding at resistance.

On process, the conservative and neutral analysts are correct that the listed $302.50 "stop" is above the $294.38 entry and is therefore a short-sale stop, not a protective stop on a long trim. I reject any short of AAPL here: the primary uptrend is intact, and a $302.50 stop is barely one ATR away on a name with this volatility. Where the aggressive analyst overreaches is calling the re-entry above $295 "defined and cheap" and advocating an aggressive reload; the conservative and neutral analysts are right that in wide Bollinger bands a single breakout print can whipsaw. Hence staged re-entry, not a binary snap-back.

What would change the call: a daily close above $295 on above-average volume with MACD crossing back above its signal and RSI above 55, followed by two to three sessions holding that zone with volatility contracting — that would justify rebuilding toward target weight. A stabilized retest of $281–$283 or $277 could justify a very small starter, but only on actual stabilization, never by averaging down into a falling tape. Missing evidence that would sharpen this call: forward guidance, a quantified memory-cost impact, a CXMT approval probability, acquisition details, and valuation multiples.
