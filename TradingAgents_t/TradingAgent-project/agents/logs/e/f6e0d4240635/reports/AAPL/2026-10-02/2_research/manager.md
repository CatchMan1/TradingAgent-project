**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: This was a genuinely close debate, and I want to be clear about what decided it and what didn't.

The bull won on the evidence that actually compounds. AAPL's fundamental engine is accelerating, not stalling: 9M FY2026 revenue +16.1% to $364.4B, Q3 revenue +16.4%, 9M diluted EPS +22.4%, Q3 EPS +28.7%, Q3 gross margin 50.1% (+357 bps YoY), 9M operating margin expanding 263 bps on opex growth of 22.9%. 9M FCF of $110.2B, TTM FCF of $136.7B, net cash of $62.2B after returning $73.9B in nine months. That is a franchise still generating cash faster than it distributes it. The bear conceded all of this — "AAPL is a phenomenal company" — and then argued the tape. That concession matters: the bear's case is a timing and valuation case, not a thesis-failure case.

On the trend, the bull is simply correct and the bear did not refute it. 200-SMA 289.159 rising, 50-SMA 322.416 rising, price 15.4% above the 200-SMA and 3.5% above the 50-SMA, 50/200 spread widening at +11.5%. The bear's own invalidation language ("still a rising 50/200-SMA structure," "the 50-SMA at 322.42 is a likely bounce zone," "size small on any tactical short") concedes the primary trend is up. The failed breakdown at 325.81 and the repair back to 333.69 through the 7/30 gap-fill line at 333.43 is a range, not a distribution top. MACD at 3.807 is still above zero and RSI at 54.72 is neutral, not deteriorating — bearish crossover inside a rising primary trend is a cooling phase, not a reversal signal.

But the bear won the entry argument, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. Price is 0.48% below the VWMA at 335.30, which is overhead supply; AAPL has closed below the 10-EMA four straight sessions; highs have stepped down 345.34 → 342.99 → 339.50 → 334.54; and the 10/1–10/2 reversal came on 36.31M and 33.28M shares against a ~42.2M average — roughly 21% below average on the gap day. The bull's own plan requires a volume reclaim of 335.30 to confirm, so buying in size *before* that confirmation is internally inconsistent. The bear's risk/reward math also landed: at $333.69, ~1.2:1 to the first target at 339.50 with a 325.80 stop that is only ~1.1 ATR away, and a 7/31 precedent of an −8.58% overnight gap. That is a real argument for staging, not for chasing.

Where the bear overreached: the inventory build (+94% total, +260% raw materials, finished goods −4.1%, ~17 inventory days) is genuinely more consistent with a product-cycle pre-build than with demand failure — the bear's read requires the worst-case interpretation as much as the bull's requires the best. The "only hard news is a price-target cut" point is weak: no old target, new target, analyst, or rating was disclosed, and the "$355" reference contradicts the "cut" framing. The uncorroborated "new CEO memo" headline is not tradeable evidence for either side. And the bear never produced a confirmed negative catalyst — the AI question, regulatory risk, and margin normalization are risks, not events.

So: the bull wins the medium-term direction, the bear wins the near-term entry. When direction is up and the entry is mediocre, the right answer is to build exposure gradually rather than either chase or stand aside. That is Overweight, not Buy (the bear's timing critique is too good to ignore and the confirmation is missing) and not Hold (the bear offered no evidence that would justify sitting flat in a rising primary trend with accelerating fundamentals, and a "wait and see" is itself a call the bear couldn't fully support).

Caveats I want on the record: I'm working only from the supplied debate. There is no peer-group valuation comparison to test the ~38x TTM EPS / ~2.8% FCF yield claim, no forward guidance, no segment revenue split, no explanation of the $25.4B net intangibles (+91%), and no breadth in the news window. The deferred-revenue-growth-lagging-revenue point (8.8% vs 16.1%) and the tax rate rising to 17.6% are legitimate quality-of-earnings questions the bull did not answer, and they belong in the monitoring list below.
