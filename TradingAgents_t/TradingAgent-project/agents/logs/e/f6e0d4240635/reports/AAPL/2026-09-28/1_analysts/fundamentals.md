## 2. Company Profile

AAPL designs, manufactures, and markets smartphones (iPhone), personal computers (Mac), tablets (iPad), wearables/home/accessories (Watch, AirPods, Vision, Home), and a broad Services portfolio (App Store, iCloud, advertising, AppleCare, payments, subscriptions, licensing). The company sells through retail stores, online, third-party carriers, resellers, and distributors, and reports geographically (Americas, Europe, Greater China, Japan, Rest of Asia Pacific).

The fundamental picture in this dataset is of a **hardware-led revenue acceleration combined with a step-change in gross margin and an aggressive R&D build** — a mix that historically occurs during a strong product refresh cycle with favorable component pricing and Services mix.

## 5. Margin Analysis — The Core Fundamental Story

| Margin | 9M FY2026 | 9M FY2025 | Δ | Q3 FY2026 | Q3 FY2025 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross | **49.07%** | 46.82% | **+225 bps** | **50.06%** | 46.49% | **+357 bps** |
| R&D % revenue | 9.34% | 8.19% | +115 bps | 10.72% | 9.43% | +129 bps |
| SG&A % revenue | 6.12% | 6.55% | −43 bps | 6.71% | 7.07% | −36 bps |
| Operating | **33.60%** | 32.08% | +152 bps | 32.62% | 29.99% | +263 bps |
| Pre-tax | 33.79% | 31.85% | +194 bps | 33.15% | 29.81% | +334 bps |
| Net | **27.85%** | 26.95% | +90 bps | 27.22% | 24.92% | +230 bps |
| Effective tax | 17.58% | 15.39% | +219 bps | 17.86% | 16.40% | +146 bps |

**Three conclusions:**

1. **Gross margin is the engine.** A 225 bps nine-month expansion and a 357 bps Q3 expansion is a structural, not seasonal, move. It is large enough to absorb a 115 bps R&D increase *and* a 219 bps tax-rate increase while still delivering net margin expansion.
2. **The tax rate is the largest offset.** Had the 9M effective rate stayed at 15.39%, tax would have been ~$18.9B instead of $21.6B — about **$2.7B, or $0.18/share**, of additional earnings. That is ~2.7 points of EPS growth given back. If the higher rate is the new run-rate, it is a persistent headwind, not a one-quarter issue.
3. **Operating leverage is coming from SG&A, not R&D.** SG&A fell 43 bps as a share of revenue; R&D rose 115 bps. Management is funding a major R&D ramp (likely AI/silicon/product development) out of gross-margin gains and overhead discipline. That is a high-quality way to fund investment — but it means **the margin story now depends on gross margin holding near 49–50%**.

**Durability question:** a 50.06% quarterly gross margin is at the high end of AAPL's historical range. The key risk to the entire thesis is gross-margin mean reversion (component costs, tariffs/trade policy, FX, or less favorable product mix). A move back to 47% would erase most of the operating-margin gains.

A 28% cut is not obviously a permanent new level for a company building AI infrastructure and custom silicon. If capex normalizes to $12B, FY2026 FCF would be ~$3B lower on a full-year basis.

| **Payout as % of FCF** | **72.9%** | 121.6% | 113.5% | 106.3% | 98.4% |

- Buybacks fell **12.0% YoY** to $62.1B despite FCF rising 52.5%.
- Payout dropped from **>100% of FCF** in each of the last three fiscal years to **72.9%** in 9M FY2026.
- Net cash rose **$28.4B** in nine months.
- No new long-term debt was issued; $14.0B of debt was repaid.

This is a **deliberate shift from "return everything" to "retain and reinvest."** For a shareholder-return thesis, it is a negative. For a strategic/optionality thesis, it is a positive — but only if the retained capital is deployed productively (the intangible build is the leading candidate).

**Dividend:** $0.27/quarter declared in Q3 FY2026 (vs $0.26), a 3.8% raise; 9M declared $0.79 vs $0.76. Annualized run-rate ~$1.08, a payout of only ~12% of TTM EPS. The dividend is immaterial to total return; **buyback pace is the variable that matters.**

## 15. Final Assessment

AAPL's reported fundamentals as of 2026-09-28 are **the strongest in the three-year dataset**: 16% revenue growth, a 50% Q3 gross margin, 33.6% operating margin, 20%+ EPS and net-income growth, $110B of nine-month FCF, and a $62B net-cash position with debt/equity down to 0.78x. The acceleration is internally consistent — gross profit, pre-tax income, and EPS growth all rose in each successive quarter of FY2026.

The two things a trader must underwrite are **sustainability** and **capital allocation**. Sustainability because the 50% gross margin and the FCF surge both contain elements that may not repeat (peak pricing/mix; a $14.6B working-capital release; a 28% capex cut). Capital allocation because the company has visibly stopped returning all of its cash — buybacks down 12%, payout down to 73% of FCF, net cash up $28B — while adding $13.3B of unexplained intangibles against $13.5B of new noncurrent liabilities.

**The correct posture is constructive but conditional.** The operating momentum supports exposure; the margin, tax, and cash-conversion caveats argue for position sizing that respects a potentially peaking margin and an unverified Q4. The FY2026 Q4/FY2026 10-K in late October is the decisive event: it will either validate the 20%+ growth and 49–50% gross margin as a new baseline, or reveal FY2026 as a high-water mark.

**Base case from the data:** FY2026 EPS of approximately **$9.08** (+21.7%), $134B net income, and $161B operating income — *if* Q4 delivers ~$2.20 EPS and ~$58B gross profit. That is the number to trade against, and it is a derived bar, not company guidance.
