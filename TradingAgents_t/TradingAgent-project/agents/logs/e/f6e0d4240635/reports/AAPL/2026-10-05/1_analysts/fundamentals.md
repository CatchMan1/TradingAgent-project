### 6.3 Capital allocation scorecard

- **Total returned 9M FY26:** $73.9B (buybacks + dividends) = **20.3% of revenue**, **72.8% of net income**, **67.0% of FCF**.
- **Buyback intensity falling:** $62.1B vs $70.6B (−12%) despite OCF up 43%. Management is retaining more cash — consistent with the $62B net cash build and possible M&A.
- **Dividend:** $0.79/share declared over 9M FY26 vs $0.76 (+3.9%); quarterly $0.27 vs $0.26. Annualized ≈ **$1.08/share**. Payout only ~12% of earnings — ample room, but Apple clearly prioritizes buybacks.
- **Implied repurchase price (derived, approximate):** $62.1B ÷ 164.3M net shares retired ≈ **$378/share**, vs ~$280 implied in 9M FY25. Caveat: net share reduction understates gross repurchases because of RSU issuance, so the true average is somewhat lower — but the **direction (sharply higher prices paid per share retired, ~30%+) is a useful signal that the equity has re‑rated**. Buybacks are therefore less EPS‑accretive per dollar than a year ago.

## 8. Red Flags & Watch Items (ranked by importance)

1. **Inventory +94% YoY / raw materials +260%.** Still only 12.4 days of COGS, so not yet a demand signal — but it is a large absolute build ($11.1B vs $5.7B). This is consistent with new‑product ramps and/or memory‑component pre‑buying amid rising prices. Negative read‑through: if holiday demand disappoints, write‑down risk hits Q1/Q2 FY27 gross margin.
2. **Opex growth > revenue growth.** Total opex +21.9% vs revenue +16.2%; R&D +32.5%. If revenue growth decelerates toward single digits in FY27 while R&D keeps compounding at 20–30%, operating margin expansion reverses.
3. **Tax rate normalization.** ETR 17.6% vs 15.4%. Each 100 bps of ETR is roughly $0.09–0.10 of EPS at current pre‑tax run‑rate. A further step‑up (OECD Pillar Two, US tax changes, state aid follow‑ons) is a direct EPS headwind.
4. **OCF growth is flattered.** $10.8B of the YoY increase is cash tax timing. Underlying ~+30%.
5. **Tough FY2027 comps.** FY26 revenue is tracking ~+16% vs a 4% two‑year CAGR. Growth mean‑reversion is the single biggest risk to consensus.
6. **Intangibles +$13.3B gross with no accompanying disclosure in this extract.** Watch the FY26 10‑K for acquisitions/IP deals and any goodwill step‑up.
7. **Buybacks less accretive.** ~$378 implied average repurchase price vs ~$280 a year ago; share count only −1.1% YTD. EPS growth (+22.4%) is now driven more by operations than by share shrinkage (9M FY25 EPS growth was helped more by buybacks).
8. **Thin current ratio (~1.00x).** Normal for Apple, but leaves little cushion if working‑capital swings are large.
9. **Information gap: no insider transaction data** and no segment/product revenue in this dataset.

### 9.2 The Q4/FY26 print (expected late October 2026) — the only near‑term catalyst

Pre‑earnings checklist, in order of price sensitivity:

| # | What to verify | Bull threshold | Bear threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **December‑quarter revenue guide** | ≥ +10% YoY (i.e., holiday growth holds double digits) | < +6% (deceleration signal) |
| 2 | **Gross margin guide** | ≥ 49% | ≤ 47.5% (memory costs/mix) |
| 3 | **Opex growth** | ≤ 15% YoY | ≥ 20% YoY (R&D outrunning revenue) |
| 4 | **Inventory & component commentary** | Build described as launch‑driven, turns stable | Write‑downs, channel inventory comments |
| 5 | **ETR guidance** | ≤ 17.5% | ≥ 18.5% |
| 6 | **Buyback authorization / net cash deployment** | New/increased authorization, M&A commentary | Slowdown without deployment plan |
| 7 | **Services growth & margin** | Sustained double‑digit, GM accretive | Deceleration, regulatory drag |

### 9.3 Trade framing

- **Base case (fundamentally constructive):** 50% gross margin and $110B 9‑month FCF support the thesis that FY26 EPS lands at or above $9.15. Any pullback toward the high‑20s forward multiple is a buying opportunity into a business with a $62B net cash cushion and ~$147B of liquidity.
- **Risk case:** the stock has likely re‑rated alongside a +16% growth year; a Q4 guide below +8% revenue growth would compress both the multiple and FY27 estimates simultaneously (double whammy), since opex is already committed at +22%.
- **Hedge idea for holders:** the key asymmetric risk is gross margin, not revenue — memory/component cost inflation hits the 50% GM line directly. Watch memory spot pricing as the leading indicator between now and the print.
- **Avoid:** extrapolating the 43% OCF growth or the 357 bps gross‑margin expansion into FY27. Both are partly one‑off (cash taxes; tariff‑affected base).
- **Income angle:** dividend is small (≈$1.08/yr annualized, ~12% payout) and grew only ~4%; this is not an income vehicle — capital return is buyback‑led and that buyback is currently less share‑accretive than a year ago.

## 10. Limitations of This Dataset

- **No insider transactions:** the vendor explicitly returned `DATA_UNAVAILABLE` — no authorized point‑in‑time insider archive exists for AAPL. No insider buying/selling conclusion can be drawn, and none was fabricated.
- **No segment/product revenue or units:** the extract contained consolidated tags only; iPhone/Mac/iPad/Wearables/Services and geographic splits could not be verified.
- **No price, market cap, consensus, or estimate data:** valuation work is therefore sensitivity‑based, not a point estimate.
- **Annual pulls returned the same XBRL fact set as quarterly pulls** (no separate annual aggregation), so FY2023–FY2025 P&L figures are drawn from 10‑K filings and cross‑checked against the 9M FY25 XBRL values, which match exactly.
- **Sign convention on working‑capital lines in the raw tag dump appears inverted** (e.g., receivables fell $8.4B but the tag shows −$8.3B). Working‑capital attribution was therefore avoided; only aggregate OCF was used.
- Q1 and Q2 FY26 individual revenue lines were not returned; 1H figures are derived from reported cumulative disclosures and are labeled as such.
