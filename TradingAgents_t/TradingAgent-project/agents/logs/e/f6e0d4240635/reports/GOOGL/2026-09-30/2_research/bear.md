## 1. The “FCF is just capex” argument is exactly the problem

You say FCF compression is 100% a capex choice. I agree—and that’s precisely why I’m cautious. A capex choice that consumes **37.5% of Q2 revenue** and turns **Q2 FCF negative at -$5.855B** is not a rounding error. It’s a different business model.

Look at the actual numbers:

- H1 2026 FCF: **$4.261B vs $24.254B** a year ago, down **82%**.
- Q2 2026 FCF: **-$5.855B**.
- H1 capex: **$80.598B**, up **103%**.
- Capex incurred but not yet paid: **$29.113B**, up from $10.635B. That’s a leading indicator that H2 cash outflows are still coming—not that a capex plateau is imminent.
- H1 depreciation: **$13.59B vs $9.49B**, up **43%**, while $80.6B of capex has barely begun to hit the P&L. That’s a 2027 margin headwind, not a bullish catalyst.

You call OCF growth of **+32.8%** the tell. True—operating cash flow is healthy. But equity holders don’t get OCF; they get FCF after capex. And if capex is *mandatory* to defend Google’s AI position, then it’s not a discretionary “choice” that can be switched off to release cash. It’s the cost of staying competitive. Your “every 5 points of capex intensity is ~$24B of FCF waiting to be released” is a theoretical option—not evidence of a plateau. The unpaid capex balance says the opposite.

## 2. The fortress balance sheet is not the floor you think it is

You’re right that GOOGL has ~$342B of cash and investments and ~$238B of net cash. That’s a real strength. But it doesn’t make the stock a bargain at $344.

The hidden NAV argument needs more honesty:

- The **$124.3B of non-marketable equity** is illiquid and marked. It is not cash available to fund capex or buybacks.
- The **$22.8B deferred tax liability** on unrealized gains is real future tax leakage.
- The **$17.4B legal/regulatory accrual** is a recurring cash/earnings drag.
- The “$224B hidden NAV” is roughly **$18/share**; net of tax, call it ~$16/share. Against a $344 stock, that’s about **5%**. It’s a cushion, not a floor.
- Asset marks are reversible. Q2’s **$99.0B** of equity fair-value gains are non-cash. A markdown would produce a violent GAAP EPS swing and remind the market that headline EPS was low quality.

And while the balance sheet is strong, the *direction* is worse: debt more than doubled to **$101.1B**, interest expense jumped to **$1.81B in H1 vs $0.30B**, buybacks went from **$28.3B to $0**, shares rose **1.2%**, and GOOGL issued **$30.5B of common stock** plus **$19.1B of convertible preferred**. You call that “confidence.” I call it dilution and releveraging. A company doesn’t pause buybacks and issue equity at scale if internal cash flow is comfortably funding the opportunity set.
