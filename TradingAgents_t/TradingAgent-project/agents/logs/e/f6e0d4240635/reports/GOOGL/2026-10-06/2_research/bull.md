But the bear’s conclusion—“therefore the AI spend is value-destructive”—doesn’t follow from the data. This is growth capex, not maintenance capex. Operating cash flow still rose **32.8% to $84.9B**. Deferred revenue rose **17.4%** to **$10.1B**, a positive demand indicator for Cloud/subscriptions. Gross margins expanded. And the balance sheet can absorb the build: GOOGL has **$242.5B** in cash and short-term investments against **$101.1B** of debt—roughly **$141B of net cash**. Debt/EBITDA is about **0.54x**, and Q2 interest coverage was **31.9x**. This is not a leveraged AI borrower straining under 5.3% Treasury yields. It is one of the strongest balance sheets in global equities funding a generational infrastructure cycle.

The bear will also point to dilution and the buyback pause. Fair. But it is also a funding choice made from strength, not distress. If Q3 confirms capex is peaking—watch for capex below **$45B** and any signal of a 2027 framework—then FCF inflects and the buyback option reopens.
