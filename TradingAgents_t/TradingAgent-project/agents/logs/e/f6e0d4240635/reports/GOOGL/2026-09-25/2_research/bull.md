### 1. Growth potential: this is not a maturing mega-cap

Look at H1 2026. GOOGL grew revenue **23.0% y/y to $229.7B**. Q2 alone was **+24.2% y/y**. Operating income rose **30.0% to $80.5B**, and operating margin expanded to **35.0% from 33.1%**. Gross margin hit **62.0%**, up 240 bps. That is operating leverage at massive scale.

And crucially, the core EPS story is still strong even after stripping out the noise. H1 2026 core EPS was roughly **$5.29, up 21.6% y/y**. Q2 core EPS was about **$2.61**. On an annualized run-rate near **$10.6–$11.0**, GOOGL at $343.92 is around **31–32x core EPS** — not cheap, but reasonable for a franchise growing revenue 23% with 35% operating margins and a net-cash balance sheet.

The bear will say, “But headline EPS was $14.24 and Q2 was $9.11, and 63–71% of pre-tax income came from non-operating equity gains.” **I agree. Don’t underwrite the headline EPS.** But that is not a bear argument against the operating business. It is an argument for using the right denominator. The operating business grew revenue 23%, operating income 30%, and core EPS 22% — while absorbing SBC up 32% and depreciation up 43%. That is not a deteriorating franchise. That is a franchise with real operating momentum.

### 2. Competitive advantages: the capex is a moat, not a bonfire

The bear’s best fundamental attack is the cash flow line: H1 capex was **$80.6B, up 103%**, free cash flow fell to **$4.3B from $24.3B**, and accrual-basis FCF was roughly **–$14B**. That is ugly. But the diagnosis matters.

GOOGL is not burning cash because the core business is weak. Operating cash flow rose **32.8% to $84.9B**. The company is deliberately investing in AI infrastructure, cloud capacity, and future monetization. It has **$242.5B of cash and short-term investments**, **$101.1B of debt**, and roughly **$141B of net cash**. Current ratio is about **2.8x**. This is not a leveraged company funding a hope story; it is a net-cash giant choosing to accelerate a land-grab in AI infrastructure.

Yes, depreciation and interest expense are rising. Interest expense was **$1.81B in H1, up 514%**, but that is still small against $80.5B of operating income. Yes, Q3 free cash flow could be negative because **$29.1B of accrued but unpaid capex** rolls into the period. But that is a timing and investment-cycle issue, not a solvency issue. If GOOGL sells off on a negative FCF print while revenue and margins remain intact, that is a buying opportunity, not a reason to abandon the franchise.

And the capex has strategic value. It supports Google Cloud, AI search, YouTube, Android, Chrome, and the broader Google Services ecosystem. The bear is treating the capex trough as if it were terminal value destruction. I’m treating it as the cost of defending and extending one of the strongest distribution networks in technology.

The exact commercial stage in Las Vegas is fuzzy, and Tokyo is long-dated, but that is the point: these are free options on autonomous driving, not the basis of today’s valuation. Add in the speculative space-based AI infrastructure work, and GOOGL has multiple long-duration call options attached to the core business.
