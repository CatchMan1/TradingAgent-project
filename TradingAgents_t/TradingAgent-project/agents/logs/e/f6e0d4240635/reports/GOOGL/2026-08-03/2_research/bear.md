But you’re making a category error: **you’re using a great company quarter to justify buying a stock that has already ripped +17.6% in seven sessions and +11.9% in two.** At **$373.51**, the market has largely paid for the quarter. The debate is what happens next — and that’s where your case gets thin.

## 2. Free cash flow is not a rounding error — it’s the core risk

You wave away the FCF collapse as “the price of admission.” Let’s put the admission price on the table:

- **H1 2026 free cash flow: $4.26B vs $24.25B — down 82.4%.**
- **Capex: $80.6B cash, 35.1% of revenue.**
- **Capex incurred but not yet paid: $29.1B, up 173.7%** — a leading indicator that reported capex is still rising.
- On an accrued basis, H1 capex commitments are closer to **~$99B**, or roughly a **$200B annualized run-rate**.
- **Depreciation rose 43.2% in H1** — and depreciation/revenue is still only **5.9%**. The full cost of this capex has not hit the P&L yet.

You say margin expansion proves the AI capex ROI. But you’re measuring the return **before the depreciation bill arrives.** The 43% depreciation increase is already here; the depreciation from the $80B+ H1 capex is not. That’s not proof of ROI — it’s proof of a lag.

And the “fortress balance sheet” argument cuts against you. Yes, GOOGL has **~$140B net cash**. But look at how it got there:

- **Financing inflow: +$86.3B**
- **Debt issued: $56.2B**
- **Common stock issued: $30.5B**
- **Convertible preferred issued: $19.1B**
- **Buybacks: $0 vs $28.3B last year**

The net cash position rose because **they raised capital**, not because free cash flow funded it. A company with $140B of net cash does not issue $30B of common equity and $19B of preferred unless the forward capex curve is bigger than internal cash generation. That’s the signal you’re ignoring.

## 3. Earnings quality: your own concession is the bear case

You conceded that **71% of Q2 pre-tax income was non-operating marks.** Let’s be precise:

- Q2 pre-tax: **$40.8B operating, $98.0B non-operating.**
- H1 equity securities fair-value gains: **$135.9B**, of which only **$836M was realized.**
- Private equity book: **$124.3B**, with **$85.7B of cumulative upward adjustments** and $9.1B of downward adjustments.
- Deferred tax liability: **$22.8B**, up from $919M.
- H1 deferred tax expense: **+$27.5B**, while accrued current taxes rose only ~$4.7B.

The marks are not cash. They are illiquid, mark-to-model, reversible, and negatively skewed. You say the assets are real — fine. But real illiquid marks can still reverse, and when they do, headline EPS collapses and the “cheap” screen flips.

And here’s the valuation kicker: **core Q2 EPS is only ~$2.65–$2.70.** Annualized, that’s roughly **$10.6**. At $373.51, GOOGL trades at **~35x core earnings** — while free cash flow is near zero. That’s not a bargain. That’s a premium multiple on the honest earnings, with the capex bill still coming.

## 4. Capital allocation has changed character — and you’re spinning it

You call 1.2% dilution “rounding error.” But it’s not just the 142M shares. It’s the direction:

- **Gross debt doubled** to **$102.0B**.
- **Interest expense rose 514%** to **$1.8B in H1**.
- **Buybacks went to zero.**
- **Common equity was issued for the first time in the modern Alphabet era.**
- **Convertible preferred is a new claim ahead of common** — $86M of preferred dividends already in Q2.

The historical **1.5–2% annual EPS tailwind from buybacks is gone** and has become a small headwind. Your “buyback call option” has no expiry date and no strike. You’re asking investors to pay today for a capital-return recovery that depends on the capex cycle peaking — which is exactly the thing that could keep FCF depressed for years.

You say if AI were hurting Search, total revenue would show it. That’s flawed. **Total revenue includes Cloud, YouTube, subscriptions, devices, and Network.** The fundamental report’s revenue line is derived arithmetically, not segmented. Strength elsewhere can mask Search softness.

And the news flow gives you a warning sign you dismissed too quickly: **Reddit’s CEO said Google Search referrals were “choppy” in Q2**, with Reddit pushing users to its app. That’s one partner’s datapoint, not proof of a collapse — but it’s exactly the kind of leading indicator you’d expect as AI answer surfaces intercept clicks.

Meanwhile, the competitive framing is not the slam dunk you think. The Nvidia-vs-Alphabet pieces note Nvidia revenue nearly tripled in two years versus Alphabet growing “steadily,” with the gap “narrowing faster than many expect,” and ask who wins “when the buildout slows.” That’s a direct challenge to your “AI winner” narrative. GOOGL is spending like a challenger to defend a franchise; Nvidia is monetizing the arms race.

Add the **$17.4B loss contingency accrual** and the **$3B payment in Q3 2025** — regulatory risk is live, sized, and not going away.

## 7. Macro/AI credit risk is a discount-rate problem

Loan investors are “pushing back for the first time in years,” which implies higher borrowing costs for AI-heavy companies. Even if GOOGL is less levered than some AI borrowers, a **$200B annualized capex program** makes it a discount-rate story. If AI monetization slows, capex becomes a de-rating catalyst, not a moat. And the AI complex is already dispersing — Micron −35.5% from highs while MSFT/AMZN rip. That’s not a rising tide; it’s a rotation.
