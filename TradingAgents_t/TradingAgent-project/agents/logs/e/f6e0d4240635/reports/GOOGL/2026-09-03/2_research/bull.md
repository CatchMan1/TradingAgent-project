
Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for GOOGL — The Coiled Spring Nobody Wants to Own

Let me start by naming the bear case before you make it, because I want to be clear about what I'm actually arguing against.

The bear will say: *The stock is 15% off its high, the 50-day is falling, a death cross is coming, Q2 free cash flow was negative for the first time in modern history, earnings are 63% accounting marks, capex is 35% of revenue with depreciation about to eat the margin, and the antitrust ruling still imposes remedies.* Every one of those facts is in my data pack and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

Here's my counter: **four of those are lagging or already-priced, one is a deliberate investment choice funded from a position of absurd strength, and the single biggest binary risk on the stock was removed after the close on September 2 — on a tape so washed out that the relief bid hasn't even shown up yet.**

Let's go through it.

---

## 1. The growth engine is *accelerating*, not decelerating

The bear wants to talk about free cash flow. Fine — but first, look at what the business is actually doing:

| Metric | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | YoY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $186.7B | **$229.7B** | **+23.1%** |
| Operating income | $61.9B | **$80.5B** | **+30.0%** |
| Gross margin | 59.6% | **62.0%** | **+240 bps** |
| Normalized EPS | ~$4.35 | **~$5.30** | **+21.8%** |

And the cadence is the tell: **Q1 +21.8% → Q2 +24.2%.** Revenue annualizes at roughly **$459B**. This is a company accelerating at a $2T+ market cap, *while* spending 35% of revenue on capex.

That last combination is the part the bear keeps missing. If you double capex and gross margin still expands 240bps, the incremental AI/cloud revenue is landing at **attractive margins**. That is the single strongest refutation of the "capex is destroying returns" thesis sitting in the data. The bear is modeling cost without modeling the revenue that cost is buying.

## 2. The catalyst you can't find in the chart: the breakup is off the table

On September 2, after the close, a judge **spared Google an ad-business breakup** and ordered operational changes instead. The second headline said it plainly: *"The worst-case breakup risk just became far less likely."*

This matters enormously because of *when* it happened and *what the tape looked like going in*:

- It landed **after the Sept 2 cash close** — so it was unpriced into the Sept 3 session.
- Sept 3 closed **+1.59% on 93% of average volume.** That is a *muted* relief bid. The market has not repriced this. A binary tail risk was removed and the stock barely moved on light volume.

And the setup going in? CNBC's own framing: **"longest monthly losing streak in over a decade."** Positioning was washed out. When a decade-long losing streak meets a positive regulatory resolution on light volume, that's not a top — that's a spring.

The bear will say "but the judge ordered remedies to benefit competitors." Sure — and the balance sheet already carries **$17.4B of legal loss-contingency accruals**, up $1.8B in Q2 alone. The market has provisioned for the bad outcome. What it *hasn't* priced is the removal of the catastrophic one.

## 3. The AI moat just got third-party validation — and the power constraint is being solved

The bear case on AI is "you're spending $180B a year and you don't own the model layer." The news flow this week says otherwise:

- **Broadcom's CEO said Google TPU orders are "exploding,"** with Anthropic on track to become Broadcom's largest XPU customer and planning **5 GW of deployments in 2027.** Alphabet's in-house silicon is becoming an external product, not just a cost center.
- **Broadcom Q3 FY26 revenue +86% y/y to $29.6B** on custom AI accelerators — the supply chain confirms the demand is real, not narrative.
- **Fervo geothermal: an expandable ~3 GW framework**, plus Eos Energy storage for a $350M West Virginia project. The "AI can't get power" bear thesis is being actively dismantled by Alphabet signing the largest geothermal PPA ever.

Yes, the Broadcom reception was "beat just isn't enough." But that's a statement about **Broadcom's valuation**, not Alphabet's. Alphabet is the one coming off a historic losing streak with low expectations — the marginal payoff to good AI news is *highest* here, not lowest.

## 4. The FCF scare is strength wearing a scary mask

Now the bear's best punch: **Q2 free cash flow was −$5.9B.** Let me concede it fully — and then explain why it's not the signal the bear thinks.

| Item | H1 2026 |
|---|---:|
| Operating cash flow | $84.9B (+32.8%) |
| Capex | $80.6B (+103%) |
| FCF | +$4.3B (−82%) |
| **Cash + ST investments** | **$242.5B** |
| **Total debt** | **$101.1B** |
| **Net cash** | **+$141.4B** |

A company with **$141B of net cash**, gross debt/equity of **16%**, and a **2.7x current ratio** is not in distress — it is choosing to invest. That's the whole point. Run the math: at the current capex trajectory, Alphabet has **3+ years of runway before it needs external funding again.** There is no financing shock lurking here.

And on the earnings-quality objection — the bear is right that **63% of H1 pre-tax income was investment marks** and that $14.24 GAAP EPS is not a run-rate. I'll give them that all day. **Use ~$5.30 normalized H1 EPS.** Guess what? That's still **+21.8% growth.** The operating engine compounds at ~20% whether or not the private-company marks reverse. The marks are non-cash and reversible; the cash-generating business underneath is not.

**On the depreciation cliff:** yes, D&A must ramp from ~17% of capex toward capex. But that's a 2027–2028 headwind arriving against a business growing 24% with *expanding* gross margins. And the stock is already 15% off its high — some of that math is in the price. The bear is treating a known, modelable, multi-year transition as if it were an surprise.

## 7. Where I'll admit the bear has a point — and why I still win

- **Insider data is unavailable.** Genuine gap. But that's uncertainty, not a negative signal — don't let it be smuggled in as one.
- **The $29.1B accrued-but-unpaid capex** is a real Q3 FCF headwind. Conceded. It's also *known and modelable* — the market can discount a disclosed balance-sheet item.
- **Behavioral ad-tech remedies** are a slow-burn margin headwind for 2–6 quarters. True. But Google's scale, data, distribution, and default-position assets don't disappear because of an interoperability mandate.
- **Dilution** (+1.2% shares, $19B convertible preferred) is real. But it funded an AI build from a net-cash position, and the stock has re-rated ~28% above the ~$267 common issuance price since — the market is validating the deployment, not punishing it.

**What would change my mind:** a decisive close below **$333.05** (breaks the 200 SMA, the rising lower band, and the wedge floor simultaneously); capex guidance raised again with no revenue acceleration; or the buyback halt extending into 2027 alongside *further* equity issuance.
