## 1. Executive Summary

Alphabet's Q2 FY2026 results show **two very different companies layered on top of each other**, and conflating them will lead to badly wrong trading conclusions:

1. **A core advertising/cloud/AI business that is accelerating and expanding margins.** 1H26 revenue reached **$229.7B (+23.1% YoY)**, operating income **$80.5B (+30.0%)**, and operating margin expanded **+190bp to 35.0%**. Q2 revenue growth of **+24.2%** was faster than Q1's +21.8% — i.e., growth *accelerated* sequentially.
2. **A portfolio of equity stakes that just revalued by an extraordinary amount.** Non-operating income was **$98.0B in Q2 alone** and **$135.8B in 1H26**, driven by **$99.0B / $135.9B of FV-NI equity securities gains**. This inflated GAAP diluted EPS to **$9.11 in Q2 (+294% YoY)** and **$14.24 for 1H26**.

**The critical trading insight:** headline GAAP EPS is **roughly 3.5x "core" earnings power** in Q2. Any screen, consensus comparison, or P/E calculation based on the $14.24 1H print will materially misprice GOOGL. The economically meaningful figure is **~$2.60 of core quarterly EPS**, growing ~15–20% YoY, with operating income growing 30%.

Offsetting that strength: **capex has exploded to $80.6B in 1H26 (35.1% of revenue)**, free cash flow collapsed **-82% YoY to $4.3B**, and **Q2 FCF turned negative (-$5.9B)** — the first negative quarter in the dataset. Management financed this with **$56.2B of new debt, $30.5B of common stock, $19.1B of convertible preferred, and $3.8B of minority interests**, while **suspending buybacks entirely ($0 vs $28.3B in 1H25)**.

## 2. Company Profile & Business Context

Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) is the parent of Google, operating the world's largest digital advertising franchise (Search, YouTube, Network), Google Cloud, and a portfolio of AI/Other Bets. The SEC fact set provided does not break out segment revenue, so segment-level conclusions below are inferred only where the aggregate data supports them; **segment detail should be treated as unverified** in this report.

What the data *does* clearly show is a company in an aggressive **AI infrastructure build-out**:
- Capex of **$80.6B in six months** (vs $39.6B), with **$29.1B of capex already incurred but unpaid** at 6/30/26 (vs $10.6B) — a leading indicator that Q3 cash capex will be *higher still*.
- Depreciation up **+43%** to $13.6B (the capex is starting to hit the P&L).
- SBC up **+32%** to $15.2B, consistent with heavy AI talent competition.
- **$22.1B** deployed into "other investments" (non-marketable equity) in 1H26 vs $2.3B a year ago, and **finite-lived intangibles gross jumped from $2.47B to $10.59B** — evidence of a meaningful acquisition and/or strategic stakes being added.

### 3.3 Isolating recurring earnings power

Q2 FY26 non-operating income of $97,983M is almost entirely the **$99,031M FV-NI equity securities gain** (interest income $1,430M, interest expense -$1,278M, FX/other roughly flat). Stripping it out:

- **Core pre-tax income ≈ $40.8B operating − $1.0B net non-operating = ~$39.7B**
- At a 19.1% tax rate: **~$32.1B core net income → ~$2.61 diluted EPS**
- vs Q2 FY25 core of roughly $2.2–2.3 → **core EPS growth of ~15–18%**, consistent with +30% operating income less higher interest expense and a 220bp higher tax rate.

**Trailing 2-quarter annualized core run-rate: ~$10.4–10.6 EPS.** That, not $14.24 (a two-quarter figure containing a one-time-style revaluation), is the correct anchor for valuation work.

Interpretation:

- The **$21.9B jump in deferred tax liabilities** confirms these are largely *unrealized* gains — non-cash, and a tax claim that will crystallize only on sale.
- **These marks are reversible.** A private-market valuation reset would produce a symmetric GAAP earnings hit with no cash impact. Traders must treat this line as a **volatility source, not earnings quality**.

### 4.2 Key balance sheet takeaways

**Liquidity is enormous but borrowed.**
- **$242.5B** of cash + short-term investments plus **$99.5B** of longer-dated AFS debt securities ≈ **$342B gross liquidity**.
- Against **$101.1B** of debt (plus $2.6B finance leases and $18.0B preferred), **net cash remains strongly positive at roughly $141B** on a cash+ST-investments basis, or **~$241B** including long-term securities.
- **However**, debt more than doubled in six months (+$52.0B) and the company simultaneously issued **$30.5B of common stock** (≈142M shares at ~$215) and **$19.1B of convertible preferred (June 5, 2026)**. Issuing equity and preferred at the same time as record GAAP earnings is a strong signal that **capex commitments exceed even Alphabet's prodigious internal cash generation**.
- **Preferred dividends of $86M/quarter** (~1.9% implied coupon on $18B) sit *ahead* of common — a new, if modest, claim on earnings, plus conversion dilution risk.

**Working capital quality is fine, with two flags.**
- Receivables grew only **+10%** against revenue **+23%** → **DSO ≈ 54 days**, implying receivables are *improving* (collection efficiency, mix shift). No channel-stuffing signal.
- But **accrued income taxes (current) jumped 10x to $5.2B** and **accrued liabilities +31%** — consistent with tax on the gains and heavy accrued capex/opex.
- **Contract liabilities (deferred revenue) +17.4% to $10.1B** — a modest positive leading indicator for Cloud/enterprise bookings, though slower than revenue growth.

**Asset quality caveat:** total assets rose $326.7B (+55%) in six months, of which roughly **$116B is cash/securities, $60B is the equity-stake book, and the remainder is capex-driven PP&E plus intangibles**. Reported book value is increasingly a mark-to-market number rather than a historical-cost one.

## 8. Actionable Insights for Traders

### 8.1 What to believe about earnings
1. **Do not use GAAP P/E on this print.** 1H26 diluted EPS of $14.24 includes ~$135.9B of pre-tax equity marks (~$110B after tax, or **~$9.00 of the $14.24**). A screen at "1H EPS × 2" would understate the P/E by roughly **3x**.
   - **Use instead:** Q2 operating income of **$40.8B** (annualized ~$163B), or core EPS of **~$2.60/quarter (~$10.4–10.6 annualized)**.
2. **The core franchise is genuinely accelerating.** Revenue growth went 21.8% → 24.2% sequentially, gross margin rose 210bp YoY, and operating income grew 30% against 24% revenue growth. That is operating leverage *despite* +43% depreciation and +32% SBC. This is the bullish anchor.
3. **Watch the sequential margin reversal.** Operating margin fell from **36.1% in Q1 to 34.0% in Q2** — a 210bp sequential decline. Q1 is seasonally strong, but with depreciation compounding on $80B+ of 1H capex, **Q3 margin could compress further**. A 34% handle holding would be a positive; a break below ~32.5% would likely be read as AI-cost digestion.

### 8.2 What to watch in the next 1–2 quarters
4. **Q3 capex vs. the ~$45B Q2 run-rate.** The $29.1B of accrued-but-unpaid capex is the key tell. If Q3 cash capex prints **above ~$48B**, expect renewed FCF anxiety and multiple compression regardless of revenue. If it plateaus near $45B, the "peak capex" narrative can take hold — a powerful re-rating catalyst.
5. **FCF inflection is the swing factor.** 1H26 FCF of $4.3B vs $24.3B is an 82% decline. Two consecutive negative-FCF quarters would likely force another capital raise (debt is the path of least resistance given $101B debt and huge untapped capacity) and keep buybacks off the table.
6. **Buyback resumption is a discrete catalyst.** $0 of repurchases in 1H26 vs $28.3B in 1H25 removes a persistent bid. Any announced resumption — even modest — would be a clear signal management sees the capex peak.
7. **Reversal risk on the equity book.** $124.3B of non-marketable equity carrying value with $85.7B of cumulative *upward* marks is now a **material earnings volatility engine**. A private-market markdown of even 15–20% would be a ~$19–25B pre-tax hit (~$1.50–2.00 EPS). Position sizing should account for this asymmetry; it is a genuine two-sided risk that consensus models likely exclude.

### 8.3 Balance sheet and dilution
8. **Net cash is still ~$141B** (cash+ST investments $242.5B less debt $101.1B), or ~$241B including $99.5B of long-term AFS securities. Credit quality is not a concern; leverage is a *choice*, not a constraint. This is the strongest argument that capex is opportunistic rather than defensive.
9. **Dilution is now a real, if small, drag.** Shares +1.2% in six months, plus $18.0B of convertible preferred with $86M/quarter dividends ahead of common. Model **~12.3B diluted shares** going forward, rising if the preferred converts.
10. **Tax rate reset higher to ~19.1%** from ~17%. Combined with the $22.8B deferred tax liability, cash taxes should be monitored — the DTL is a future claim, not a current cash cost.

### 8.4 Structural/M&A signals
11. **$22.1B into "other investments" + $8.1B of new finite-lived intangibles** signals a step-up in inorganic capital deployment (stakes and/or acquisitions). This creates integration and impairment risk, but also optionality on AI ecosystem assets. The absence of segment disclosure in this dataset means **the return on these investments cannot yet be verified** — request the 10-Q business-combination and investment notes before sizing a position on this theme.

### 8.5 Net assessment
**Constructive on the operating business; cautious on the equity story's near-term mechanics.** Fundamental momentum is exceptional (revenue +24%, operating income +30%, margin +190bp), liquidity is fortress-like, and deferred revenue is growing. But three things cap near-term multiple expansion: (a) **negative Q2 FCF**, (b) **no buyback**, and (c) **GAAP EPS that is ~3.5x core and volatile in both directions**. The cleanest expression of the bull case is *operating income growth*; the cleanest expression of the bear case is *capex intensity at 35% of revenue with FCF at 1.9%*. Watch Q3 capex and the first positive FCF print as the pivot.
