## 1. Executive summary

GOOGL’s Q2 2026 was an extraordinary quarter on the surface and a very good quarter underneath.

- **Revenue accelerated sharply:** Q2 2026 revenue was **$119.8B, +24.2% YoY**, after +21.8% in Q1 2026 and +15.7% for FY2025. H1 2026 revenue was **$229.7B, +23.1% YoY**.
- **Operating leverage is real:** Q2 operating income rose **+30.4% to $40.8B** (34.0% margin); H1 operating income rose **+30.0% to $80.5B** with a **35.0% margin**, up ~190bp YoY. H1 gross margin expanded ~240bp to **62.0%**.
- **But reported EPS is heavily distorted by non-cash investment marks:** Q2 diluted EPS of **$9.11** included roughly **$6.51/share** of after-tax equity-securities revaluation gains. H1 diluted EPS of **$14.24** included roughly **$8.96/share** of such gains. Excluding all non-operating items, “core” EPS was approximately **$2.67 in Q2 (+25.4% YoY)** and **$5.30 in H1 (+26.6% YoY)**.
- **Free cash flow broke down:** H1 operating cash flow rose **+32.8% to $84.9B**, but capex more than doubled to **$80.6B (+103.3%)**, leaving **FCF of only $4.3B, down 82.4% YoY**. Capex was **35.1% of revenue** in H1 2026 versus 21.2% in H1 2025.
- **Capital allocation has regime-shifted:** H1 2026 share repurchases were **$0** versus **$28.3B** in H1 2025. GOOGL raised **$56.2B of debt**, **$30.5B of common equity**, **$19.1B of convertible preferred**, and **$3.8B from minority shareholders**.
- **Balance sheet remains a fortress:** Cash plus short-term investments were **$242.5B** against **$101.1B of debt**, for **net cash of ~$141.4B** and 2.4x cash coverage.
- **A large acquisition is strongly implied:** Roughly **$34–36B of H1 2026 investing outflow** is unexplained by capex, marketable securities, or other-investment lines — most likely acquisition payments. Gross finite-lived intangibles jumped **+329% to $10.6B**, and the Q2 investment gain is consistent with remeasurement of a pre-existing equity stake in an acquired business (inference; confirm in the 10-Q business-combination note).

**Fundamental stance:** Constructive on GOOGL’s operating momentum and balance-sheet strength; cautious on headline earnings quality and near-zero FCF. GOOGL should be underwritten on **core EPS (~$10.6 annualized)** and operating cash flow, **not** headline EPS (~$28.5 annualized), which overstates run-rate earnings by roughly **2.7x**. Without a price input, no Buy/Sell rating is assigned; the drivers below are what should determine the trade.

## 2. Company profile

GOOGL is the ticker for **Alphabet Inc.**, the parent company of Google. Alphabet reports three segments:

- **Google Services:** Google Search and other advertising, YouTube advertising, Google Network, subscriptions/platforms/devices (Android, Chrome, Google Play, hardware, YouTube subscriptions).
- **Google Cloud:** Google Cloud Platform infrastructure, data analytics, AI/ML platform, and Google Workspace collaboration and productivity tools.
- **Other Bets:** Early-stage businesses, including autonomous driving (Waymo), life sciences (Verily), and other ventures.

**Business model:** Primarily advertising-funded, with growing cloud, subscription, device, and licensing revenue. Alphabet’s competitive advantages include search distribution and user data, global compute infrastructure, AI research depth, the Android/Chrome ecosystem, and YouTube’s scale. Key structural risks include advertising cyclicality, AI-driven changes to search economics, cloud/AI competition, antitrust and regulatory exposure, and the capital intensity of AI infrastructure.

## 6. Earnings quality — the $135.9B question

The most important analytical adjustment for GOOGL is separating operating earnings from investment marks.

| Item | Q2 2026 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Equity securities FVNI gain (pre-tax) | 99,031 | 135,946 | 11,044 |
| After-tax equity gain at effective rate | 80,077 | 109,913 | 9,150 |
| Equity gain per diluted share | ~$6.51 | ~$8.96 | ~$0.75 |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $9.11 | $14.24 | $5.12 |
| Core EPS excluding all non-operating items | $2.67 | $5.30 | $4.18 |
| Share of GAAP EPS from equity marks | ~71% | ~63% | ~15% |

**Interpretation:**

- Q2 2026’s **$99.0B** equity-securities gain is roughly **2.4x operating income**. It is non-cash and runs through the income statement under fair-value accounting for equity securities.
- The gain is concentrated in Q2 and coincides with a likely large acquisition. A plausible explanation is **remeasurement of a pre-existing equity stake** upon obtaining control of a business. That would make the gain largely **one-time and non-recurring**, not an ongoing return on an investment portfolio. This must be confirmed in the 10-Q business-combination note, which was not returned by the vendor.
- The balance-sheet corroboration: **non-marketable equity securities rose 93.9% to $124.3B** in H1 2026, and cumulative upward revaluation rose from **$44.5B to $85.7B**.
- **Trading implication:** any valuation screen using GAAP EPS is misleading. Annualized Q2 headline EPS is ~$36.44; annualized core EPS is ~$10.69. Headline EPS overstates run-rate earnings by roughly **3.4x** at the Q2 rate and **2.7x** at the H1 rate.

## 10. M&A and investment signals

Several independent data points point to a large business combination in H1 2026:

1. **Unexplained investing outflow of ~$34–36B.** H1 2026 net investing cash flow was -$145.8B. Capex, marketable securities, and other-investment lines explain roughly -$109B to -$112B. The residual is ~$34–36B, versus a ~$1B residual in H1 2025. This is most likely acquisitions, net of cash acquired.
2. **Gross finite-lived intangibles jumped from $2.5B to $10.6B (+329%).** Acquired intangibles typically rise with a business combination. Accumulated amortization rose only from $1.38B to $1.69B, implying net intangibles of roughly $8.9B.
3. **Common stock issued: $30.5B.** Shares outstanding rose by 142M to 12.230B, implying an average issuance price of roughly **$215/share**. This is consistent with stock consideration in an acquisition or a large equity offering. Either way, it is a major funding event.
4. **Convertible preferred issued: $19.1B gross / $18.0B carrying.** Combined with $3.8B of minority-interest proceeds, this suggests capital raised at the subsidiary level.
5. **Q2 equity-securities gain of $99.0B.** As noted, this is consistent with remeasurement of a pre-existing stake upon consolidation of an acquired business.

**Inference:** GOOGL likely closed a multi-tens-of-billions acquisition in H1 2026, funded with a mix of cash, stock, and subsidiary-level preferred/minority capital, and recognized a large one-time gain on a pre-existing equity stake. The target and purchase price are not identifiable from the returned tags. Goodwill and the business-combination note were not returned; these are the key missing items for assessing deal quality and impairment risk.

## 13. Fundamental risks

1. **Earnings quality and reversal risk.** 63% of H1 2026 EPS and 71% of Q2 EPS came from non-cash equity marks. A 10% reversal of the $124.3B non-marketable equity portfolio would reduce pre-tax income by ~$12.4B (~$1.00/share after tax). A 20% reversal would cut EPS by ~$2.00. Headline EPS is highly volatile and not a reliable valuation base.
2. **Free-cash-flow compression.** Capex is 35% of revenue and still ramping (capex incurred but unpaid +174% YoY). If capex stays at ~$40B/quarter, FY2026 FCF could be near zero. FCF-based valuation screens will look extremely unattractive.
3. **Rising leverage and funding needs.** Debt doubled to $101.1B in six months; interest expense rose 6x. GOOGL also issued $30.5B of common equity and $19.1B of convertible preferred. Further equity or debt issuance would increase dilution or interest burden. Buybacks are paused.
4. **M&A integration and goodwill impairment risk.** A likely ~$35B+ acquisition, a 329% jump in gross intangibles, and a large one-time gain raise questions about purchase price, goodwill, and integration. These details were not returned by the vendor.
5. **Tax headwind.** The effective tax rate rose to 19.1% from 16.8% in FY2025 and 13.9% in FY2023. Deferred tax liabilities of $22.8B represent a future cash tax overhang if investment gains are realized.
6. **Depreciation ramp.** Capex of $80.6B in H1 versus depreciation of $13.6B implies a substantial future depreciation increase. This will pressure operating margins over the next 2–3 years unless revenue growth outpaces it.
7. **Legal and regulatory exposure.** Current loss contingency accruals of $17.4B, plus antitrust and AI-regulation risk. The accrual is large but may not capture tail outcomes.
8. **Competitive/AI disruption.** Search economics face AI-driven change; cloud and AI infrastructure competition is intense. The capex arms race could compress returns if demand growth slows.

## 14. Actionable insights and what to watch

### For valuation and positioning

1. **Underwrite GOOGL on core EPS, not GAAP EPS.** Annualized H1 2026 core EPS is ~$10.6; annualized headline EPS is ~$28.5. A headline P/E of X is equivalent to a core P/E of roughly **2.7X**. Any “cheap on earnings” conclusion based on GAAP EPS is a screen artifact.
2. **Treat the $135.9B H1 gain as non-recurring until proven otherwise.** The concentration in Q2 and the acquisition signals strongly suggest a one-time remeasurement. Do not capitalize it into normalized earnings.
3. **The operating momentum is genuine and broad.** Revenue growth accelerated for four consecutive quarters (Q3 2025 ~+16%, Q4 2025 ~+20%, Q1 2026 +21.8%, Q2 2026 +24.2%). H1 operating margin of 35.0% is a record in the dataset. This supports a premium multiple on core earnings if sustained.
4. **FCF is the key swing factor.** H1 FCF of $4.3B is the weakest in the dataset. The bull case requires capex to plateau and OCF to keep growing; the bear case is another year of $160B+ capex with no FCF inflection.
5. **Watch for buyback resumption as a positive catalyst.** H1 buybacks were zero versus $28.3B a year ago. A resumption would signal management confidence in FCF and support per-share value. Continued zero buybacks plus further equity issuance would be a negative signal.
6. **Balance-sheet strength provides downside support.** Net cash of ~$141B and 44x interest coverage mean GOOGL can fund the investment cycle without distress. This makes the equity more resilient than the FCF headline suggests.
7. **Monitor the equity marks.** The $124.3B non-marketable portfolio is ~13.5% of total assets. Its marks can swing EPS by several dollars per share per quarter. Options markets may misprice this volatility.

### Trade framing

## 15. Methodology and limitations

- **Source:** Point-in-time SEC XBRL facts returned by the configured fundamental data vendor, filtered by EDGAR acceptance time through 2026-09-22.
- **Revenue derivation:** For Q2/H1 2026, revenue = `CostsAndExpenses` + `OperatingIncomeLoss`. This is an accounting identity on Alphabet’s income statement. For FY2021–FY2024, reported revenue and operating income are used and are consistent with the dataset’s cost and net-income facts. FY2025 revenue and operating income are derived from FY2025 diluted EPS, implied diluted shares, and the reported tax rate; they are approximate.
- **Core EPS:** Operating income taxed at the reported effective rate, less preferred dividends, divided by diluted shares. This excludes all non-operating investment gains/losses, interest income, and interest expense. It is a non-GAAP analytical construct, not a company-reported metric.
- **Equity:** The `StockholdersEquity` and `Liabilities` tags were not returned. The ~$49–52/share book value estimate is derived from total assets less disclosed liability components and should be treated as approximate.
- **Segment data, stock price, consensus estimates, and insider transactions were not available.** No market-price-based valuation multiples are presented.
- **Rounding:** Percentages and totals may not foot exactly due to rounding.
