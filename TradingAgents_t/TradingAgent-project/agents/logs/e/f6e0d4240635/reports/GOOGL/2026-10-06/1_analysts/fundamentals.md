## 1. Executive summary

- **Core operating momentum is strong.** H1 2026 revenue reached **$229.7B**, up **23.1% YoY**, with Q2 revenue of **$119.8B**, up **24.2% YoY** — a clear acceleration from Alphabet’s historical growth rate.
- **Margins expanded.** H1 gross margin rose **243 bps to 62.0%**, and operating margin rose **184 bps to 35.0%**. Q2 operating margin was **34.0%**, down **209 bps QoQ** as operating expenses stepped up.
- **Reported earnings are massively distorted by non-cash investment gains.** H1 2026 pretax income was **$216.2B**, of which **$135.9B** came from equity-securities fair-value gains. Q2 alone included **$99.0B** of such gains. Reported diluted EPS of **$14.24** compares with estimated **core EPS of ~$5.29**.
- **Cash generation remains healthy, but free cash flow has collapsed.** H1 operating cash flow rose **32.8% to $84.9B**, but capex more than doubled to **$80.6B**. H1 free cash flow fell **82% to $4.3B**, and Q2 FCF was **negative $5.9B**.
- **Capital allocation has undergone a regime change.** Alphabet raised **$56.2B of debt**, issued **$30.5B of common stock**, and issued **$19.1B of convertible preferred** in H1 2026, while **pausing buybacks entirely** (vs. $28.3B in H1 2025).
- **The balance sheet remains a fortress.** Cash plus short-term investments totaled **$242.5B**, against **$101.1B of debt**, implying **~$141B of net cash**. Interest coverage is roughly **32x** in Q2.
- **The central debate is capex returns, not near-term earnings.** H1 capex was **35.1% of revenue**; Q2 capex alone annualizes to roughly **$180B**. Investors must decide whether this AI-infrastructure spend produces durable Cloud/Search monetization before depreciation and dilution pressure per-share value.
- **Insider transaction data is unavailable** in the vendor’s point-in-time archive. No inference about insider buying or selling should be drawn.

## 2. Company profile and business model

Alphabet Inc. is the parent company of Google and is one of the world’s largest internet, advertising, cloud, and AI companies. It reports three business segments:

| Segment | Principal businesses | Economic characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Services** | Google Search & other, YouTube ads, Google Network, subscriptions/platforms/devices, Android, Chrome, Maps, Play | Largest revenue and profit pool; advertising-led, high-margin, massive global scale |
| **Google Cloud** | Google Cloud Platform, Google Workspace, enterprise AI infrastructure | Faster-growing, capital-intensive, increasingly profitable at scale |
| **Other Bets** | Waymo, Verily, Wing, and other early-stage ventures | Small revenue base; historically loss-making; long-duration optionality |

Alphabet’s competitive advantages include search distribution, Android/Chrome ecosystems, YouTube’s content graph, vast proprietary data, and vertically integrated AI infrastructure (TPUs and data centers). The company has a dual-class share structure: GOOGL represents Class A shares, while Class B shares carry super-voting rights.

**Note on segment data:** The vendor extract did not surface segment-level revenue or operating income for 2026. Segment commentary below is therefore qualitative and should not be treated as reported segment performance.

### 4.2 What the income statement actually says

**Revenue growth is accelerating.** Q1 2026 revenue grew **21.8%**, Q2 grew **24.2%**, and H1 grew **23.1%**. This is well above Alphabet’s pre-2026 trajectory and suggests AI-related demand, Cloud strength, and resilient Search/YouTube monetization. Q2 revenue rose **9.0% QoQ**.

**Gross margin expansion is meaningful.** Gross margin improved from **59.6% to 62.0%** in H1, a **243 bp** gain. Cost of revenue grew only **15.7%**, well below revenue growth of **23.1%**, indicating operating leverage in the underlying business and a favorable mix shift.

**Operating income grew 30.0%**, faster than revenue, but the margin story is more nuanced:
- Q1 2026 operating margin was **36.1%**, a strong sequential high.
- Q2 2026 operating margin fell to **34.0%**, down **209 bps QoQ**, despite revenue rising **9.0%**.
- Q2 operating expenses rose **14.4% QoQ** and **26.7% YoY**, driven by G&A (+50.6% QoQ), SBC (+11.1% QoQ), and likely AI-related R&D.
- Q2 G&A of **$6.46B** was up from **$4.29B** in Q1; the company’s current legal accrual rose to **$17.4B**, suggesting legal/regulatory charges may be part of the step-up.

**The tax rate is a new headwind.** The effective tax rate rose from **17.1% to 19.1%**, a **200 bp** increase. If sustained, this alone is roughly a **2.5% headwind** to core EPS growth.

### 4.3 Quality of earnings: the $136 billion question

The reported income statement is not a clean representation of operating performance:

- **H1 2026 pretax income of $216.2B included $135.9B of equity-securities fair-value gains** — **62.9%** of pretax income.
- **Q2 2026 pretax income of $138.8B included $99.0B of such gains** — **71.4%** of pretax income.
- **Nonoperating income of $135.7B is almost entirely investment marks.** Excluding equity gains, core nonoperating income was **negative $247M** for H1.
- **Operating cash flow / reported net income = 0.49x**, versus **1.02x** in H1 2025. The gap confirms the gains are non-cash.
- **Deferred income tax liabilities jumped from $0.9B to $22.8B**, an increase of **$21.9B**, consistent with book-tax timing differences on unrealized investment gains.
- **Equity securities without readily determinable fair value rose to $124.3B**, or **13.5% of total assets**. Cumulative upward fair-value adjustments reached **$85.7B**. These are illiquid, valuation-sensitive marks, not cash earnings.

**Actionable conclusion:** Screeners and headline GAAP P/E ratios will make GOOGL look far cheaper than it is. Traders should value the business on **core EPS of ~$5.29 for H1 2026**, or **~$10.58 annualized**, not on the reported **$14.24 H1 EPS**.

### 5.2 Liquidity and leverage

- **Cash + short-term investments of $242.5B** exceed total debt of **$101.1B** by **$141.4B**.
- Even including finance and operating leases, the company remains in a **net cash position of roughly $120B**.
- **Debt/EBITDA:** annualized core operating income of **$160.9B** plus annualized D&A of roughly **$27.2B** gives EBITDA of ~**$188B**. Debt/EBITDA is therefore approximately **0.54x** — very low for a company of this size.
- **Interest coverage:** Q2 operating income of **$40.8B** covered interest expense of **$1.28B** by **31.9x**. H1 coverage was **44.4x**.

Interest expense is rising quickly, however: Q2 interest expense of **$1.28B** was up from **$261M** a year earlier, and H1 rose from **$295M to $1.81B**. Net interest income narrowed to **$152M** in Q2 from **$789M** a year earlier. As debt doubles, the interest tailwind is fading.

### 6.2 The capex ramp is unprecedented for Alphabet

- H1 2026 capex of **$80.6B** was **2.03x** H1 2025 capex of **$39.6B**.
- Q2 2026 capex of **$44.9B** annualizes to roughly **$180B**.
- Capex reached **37.5% of Q2 revenue**, versus **23.3%** a year earlier.
- **Capex incurred but not yet paid** reached **$29.1B** at 6/30/26, up from **$10.6B** a year earlier. This is **36% of H1 capex** and suggests Q3 cash capex could remain elevated as payables settle.

The implications are important:

1. **Operating cash flow is flattered by unpaid capex.** H1 OCF benefited from an **$8.1B increase in accounts payable** and a **$17.5B increase in accrued liabilities**, much of which relates to capex accruals. True cash capex may catch up in Q3/Q4.
2. **Free cash flow is now negative in a quarter.** Q2 FCF of **−$5.9B** is a stark reversal from **+$5.3B** a year earlier. H1 FCF of **$4.3B** represents a **1.9% FCF margin**, down from **13.0%**.
3. **Depreciation is set to accelerate.** H1 depreciation rose **43.2%** to **$13.6B**. With capex running at roughly **6x depreciation**, future D&A will be a significant margin headwind in 2027 and beyond.
4. **External financing was required.** The company raised over **$100B gross** from debt, common equity, preferred equity, and minority interests in H1 to fund the **$145.8B investing outflow**.

**Bull interpretation:** Alphabet is front-loading AI infrastructure while demand is strong; capex will peak, and FCF will inflect sharply in 2027 as utilization and monetization ramp.

**Bear interpretation:** Capex is becoming structurally higher, depreciation will compress margins, and the company is diluting shareholders to fund a low-visibility return profile.

## 7. Capital allocation: from buybacks to issuance

This is one of the most important fundamental changes in the report.

### 7.1 Historical capital allocation

| USD millions | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Financing cash flow | −72,093 | −79,733 | −37,388 | +86,320 |
| Capex incurred but unpaid (year-end) | 7,435 | 10,326 | 15,090 | 29,113 |

Alphabet was a **massive repurchaser** of stock in 2023–2024, with financing outflows of **$72B** and **$80B**, respectively. In 2025, buybacks moderated and financing outflow fell to **$37B**. In H1 2026, the company became a **net issuer of capital**:

- **Buybacks: $0**, versus **$28.3B** in H1 2025.
- **Common stock issuance: $30.5B** — equivalent to roughly **1.2% of shares outstanding**, at an implied average issuance price of approximately **$215/share**.
- **Convertible preferred issuance: $19.1B** on 2026-06-05 — a new and material capital-structure event.
- **Debt: +$52.0B**, more than doubling gross debt to **$101.1B**.
- **Minority interest proceeds: $3.8B**, likely subsidiary-level financing for infrastructure or Other Bets.
- **Dividends: $5.2B**, up **5.1%**, with the quarterly dividend raised to **$0.22** from **$0.21**.

### 7.2 Why this matters for shareholders

- **Dilution is back.** Shares outstanding rose from **12.088B to 12.230B** in H1, a **1.17%** increase. With SBC running at **$15.2B** in H1 and buybacks paused, share count will likely continue to drift higher.
- **Preferred capital is expensive.** The **$86M** preferred dividend in Q2, for a security issued on June 5, implies a relatively high annualized coupon if the payment is prorated. The exact terms were not in the extract, but the issuance signals that management wants capital without further near-term common dilution — or is funding a specific large transaction.
- **Dividend yield remains negligible.** At the implied ~$215 issuance price, the annualized **$0.88** dividend yields only **~0.41%**. This is not an income story.
- **Capital return has collapsed.** Total common capital returned fell from **$33.3B in H1 2025** to **$5.2B in H1 2026**, an **84% decline**.

**Trading implication:** The market has historically valued Alphabet partly on its buyback-driven EPS growth. That support has disappeared in 2026. EPS growth must now come from operating leverage, not share-count reduction — and the new preferred dividend is a senior claim on earnings.

## 8. Segment and operational commentary

Because segment-level 2026 data was not surfaced, the following is a qualitative assessment based on the consolidated numbers and business structure.

### Google Services
The consolidated revenue acceleration to **24% YoY** suggests Search and YouTube remain resilient despite the rise of AI chatbots. Gross margin expansion is consistent with a favorable mix toward higher-margin search/YouTube advertising and subscription revenue. The key debate is whether AI Overviews and AI Mode can monetize at similar or better rates than traditional search results, and whether query share is holding against generative-AI competitors.

### Google Cloud
Cloud is the most likely beneficiary of the AI infrastructure build. Deferred revenue of **$10.1B**, up **17.4%** from year-end, and the massive capex program point to strong enterprise AI demand. However, without segment disclosure, we cannot quantify Cloud growth or margin. Watch Cloud revenue growth and operating margin at Q3.

### Other Bets
Waymo remains the most valuable Other Bet, with potential robotaxi optionality. Other Bets are likely still loss-making, but the consolidated numbers do not isolate their drag.

### AI infrastructure
The **$80.6B** H1 capex program is overwhelmingly AI/data-center related. Alphabet’s vertical integration — TPUs, data centers, models, and distribution — is a structural advantage, but it also concentrates risk. The company is effectively making a multi-hundred-billion-dollar bet that AI demand will scale faster than depreciation and competition.

5. **Monitor dilution as a sell signal.** If Q3 shows another large equity/preferred issuance or a further pause in buybacks, reduce exposure to per-share growth.
6. **Relative-value angle.** GOOGL’s balance sheet and core growth compare favorably with more levered AI-capex peers. A pair trade long GOOGL / short a basket of highly levered AI-infrastructure names could isolate Alphabet’s operating momentum while hedging the capex cycle.
7. **Do not extrapolate the equity gains.** The $136B H1 gain is non-cash and illiquid. Any model that capitalizes it is wrong. Conversely, a reversal would crush reported EPS without affecting cash flow — a potential mispricing opportunity.

### 11.4 What would change the view

## 12. Bottom line

Alphabet’s **core business is performing exceptionally well**: revenue is accelerating at **23–24%**, gross margins are expanding, and operating income is growing **30%**. The balance sheet remains one of the strongest in global equities, with **$242B of cash and investments** and only **~0.5x debt/EBITDA**.

However, **2026 is a year of aggressive reinvestment and financial engineering**. Capex has more than doubled, free cash flow has collapsed, buybacks are paused, and the company has raised over **$100B** of external capital across debt, common equity, and convertible preferred. Reported earnings are heavily distorted by **$136B** of non-cash equity marks, making headline valuation metrics unreliable.

**The trade is therefore nuanced:** be constructive on Alphabet’s core operating momentum and fortress balance sheet, but demand evidence that the capex cycle is peaking before paying a full multiple for the AI story. The Q3 2026 report — particularly **capex guidance, Cloud growth, operating margin, and free cash flow** — is the key near-term catalyst.
