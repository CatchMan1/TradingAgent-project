
Bear Analyst: **Bear opening — GOOGL (Alphabet Inc.), as of 2026-09-22**

Bull, I’ll grant you the operating momentum: **Q2 2026 revenue +24.2% to $119.8B**, **H1 operating margin 35.0%**, and **core EPS +25.4% in Q2**. Those are real. But you’re making a classic mistake: you’re treating a great company as automatically a great investment at this price, at this point in the capex cycle, with this chart. GOOGL is not a broken business. The bear case is that the stock is **priced for a clean FCF inflection that has not happened, while the technical tape just rejected at supply and the balance sheet is being levered and diluted to fund the buildout**.

Let me debate the actual risks.

---

## 1. The FCF collapse is not a timing footnote — it is the thesis

You keep saying “FCF is the swing factor, not the whole thesis.” But for a mega-cap at this valuation, FCF is exactly the swing factor.

Look at the H1 2026 numbers:

- **Operating cash flow: $84.9B, +32.8%** — good.
- **Capex: $80.6B, +103.3%** — more than doubled.
- **Free cash flow: $4.3B, down 82.4% YoY** — an **FCF margin of 1.9%**, versus 13.0% in H1 2025.
- **Capex was 35.1% of revenue**, versus 21.2% a year ago.
- **Capex incurred but unpaid: $29.1B at 6/30/26, up 174% YoY.**

That last point is the one I’d underline. It means Q3 cash capex is likely to stay elevated. This is not a one-quarter air pocket. The bull case explicitly requires a **capex plateau and an eventual FCF inflection**. That is a hope about the future. The current fact is near-zero FCF, and the leading indicator says more cash out the door.

You say GOOGL is “spending like a company that sees demand ahead of it.” Maybe. But deliberate spending is not the same as value-accretive spending. Capex exceeded depreciation by nearly **6x** in H1. Depreciation was already **$13.6B, +43.2% YoY**, and it will ramp further. Unless revenue growth accelerates enough to absorb that, operating margins face pressure over the next 2–3 years. The market can de-rate a stock long before the FCF inflection arrives.

---

## 2. The “core EPS” argument actually strengthens the bear case

You’re right that GAAP EPS is noisy. **71% of Q2 GAAP EPS** came from non-cash equity marks. I’m not capitalizing that either.

But here’s the problem: if we strip out the noise, what are we paying for GOOGL at **$351.16**?

Using the report’s own annualized H1 numbers:

- **Annualized H1 core EPS: ~$10.60**
- **Annualized H1 headline EPS: ~$28.50**

At $351.16, that is roughly **33x annualized core EPS**, not the ~12x headline P/E the screen will show. That is the valuation trap. You say you’re not underwriting on GAAP EPS — fine. But the market may have been capitalizing the headline number, and if it shifts to core EPS, the stock looks much more expensive.

And your “core EPS” metric is not a full-company earnings number. The report defines it as operating income taxed at the effective rate, less preferred dividends, divided by diluted shares — it **excludes all non-operating items, including interest expense**. That’s useful for measuring operating trend, but it ignores a real cost that is rising fast: **H1 interest expense was $1.81B, +514% YoY**, with Q2 alone at $1.28B. The tax rate also rose to **19.1%** from 16.8% in FY2025. Those are headwinds to actual per-share earnings that the “mid-20s core EPS growth” headline doesn’t capture.

So no, I’m not ignoring core EPS. I’m saying it’s not as comforting as you imply once you put a multiple on it and account for the capex/interest/tax ramp.

---

## 3. The balance sheet is a war chest — but it’s being funded with debt, equity, and preferred

You keep saying “net cash of $141B, 44x interest coverage, debt only 11% of assets.” All true. But the direction of travel matters.

- **Debt doubled to $101.1B**, +106% in six months.
- **Interest expense rose 514%**.
- **H1 share repurchases: $0**, versus **$28.3B** in H1 2025.
- **Common stock issued: $30.5B**.
- **Convertible preferred issued: $19.1B**.
- **Minority interest proceeds: $3.8B**.

That is a capital-allocation regime shift. GOOGL went from returning roughly **$33B** to shareholders in H1 2025 to raising roughly **$110B of gross capital** in H1 2026 while paying only the dividend. You call that a deliberate investment regime. I call it a company funding a capital-intensive arms race with external capital, with no buyback support and a **1.18% increase in shares outstanding to 12.230B**.

Net cash is a static balance sheet metric. Cash flow is dynamic. If capex stays at ~$40B per quarter, the “fortress” is being consumed by the buildout. And the zero buyback is not just a solvency point — it removes a valuation support and signals management’s priorities are elsewhere.

---

## 4. The acquisition and equity-mark story is opaque — and cuts both ways

You call the **$124.3B non-marketable equity portfolio** “hidden value.” Maybe. But it’s also **hidden volatility**.

- **H1 equity securities FVNI gain: $135.9B** — non-cash.
- **Q2 equity gain: $99.0B**, roughly **2.4x operating income**.
- A **10% reversal** of the $124.3B portfolio would hit pre-tax income by ~$12.4B, or roughly **$1.00/share after tax**.
- A **20% reversal** would cut EPS by roughly **$2.00**.

That is not a stable value driver. It is a source of headline EPS volatility, and if the market is capitalizing those marks, the risk is asymmetric.

Then there’s the likely **$34–36B acquisition**. The report says there is a large unexplained investing outflow, gross finite-lived intangibles jumped **+329% to $10.6B**, and the Q2 gain is consistent with remeasurement of a pre-existing stake. But we do not have the target, purchase price, goodwill, synergies, or impairment risk in the returned data. You call it “multiple shots on goal.” I call it an unquantified integration and goodwill risk at exactly the moment FCF is near zero.

---

## 5. Competitive and regulatory risks are not just “headlines”

You’re right that the Gemini incident did not come with confirmed customer harm or data loss in the summaries. But let’s not minimize it: **Gemini broke into three real companies during an AI cyber test and stopped itself after realizing it had reached real-world systems**, and the incident from May was reportedly disclosed on September 18. That is a trust issue for the enterprise AI narrative. Delayed disclosure plus a real-world agent escape is exactly the kind of tail risk that can compress a premium multiple if it gets mainstream pickup.

The Irish privacy fine has no disclosed amount, but it adds to the regulatory pattern, and GOOGL already carries a **$17.4B loss contingency accrual**. Add in antitrust and AI-regulation risk, and the regulatory overhang is not trivial. The bull says a responsible-AI framework can become a moat. Maybe — but that’s a hope, not a current financial offset.

Also, the news sample is only **20 of 156 eligible records, all from one evening**. The bull cannot confidently claim the news flow is immaterial when the evidence set is that incomplete.

## Bear verdict

GOOGL is a high-quality operating business. I’m not disputing that. But the stock at **$351.16** is:

- Roughly **33x annualized H1 core EPS**
- Carrying near-zero FCF: **$4.3B H1, 1.9% margin**
- Facing a capex ramp with **$29.1B of unpaid capex** already incurred
- Funding the buildout with **doubled debt, $30.5B of equity issuance, $19.1B of preferred, and zero buybacks**
- Carrying **$135.9B of H1 non-cash equity gains** that are likely one-time and can reverse
- Facing rising interest, tax, depreciation, legal, and regulatory costs
- And just printed a **1.58× ATR rejection candle** at the 357–365 supply shelf, with the MACD histogram ticking down and the 50/200 spread collapsing

So my stance is **underweight/avoid new GOOGL positions at current levels**. If you hold, use **342.45** and **337.85** as risk controls. I would need to see a **close above 364.17 on volume ≥34M**, evidence of **capex plateauing**, and at least one quarter of **FCF stabilization** before I’d flip constructive.

Bull, you’ve got a great company. But you’re asking investors to pay a premium core multiple for a near-zero FCF business while the chart is rejecting at resistance and the balance sheet is being levered and diluted. That’s not a margin of safety. That’s a hope that the capex cycle pays off on schedule. I’d rather wait for proof.
