## 4. Now the bear's three punches — and why they miss

**Bear punch #1: "Free cash flow collapsed 82%, to $4.3B from $24.3B. Capex is 35% of revenue."**

Guilty. And it's the *right decision.* Capex went from $39.6B to $80.6B in H1 while **operating cash flow rose to $84.9B.** The company is converting nearly all incremental operating cash into AI capacity — and the payoff is showing up *now*, not in 2029: gross margin +220 bp and operating margin +160 bp **in the same six months that depreciation rose 43% and SBC rose 33%.** That is the ROI proof. If the capex were value-destructive, margins would have compressed while depreciation ramped. They expanded.

Moreover, the bear's own number argues against them: **capex incurred but not yet paid jumped to $29.1B from $10.6B.** That's committed spend already contracted — meaning the revenue-generating capacity is being built with a lag, not wasted. And critically, **FCF is not the binding constraint here:** GOOGL holds **$242.5B in cash + short-term investments** against **$102.0B face debt** — a **net cash position of roughly +$140B**, up from ~+$78B at year-end, with interest coverage above 20× on operating income. Penalizing GOOGL for deploying a fortress balance sheet into a 24%-growth AI build is penalizing it for having the optionality competitors lack.

**Bear punch #2: "Debt doubled, buybacks went to zero, they issued $30.5B of common and $19.1B of convertible preferred — 1.2% dilution. The capital-return regime is broken."**

Let's get the scale right. **142M shares added against 12,088M — 1.2% dilution — while revenue grew 24.2% and operating income grew 30.4%.** Dilution of 1.2% against ~30% operating profit growth is not a value transfer; it's rounding error in service of the largest secular capex cycle in the company's history. And note the *quality* of the raise: the common was issued into a stock $373.51 and within 7.2% of its all-time high — you raise equity when your currency is dear, not when you're desperate. The convertible preferred adds a cheap layer of capital without touching the share count on day one.

On buybacks: zero in H1 does not mean zero forever. It means capital allocation was triaged toward a once-in-a-generation infrastructure build. **The buyback is a call option on the capex cycle peaking** — and when it does, $80B+ of annualized operating cash flow with a decelerating capex line is a monstrous FCF return story. The bear is measuring a transitional period as if it were a terminal state.

**Bear punch #3: "Headline EPS of $9.11 is junk — ~71% of Q2 pre-tax income was non-operating marks. Core EPS is only ~$2.65–2.70. The P/E is a mirage."**

I'll accept this correction enthusiastically, because **the bear just handed me my own valuation argument.** Yes — strip out the $97,983M non-operating line and Q2 core EPS is roughly **$2.65–2.70, up 15–17% YoY.** That's the *honest* growth rate on the operating business, and it's excellent for a company this size.

But the bear's framing has a symmetry problem. If the marks are "fake," the bear must also explain why the assets behind them aren't real. They are: the **private equity book is carried at $124.3B** (up from $64.1B), and the **AFS debt portfolio is $99.5B with an accumulated unrealized loss of just $416M** — i.e., essentially no duration or credit impairment. The marks are non-cash and reversible, yes. They're also backed by a genuine private-AI revaluation cycle and a $22.8B deferred tax liability that proves the gains are being taken seriously by the tax code.

And here's the kicker for the bear: **if the market is allegedly mispricing GOOGL off a fake $14.24 of H1 EPS, that mispricing has an obvious resolution — and it cuts toward owning the stock, not shorting it.** When Q3 marks normalize (up or down), the *operating* line keeps compounding at +30%. That's the durable engine.

**Bonus bear punch: "Reddit says Google Search referrals were 'choppy.'"**

One partner's CEO describing *its own* referral traffic while explicitly pivoting users to its own app is not evidence of a Search franchise in decline — it's evidence of a publisher's distribution strategy. If Search were deteriorating, we would not be looking at **+24.2% total revenue growth and +220 bp of gross margin expansion in the same period.** The datapoint is worth monitoring on a multi-quarter horizon; it is not a reason to be positioned against a company compounding operating income at 30%.
