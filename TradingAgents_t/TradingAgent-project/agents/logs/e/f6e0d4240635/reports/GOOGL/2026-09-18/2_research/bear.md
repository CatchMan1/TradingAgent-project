
Bear Analyst: **Bear opening: GOOGL — the operating story is real; the investment case at this price is not**

Bull, I’ll concede the easy part: GOOGL’s operating momentum is impressive. Q2 revenue +24.2%, gross margin +210bp, operating income +30.4%, core EPS +27% — that is not a broken business. But your recommendation is not “own the operating business for a decade.” It is “buy GOOGL around 344 with a stop at 336.90 after a 1.9x-volume rejection at the upper Bollinger band, inside a mid-range coil, with negative free cash flow and a ~33x core P/E.” That is where the bull case breaks down. The equity story has three new problems — **cash conversion, earnings quality, and distribution** — and none of them are fixed by a strong revenue print.

Let me take your points one by one.

### 1) “Growth is accelerating” — true, but you’re ignoring what’s happening below the revenue line

You’re right that revenue accelerated to +24.2% in Q2 and H1 core EPS rose ~27%. But look at the sequence:

- Q1 2026 operating margin: **36.1%**
- Q2 2026 operating margin: **34.0%**
- Sequential decline: **210bp**
- Q2 opex growth: **+26.7%** vs revenue **+24.2%**
- H1 SBC: **$15.2B, +32%** — faster than revenue
- H1 interest expense: **$1.81B, +514% YoY**
- H1 effective tax rate: **19.1%, +200bp YoY**

So the “operating engine” is still growing, but the margin trajectory has already rolled over sequentially, and the cost lines are moving the wrong way. You’re leaning on gross margin expansion, but capex is capitalized — depreciation is what eventually hits gross margin. Depreciation rose **+43%** in H1 while capex rose **+103%**. The D&A wave has not fully arrived yet. That is the key second-derivative risk for 2027 EPS, and it is not in the current margin print.

### 2) Free cash flow is not just “a cycle” — it is a change in the equity story

You say Q2 FCF of **–$5.9B** is just an aggressive AI build. I say the market prices cash flow, and the cash flow trend has broken:

- H1 operating cash flow: **$84.9B, +32.8%**
- H1 capex: **$80.6B, +103.3%**
- H1 free cash flow: **$4.3B, down 82.4% YoY**
- Q2 free cash flow: **–$5.9B**
- Capex/revenue: **35.1%**, with ~$29.1B of capex incurred but unpaid at 6/30
- Annualized capex run-rate: roughly **$160–180B**

And the capital-return signal has inverted: buybacks went from **$28.3B in H1 2025 to zero** in H1 2026, while GOOGL raised roughly **$105B of gross capital** — $56.2B debt, $30.5B common stock, $19.1B convertible preferred, $3.8B minority. Diluted shares are rising, not shrinking. That means no EPS tailwind from repurchases, plus dilution. Debt doubled to **$101.1B** and interest expense is no longer trivial. The $141B net cash cushion is real, but it is shrinking as the buildout is debt- and equity-funded.

You frame capex as “the price of owning the next compute layer.” Maybe. But investors don’t get paid in compute layers — they get paid in free cash flow. Right now GOOGL is converting a fantastic operating result into almost no FCF.

### 3) Earnings quality: your own “use core EPS” argument cuts against the stock

I agree with you that headline EPS is misleading. H1 GAAP EPS was **$14.24**, but H1 pretax income included **$135.7B of non-operating income**, about **62.8% of pretax**. Q2 headline EPS was **$9.11** versus core EPS of roughly **$2.68** — headline overstates run-rate by about **2.7x**. There is also a **$22.8B deferred tax liability** tied to unrealized investment gains. If those marks reverse, GAAP EPS can fall violently independent of operating performance.

But here is the problem for the bull case: if we use core EPS, GOOGL is not cheap. Annualized H1 core EPS is roughly **$10.6**. At the 2026-09-18 close of **$349.54**, that is roughly **33x core earnings**. You are paying a growth multiple for a company with negative Q2 FCF, zero buybacks, rising dilution, doubling debt, and a capex program that is about to drive depreciation higher. That is not a margin-of-safety setup. That is a “everything must go right” setup.

### 4) The regulatory “relief” is a headline, not a quantified fact

We do not have the remedy term, scope, compliance mechanics, product-architecture requirements, monetary exposure, or appeal status. A behavioral remedy that forces separation of buy-side and sell-side tooling could still compress ad-tech take rates over multiple quarters. And GOOGL already carries **$17.4B of current legal/regulatory accruals**, up $1.8B in six months. That is not a clean slate — that is an overhang that keeps growing.

Also, look at the tape. The court ruling and the after-close upgrade were both fresh inputs into the 2026-09-18 session. GOOGL gapped up **2.87%** to 357.31, traded to 359.44, then closed at 349.54 — below the prior day’s high and in the bottom **9.9%** of the day’s range on **47.63M** shares, about **1.9x** the 20-day average. That is what “good news sold” looks like. If the market couldn’t hold the gap on “behavioral, not structural” plus a buy upgrade, what happens when the FCF and depreciation realities get more attention?
