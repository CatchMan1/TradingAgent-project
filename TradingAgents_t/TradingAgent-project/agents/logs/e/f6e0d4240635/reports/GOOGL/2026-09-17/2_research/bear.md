
Bear Analyst: **Bear case: GOOGL at $347.33 is a good company — but not a good risk/reward here.**

## 1. The bull’s “core earnings” argument actually proves the stock is expensive

You’re right that headline EPS is distorted. GOOGL’s H1 non-operating income was **$135.7B**, about **63% of pretax income**. Q2 non-operating income was **$98.0B** versus operating income of **$40.8B**. Headline Q2 EPS of **$9.11** versus core EPS of roughly **$2.68** is not a clean number — agreed.

But when you strip out the marks and value GOOGL on core earnings, the valuation is not cheap. Annualized core EPS is about **$10.6**. At **$347.33**, that’s roughly **32.8x core earnings** — for a company that just printed **negative Q2 free cash flow of ~$5.9B**. And using the fundamental report’s own NOPAT scaffolding: at **30x NOPAT**, GOOGL is worth roughly **$328/share**; at **25x**, roughly **$275**; at **20x**, roughly **$221**. The current price is already above the 30x NOPAT math. So the bull case requires either multiple expansion from here or a very fast FCF recovery. That’s not a margin of safety — that’s a leap of faith.

## 2. The FCF problem is not backward-looking — it’s the forward warning

Bull, you said the FCF panic is “backwards-looking.” No. Look at the forward signals:

- H1 capex was **$80.6B**, or **35.1% of revenue**, up from **21.2%** a year ago.
- H1 FCF collapsed to **$4.3B** from **$24.3B**, down **82%**.
- Q2 FCF was **negative ~$5.9B**.
- **Capex incurred but unpaid** jumped to **$29.1B** from **$10.6B** — that is a direct warning that Q3 cash capex is still coming.

You said operating cash flow was “more than enough” to cover capex. In H1, OCF of **$84.9B** covered **$80.6B** of capex by only **$4.3B**. In Q2, OCF was **$39.1B** against **$44.9B** of capex — it did not cover it. That is not a fortress self-funding machine; that is a company that has temporarily become cash-constrained after capex and is now issuing debt, equity, and preferred to keep the build going. Buybacks went from **$28.3B** in H1 2025 to **zero**. That is a capital-return model that has been put on pause — and the bull is asking you to pay a premium while you wait for it to come back.

## 3. “Net cash” doesn’t erase the debt and dilution signal

Yes, GOOGL still has roughly **$123B of net cash** after **$101.1B of debt** and **$18.0B of convertible preferred**. It is not a solvency story. But the direction of travel matters:

- Total debt nearly doubled to **$101.1B** in six months.
- Interest expense rose from **$0.30B to $1.81B**, up over **500%**.
- GOOGL issued **$30.5B of common stock** and **$19.1B of convertible preferred** in H1.
- Deferred tax liabilities jumped to **$22.8B** from **$0.9B**, largely the footprint of unrealized investment gains.
- The legal/loss contingency sits at **$17.4B** — an unresolved antitrust/regulatory overhang.

Bull says “net cash means no leverage stress.” Fine. But the stock is not pricing leverage stress; it’s pricing flawless AI execution. And the **$22B Crux AI financing** headline cuts both ways. Without recourse, guarantees, pricing, or Alphabet ownership details, it validates capital access — but it also feeds the “AI debt/overbuild” narrative. Apollo’s Torsten Slok warning that hyperscaler debt stress resembles 2008 housing signals is not company-specific proof, but it is exactly the kind of macro/credit headline that can compress multiples across the AI complex. Add the **25bp Fed hike to 3.75%–4.00%** — the first since 2023 — and you have a discount-rate headwind for a 32x core-earnings stock.
