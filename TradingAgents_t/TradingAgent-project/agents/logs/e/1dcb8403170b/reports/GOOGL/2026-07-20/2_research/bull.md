
Bull Analyst: **Bull Analyst opening case — GOOGL**

Let’s not bury the lede: **I’m a buyer of GOOGL here, and I think the bear case is mistaking a sharp correction for a broken thesis.** The tape is choppy and the next catalyst is binary — Q2 earnings on July 22 — so I’m not going to pretend the stock is in a perfect technical setup. But the operating business is accelerating, the balance sheet still has real firepower, and the primary trend is intact. The bear can wave the MACD at me; I’m going to make them explain why a company growing revenue **21.8%** at a **36.1% operating margin** with a **$467.6B backlog** should be sold after a 12% drawdown.

### 1. Growth potential: this is not a slowing mega-cap

GOOGL’s Q1 FY2026 numbers are the core of my case:

- Revenue: **$109.896B, +21.8% YoY**
- Operating income: **$39.696B, +29.7% YoY**
- Operating margin: **36.1% vs 33.9%**
- Gross margin: **62.5% vs 59.7%**

That is the combination bulls dream about: **growth accelerating while margins expand**. Revenue rose 21.8%, but operating income rose 29.7% — real operating leverage, even with R&D up 25.6% and depreciation up 44.5%. That tells me the AI/ad/cloud up-cycle is not just a narrative; it is showing up in the P&L.

And the forward visibility is exceptional. GOOGL disclosed **remaining performance obligations of $467.6B**, roughly **4.3x quarterly revenue**. That is contracted, multi-year revenue cover. Bears can argue backlog quality, but a backlog of that size in an AI/cloud cycle is a competitive weapon — it gives GOOGL visibility to keep investing while smaller players have to pull back.

One more clean-collection point the bear should not skip: accounts receivable was **$62.999B vs $62.886B** at year-end — essentially flat while revenue grew 21.8%. That means days sales outstanding is falling. This growth is being collected, not financed through receivables.

### 2. Competitive advantages: scale, distribution, and an AI stack that is already monetizing

GOOGL’s moat is not one model or one product. It is the combination of **Search, YouTube, Android, Chrome, Cloud, Workspace, Gemini, and a custom infrastructure stack including TPUs**. Very few companies can distribute AI across billions of users *and* sell enterprise compute *and* fund the capex internally.

The bear wants to frame the Gemini 3.5 Pro delay as “Alphabet is falling behind.” I take that headline seriously — a delayed flagship model into earnings is a real sentiment negative. But it is a product-cadence risk, not a franchise break. Alphabet’s AI story is broader than one launch date. The consolidated numbers show monetization already happening: **22% revenue growth**, **36% operating margin**, and a **$468B backlog**. A company that is “falling behind” does not usually post those numbers simultaneously.

I also don’t need to rely on the truncated BofA “$80B” item — I don’t know what it is, and I won’t build a thesis on it. My case stands on operating income, normalized EPS, and backlog. BofA reiterating constructive into the print is a mild positive, and Berkshire’s ~$31B Alphabet stake is a confidence signal, but I’m not calling either a fundamental catalyst. The real catalyst is the business conversion.

### 3. Positive indicators: the primary trend is still up

The technical report is bearish short-term — I concede that:

- MACD **−2.38 vs signal −2.12**, fresh bearish cross
- Price below the 10 EMA **356.20**, VWMA **358.74**, and 50 SMA **369.67**
- Rallies on lighter volume: **24.7M** on the 7/20 bounce vs **41.2M** on the 7/16 drop
- ATR expanded to **11.78**, so volatility is high

That is why I’m scaling, not chasing. But the bear is over-reading the intermediate tape. The primary trend is intact:

- 200-day SMA: **322.20 and rising every session**
- Price is still **~9.2% above** the 200-day
- The 50-day remains above the 200-day — the golden-cross structure from the 2025 rally is still in place
- GOOGL went from **175.84** in July 2025 to **351.99** now — roughly a double — and the current drawdown from the May peak is **−12.6%**

More importantly, the structure is not a clean breakdown. We have **lower highs** from 402.62 to 373.25 to 370.92, but also **higher lows** from 330.20 to 341.36. That is a contracting range — a coil. The 6/26 capitulation low at 330.20 came on **114.7M shares**; that is a demand shelf. The 7/17 low at 341.36 held above it. RSI also made a higher low: **42.23 on 7/17 vs 33.27 on 6/26**. That is not a bull signal by itself, but it supports the idea that selling pressure is being absorbed.

The triggers are clear: a close above **358.74** on volume above 30M would reclaim VWMA; a close above **375.27** would neutralize the 7/16 distribution candle and put **369.67–372.33**, then the May highs, back in play. I want to own GOOGL before those triggers, not after, because the fundamental catalyst is days away.

### 4. Bear counterpoints — let me take them head-on

**Bear: “The chart is broken — below the 50-day, MACD just crossed down.”**  
Fair tactically. But a 12.6% correction inside a rising 200-day trend is not a broken thesis. The stock is still above a steeply rising 200-day SMA, the 50-day is above the 200-day, and the recent low is higher than the June low. I’m using the weakness. If GOOGL closes below **341.36**, then **337.87**, then **330.20**, I’ll reassess. Until then, the base case is a range with an upward fundamental catalyst, not a trend reversal.

**Bear: “EPS is inflated by $36.8B of securities gains.”**  
Correct — and I’m not using the $5.11 headline. Reported EPS is roughly half non-operating marks. But strip those out: normalized EPS is about **$2.68 vs ~$2.14 a year ago, +25%**. Operating income rose **29.7%**. The bear is attacking a GAAP number I’m not defending. My valuation anchor is normalized earnings, and normalized earnings are still growing at a premium rate.

**Bear: “Capex is crushing FCF — $35.7B in one quarter, FCF down 46.6%.”**  
This is the most serious bear point, and I won’t dismiss it. Q1 FCF was **$10.116B**, a **9.2% margin** vs 21.0% a year ago. But capex is **32.5% of revenue** because GOOGL is building AI infrastructure against visible demand — a **$467.6B RPO** and 21.8% revenue growth. The balance sheet can fund it: **$126.8B cash + short-term investments** against **$80.3B gross debt**, still about **$46.5B net cash**, with a current ratio near **2.0x** and long-dated debt — **$36.1B beyond year five**. Interest expense jumped to **$533M**, but that is still small against **$39.7B** of quarterly operating income.

Would I like buybacks back? Yes. Zero repurchases vs $15.1B a year ago is a real per-share headwind. But a temporary pause to fund an AI buildout with a 36% operating margin and a $468B backlog is a defensible capital-allocation choice. If Q2 capex guide comes in above **$160B** with FCF below **$60B** and no buyback signal, I’ll tighten. But the current evidence says investment, not distress.

**Bear: “AI bubble, risk-off tape, Iran, Fear & Greed.”**  
Macro risk is real. I’m not ignoring it. But in a risk-off tape, I want the mega-cap with **net cash**, **36% operating margins**, **22% revenue growth**, and a **$468B backlog** — not the unprofitable AI long tail. GOOGL is the quality-with-growth expression. I’ll size for the **ATR of 11.78** and the July 22 event, but I want exposure.

### 5. My recommendation

**GOOGL — Buy/Overweight.** I would accumulate in the **341–352** zone, add on a close above **358.74** with volume above 30M, and add again above **375.27**. I’d use a tactical risk line under **341.36**, with a deeper thesis-invalidation level near **330.20/322.20** on the 200-day.

So, bear, here’s my challenge: you can point to the MACD cross and the light-volume bounce, and I’ll agree the near-term tape is soft. But you have to explain why GOOGL should be sold — not just “not chased” — when the business is growing revenue **21.8%**, expanding operating margins to **36.1%**, generating **25% normalized EPS growth**, sitting on **$46.5B net cash**, and carrying a **$467.6B backlog** into a July 22 catalyst. That is a much harder case to make than pointing at a 12% correction in a stock that has roughly doubled in a year. I’m on the bull side of GOOGL.
