One strong session after an after-hours slide does not prove the market has fully priced a regulatory and supply-chain issue involving CXMT and YMTC on a Pentagon blacklist. The 75.4 million share rally and the reclaim of the 50-SMA at $293.52 and 10-EMA at $293.59 are real positives, and RSI at 60.26 leaves room before overbought, but AAPL is still pressing into the $312–$317 supply shelf with the MACD line at −0.686 below zero. So the aggressive analyst is overstating the “failed catalyst” point and understating the risk of buying directly into resistance with gap risk.

At the same time, the conservative analyst is too close to a blanket no-buy. He is correct that the 12.2% five-session bounce, upper Bollinger at $312.31, June highs at $315.45 and $316.94, ATR of 8.74, and unresolved China headline risk argue against a full 0.75x chase. He is also right that a stop at $288.50 does not protect against a headline gap through it. But demanding either a confirmed hold of $290–$295 or a decisive close above $317 before doing anything risks underparticipating in a name where the primary trend is still intact, with AAPL above a rising 50-SMA at $293.52 and 200-SMA at $270.69, and where the fundamental backdrop is not deteriorating. H1 FY2026 revenue growth of 16.1%, Q2 gross margin of 49.3%, H1 EPS of $4.85, operating cash flow of $82.6 billion up 53.3%, and roughly $62 billion of net cash are supportive. The conservative analyst is right that fundamentals are not a timing tool, but they are a reason not to treat AAPL as a speculative bounce that must be avoided entirely. His 0.25x–0.35x starter may be too small if the research plan is genuinely overweight and the long-term structure remains bullish.

The balanced adjustment is not to split the difference mechanically. It is to separate the direction from the execution. Directionally, I would keep a constructive or overweight bias on AAPL, but I would not execute the full 0.75x starter at $308.63 into the $312–$317 shelf. I would reduce the initial tranche to roughly 0.40x–0.50x of standard single-name allocation, sized so that both the $288.50 stop and a gap-through-stop scenario are tolerable for the caller’s own portfolio. That gives the aggressive analyst the participation he wants if AAPL is turning, while giving the conservative analyst the smaller first tranche and gap-risk protection he is asking for.

Then I would reserve the rest of the planned AAPL exposure for the two triggers the trader already identified. Add roughly 0.25x only if AAPL pulls back into the $290–$295 confluence and holds it with the MACD histogram staying positive. Add another 0.25x–0.35x only on a decisive close above $317 with volume follow-through and the MACD line crossing above zero. Until those happen, cap total exposure below the full 1.20x–1.25x target, maybe around 1.00x–1.10x at most. Keep the $288.50 stop for the starter, cancel adds on a close below $290, and reassess the structural thesis below $275. If the caller already owns AAPL, I would not add at $308.63 unless underweight and comfortable with the smaller tranche; if there is a tactical overweight, trimming into $312–$317 is reasonable, but I would not abandon the core position solely on the China headline.

So my neutral recommendation is still constructive on AAPL, but the execution should be a scaled, conditional buy rather than an immediate 0.75x market chase. The aggressive case is too confident that the market has already dismissed the memory-supplier risk, while the conservative case is too cautious given the intact 50>200 structure, improving momentum, and strong fundamentals. A smaller AAPL starter now, with explicit add triggers at either a held $290–$295 pullback or a confirmed close above $317, is the more sustainable balance between growth potential and volatility protection.
