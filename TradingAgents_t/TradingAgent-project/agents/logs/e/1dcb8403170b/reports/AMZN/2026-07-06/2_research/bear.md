## 1. You’re Calling Negative FCF a “Choice” — the Market Will Call It a Risk

You framed the capex super-cycle as the entire bull thesis. I’ll grant the operating engine is real: Q1 revenue **+16.6%**, operating income **+29.6%**, operating margin **13.1%**. That is strong.

But here’s what you’re glossing over: **TTM free cash flow is roughly −$2.47B**. Capex was **$151.0B** against operating cash flow of **$148.5B**. And the trend is ugly: FCF went **$32.2B → $32.9B → $7.7B → negative**. Q1 capex alone was **$44.2B, +77% YoY**.

You say that’s “discretionary growth capex.” Fine — but discretionary capex still consumes cash, and this time it’s increasingly debt-funded. Long-term debt rose **+78% QoQ**, up roughly **$53.4B in a single quarter**. Interest expense rose **+48% YoY**. D&A is already **$70.4B TTM** and still below capex, meaning the depreciation drag is building, not fading. Acquisitions added another **$15.4B** of cash use in Q1.

So no, I’m not calling AMZN distressed. I’m calling it a company that has flipped from FCF machine to FCF-compressed, debt-funded infrastructure buildout — and the market is being asked to underwrite the payoff before the evidence shows AWS reaccelerating enough to justify it. The 2018–2020 capex analogy is a hope, not a data point.

## 2. The EPS “Noise” Matters More Than You Admit

You said ignore the headline EPS because of the **$12.3B non-cash equity mark-up**. I agree it’s non-cash — but you can’t have it both ways. You cite the headline net income growth as part of the bull case, then dismiss the mark-up when I challenge it.

The facts: non-operating income was **$15.98B** versus **$3.27B** a year ago. Excluding the excess mark-up, core net income growth is closer to **~20%**, not the headline **+76.6%**. If the next quarter lacks a similar mark-to-market gain, headline EPS decelerates optically. The market may eventually look through it, but it often doesn’t wait around while the optics turn.

The clean leg of the bull case is the operating margin. I’ll concede that. But an operating margin that still produces negative FCF is not enough by itself to justify chasing **AMZN** into resistance.
