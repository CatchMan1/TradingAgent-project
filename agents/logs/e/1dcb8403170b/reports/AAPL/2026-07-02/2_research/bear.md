You say RSI at **60.26** has room to 70. True — but RSI just came from **32.2**, and it can roll over at 60 just as easily in a lower-high structure. You say the MACD histogram flipped positive to **+0.261**. Also true — but the MACD line is still **−0.686**, below zero. That is an early countertrend cross, not a confirmed trend signal.

And the risk/reward for a new buyer here is poor:

- Upside to the **$312–$317** resistance shelf: roughly **1–3%**.
- Downside to the **$290–$295** support confluence: roughly **4–6%**.
- Downside to the **$275** structural low: roughly **11%**.

That is not asymmetric in the bull’s favor. You say “a checkpoint is not a ceiling.” Fair. But until AAPL closes decisively above **$317**, the burden of proof is on the bulls, not the bears.

You also lean on volume. The **June 26 print of 261.8M shares** was almost certainly tied to quarter-end/Russell reconstitution, not organic accumulation. ### 2. The China memory story is not a side issue — it is a lose-lose near-term overhang

Bull, you minimize the **CXMT/YMTC** story by saying it is “negotiations,” not a done deal. That cuts both ways.

If AAPL does source memory from Pentagon-blacklisted Chinese suppliers, it invites U.S. political, regulatory, and reputational risk. The report itself notes Apple is lobbying Washington on this, and the stock slid after-hours. That is the market’s first read. If AAPL does not complete the arrangement, the memory crunch persists — and that crunch has already forced rare, widespread price hikes across the product lineup.

You call the price hikes a sign of pricing power. I call them a potential demand-destruction risk. Raising prices protects revenue per unit in the short term, but it can weaken unit volumes, stretch upgrade cycles, and damage the affordability of Apple’s hardware franchise. And the margin data you cite — **Q2 gross margin 49.3%** — is backward-looking. Memory costs can lag, and contracted supply can reset. You are extrapolating a favorable H1 margin into a future where input costs are a live threat.

You also say the market “bought anyway” on July 2. One regular session after an after-hours slide is not a regulatory all-clear. It may simply be short covering and oversold mean reversion. Regulatory and political risks take weeks or months to price. If Washington pushes back, or if the blacklist issue escalates, the July 2 candle will look like a lower high — not a validation.

And the broader regulatory backdrop is not friendly. The Google EU antitrust ruling has no direct AAPL link in the evidence, but it reinforces a more assertive EU regulatory posture. Apple’s high-margin Services and App Store economics are exactly the kind of businesses that face scrutiny. If you are underwriting **49% gross margins**, you cannot ignore that tail risk.

### 3. The fundamentals are good — but you are extrapolating peak momentum

The H1 numbers are strong: revenue **+16.1%**, EPS **+19.8%**, operating cash flow **+53.3%**, net cash around **$62B**. I am not disputing that. But a good company is not automatically a good buy at any price, and you have no valuation support in this dataset. There is no P/E, no EV/EBITDA, no FCF yield. You are asking investors to buy near the highs on the assumption that H1 growth is the new normal.

That assumption has cracks:

- **Tax rate:** effective rate rose to **17.5% from 15.0%**, a 250bp headwind. Operating income grew **19.8%**, but net income grew **17.3%** — the conversion leakage is real.
- **R&D:** up **32.6% YoY** to **$22.3B**. That is the right long-term bet, but it is also a margin pressure point if revenue growth decelerates.
- **Buybacks:** down about **25% YoY** to **$37.0B**. Still huge, but the EPS tailwind is weakening.
- **Intangibles:** gross intangibles jumped **+$12.8B** to **$37.8B** with little amortization movement. That pattern strongly suggests an acquisition or asset purchase. Maybe it is fine. But it is unconfirmed in the supplied summary, and it introduces integration and capital-allocation risk.
- **Product cycle:** five iPhones through 2027, a foldable push, and a 2027 hardware refresh are reports, not signed demand. Foldables are catch-up, not leadership. AI/Siri catalysts are still unproven. These are long-dated, forward-looking narratives that may already be in the price.

The bull case says “strong fundamentals.” My response is: **at what price, and against what expectations?** AAPL is being asked to prove a breakout above **$317** while carrying unresolved blacklist risk, elevated volatility, a higher tax rate, rising R&D, slowing buybacks, and an unexplained intangible jump. That is a lot of execution risk for a stock already near its June high.

- The stock is **below its June high**, testing **$312–$317** resistance after a vertical five-day bounce.
- MACD is still below zero; the bullish cross is early and unconfirmed.

- Fundamentals are good, but the tax rate is rising, R&D is surging, buybacks are slowing, and the intangible jump needs explanation.

**My call: AAPL — Avoid / Underweight. Sell into $312–$317 strength.** I would only reconsider if AAPL delivers a decisive close above **$317** with MACD crossing above zero and volume follow-through, or if it pulls back to **$290–$295** and holds. The bull has a strong company and a good H1 report. But the bull does not have a compelling entry point. And at this price, with this headline risk and this technical setup, that matters more.
