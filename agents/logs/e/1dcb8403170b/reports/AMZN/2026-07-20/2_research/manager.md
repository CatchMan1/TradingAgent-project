**Recommendation**: Overweight

**Rationale**: Both sides made real points, but they were partly arguing on different horizons — so let me say plainly where each won and how I netted it out.

The bear won the single sharpest exchange of the debate, and I weight it heavily: the "$$569B contracted obligations" the bull built his moat argument on are Amazon's own OUTBOUND purchase commitments (chips, data centers, compute) — a spending/liability stack — not customer demand. Actual customer precommitment (deferred revenue / contract liabilities) is only ~$25B. That is a genuine ~96% category error and it guts the bull's marquee "proof of demand" line. The bull was sloppy here.

But that exchange doesn't sink the investment case, because the real demand proof was never the backlog — it's the income statement, and it's real: Q1 2026 revenue +16.6% to $181.5B, operating income +29.6%, operating margin +132 bps to 13.14%, ROE ~21.7%, with G&A down in absolute dollars while revenue grew mid-teens. Tellingly, the bear conceded "the operating engine is genuinely strong." The bear's own prescription was "wait — make them prove the capex earns its return," which is a timing overlay, not a thesis rejection.

The bear's secondary points are valid but directional, not thesis-breaking: negative FCF (−$2.5B TTM) driven by a deliberate capex super-cycle, a debt-funded buildout (+$53.4B LTD in one quarter, interest +48%, D&A +32.8%). These are genuine watch items — direction of travel matters — but interest is ~3.4% of quarterly operating income, D/E ~28%, $144B cash, ~$84B net debt against a $434B equity base. That's a burden to monitor, not distress. The most credible near-term bear point is earnings quality: roughly one-third of headline EPS is mark-to-market on a $48.1B non-marketable book that can reverse right into an imminent print — a genuine two-way event risk. That's precisely why I'm not at Buy and why entry discipline matters.

On the tape it's genuinely mixed: the bull has the primary trend (rising 200-SMA, price +6.7% above it); the bear has the near-term setup (declining 50-SMA overhead, three-session MACD histogram contraction, negative RSI divergence on 7/16, ~66% volume, a 252.89–256.65 resistance wall, ~10% off the 5/5 high).

Net: a strong, accelerating business sitting at a mediocre entry with real event risk. That is constructive, not bearish — the fundamentals clearly favor the bull, the near-term risk argues against aggressive buying into resistance. Overweight, sized moderately and built gradually, threads that needle better than a Hold (which ignores the conceded operating strength) or a Buy (which would over-commit into a coin-flip print).
