
Bear Analyst: ## Bear Analyst: GOOGL — Great Company, Bad Setup, Bad Price

At **$143.68** on 2024-02-05, GOOGL is not a “Buy/Accumulate in stages” setup. It is a **broken short-term tape sitting under a tight overhead-supply cluster, with the freshest fundamental data showing a free-cash-flow inflection and tax-flattered EPS**. That is an Avoid/Underweight until proven otherwise.

You say the bear case is “tactical, not strategic.” I say the tactical and fundamental cases are now aligned enough to matter. Let me take your points one by one.

The bull says, “A single volume-backed session above $145.50 clears all of it.” Correct — but it hasn’t happened. You don’t get credit for a trigger that hasn’t triggered. Until GOOGL proves it can reclaim that zone on volume, the path of least resistance is lower.

### 2. The fundamental “reacceleration” story is overstated

Bull, you say growth is re-accelerating. But the annual data shows revenue growth actually **decelerated** from **+9.8% in FY22 to +8.7% in FY23**. What recovered was profitability from a depressed FY22 base. That is not the same thing.

Yes, FY23 was better:

- Revenue: **$307.4B**
- Operating income: **$84.3B**
- Operating margin: **27.4%**
- Net income: **$73.8B**
- Diluted EPS: **$5.80**

But operating margin is still below FY21’s **30.6%**, and the margin repair was driven by cost-of-revenue growth collapsing to **+5.6%** from **+13.8%** in FY22. That is exactly the line that AI infrastructure depreciation will hit. If cost-of-revenue growth runs above roughly 6%, each 100bp removes about **$3B of operating income**, or roughly **$0.20 of EPS**. The margin story is fragile, not fortress-like.

More importantly, the freshest data is the Q4 implied numbers, and they are ugly:

- Q4 operating cash flow: **$18.9B, −19.9% YoY**
- Q4 capex: **$11.0B, +45.1% YoY**
- Q4 free cash flow: **$7.9B, −50.7% YoY**
- Q4 buybacks: **$16.2B**

That last line matters. Q4 buybacks were more than **2x Q4 free cash flow**. The 3% annual share shrink is not free — it is being supported by the balance sheet when FCF weakens. If the Q4 capex run-rate persists, FY24 capex could approach **$42–45B**, and the buyback engine that props up EPS growth gets squeezed.

You say the AI capex will earn a return. Maybe. But the data set does not show Cloud or AI segment ROI. You’re asking investors to pay for unproven future returns while the current FCF trend is deteriorating. That is not a margin of safety; that is a leap of faith.

And the headline cash flow is less impressive after adjusting for stock-based compensation:

- SBC: **$22.1B**, or **7.2% of revenue**, rising from 6.1% in FY21
- SBC-adjusted FCF: **$47.4B**, still below FY21’s **$51.3B** despite roughly 19% more revenue

So the “$100B operating cash flow” headline overstates shareholder-level cash generation.

### 3. EPS quality is weaker than the bull admits

You correctly concede the tax point, then wave it away. But it matters.

FY23 GAAP effective tax rate was **13.9%**, while cash taxes were **$19.2B**, a **22.4%** cash tax rate. The deferred-tax benefit was roughly **$7.7B**, or about **$0.57 per share**. That is a real EPS tailwind that may not repeat at the same magnitude.

On normalized EPS closer to **$5.23**, GOOGL at $143.68 trades at roughly **27.5x**. For a business with high-single-digit revenue growth, rising capex, and FCF pressure, that is not cheap. It is a full multiple that assumes the AI capex cycle works smoothly. If FY24 consensus is built on a mid-teens tax rate, estimates are vulnerable.

There is also a working-capital yellow flag: receivables grew **+19.2%** versus revenue **+8.7%**, with DSO up about **5 days**, and working capital consumed **$7.8B of operating cash flow** versus $2.3B in FY22. That may reflect Cloud/enterprise mix, but it reduces the quality of the OCF headline.

The balance sheet? Yes, roughly **$96B net cash** is a real floor. But a floor is not a catalyst. It does not prevent multiple compression, it does not fix FCF, and it does not make a 27.5x normalized multiple cheap.

---

### 4. The competitive moat is real, but the risks are being dismissed

Bull, you list Search, YouTube, Android/Play, Cloud, and AI optionality. All true. But none of that is a new insight, and the market is already paying for it.

The bear concerns are straightforward:

- GOOGL remains heavily advertising-dependent and cyclical.
- AI search could cannibalize high-margin Search monetization rather than simply extend it.
- Google Cloud competes against AWS and Azure, and its profitability is not verifiable in this dataset.
- Android/Play default advantages face regulatory and antitrust remedies risk.
- The bull calls regulatory risk “unquantifiable” — exactly. That is a reason to demand a discount, not to ignore it.

A fortress balance sheet gives GOOGL the ability to absorb shocks. It does not give investors a free pass on valuation or near-term execution.
