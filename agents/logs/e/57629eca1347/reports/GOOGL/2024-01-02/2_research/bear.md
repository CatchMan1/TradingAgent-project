
Bear Analyst: **Bear case for GOOGL — the bull is owning a 12-month story while ignoring a 3-month setup that is deteriorating**

I’ll start by giving the bull real credit: GOOGL is a high-quality franchise. The balance sheet is fortress-grade, the buyback is real, and Q3 revenue growth of **+11.0%** was a genuine step up from H1’s implied **+4.9%**. But that’s not the debate. The debate is whether GOOGL at **$138.17** on 2024-01-02 is a good risk/reward investment today. It isn’t. The bull is conflating “good company” with “good entry,” and conflating a one-quarter operating rebound with a durable forward inflection.

The technical report the bull cites actually supports the bear case more than his. It says GOOGL is **“structurally bullish but tactically stalling,”** that this is **“not a fresh-momentum chase,”** and that the highest-probability outcome over the next 2–4 weeks is **continued consolidation between roughly $136.5 and $142**. The base case is a range, the bear case is a **20% probability trend-break pullback**, and the bull case is a **40% probability breakout that requires a close above $139.72 on at least 30M shares** — volume GOOGL has not been producing at the highs. That is not an “accumulate now” setup. That is a “wait for proof” setup.

**The tape is not just pausing — it is showing distribution behavior**

The bull points to the rising 200-day SMA at **$125.10** and the 50-day above the 200-day. Fine. But that is the long-term trend. The short-term tape is what determines whether you get paid or stopped out first. GOOGL is trading **below its 10-day EMA at $138.66**, RSI has cooled from **65.69** to **54.93**, and MACD has rolled from **1.935** on 2023-12-28 to **1.764** while the histogram is compressing. The technical report explicitly warns of a possible MACD/signal bearish crossover within 3–7 sessions if price fails to reclaim $138.7–$139.0.

And the volume signature is a warning, not confirmation. The December 26 peak close of **$141.52** came on **16.78M shares**, versus a 20-session average near **30.6M**. The last four sessions of 2023 averaged roughly **17.8M**. The bull calls it a holiday-week excuse. Maybe. But the market’s job is to discount, not to excuse. A new high on shrinking participation, followed by a four-session pullback and a close in the lower half of the January 2 range, is classic low-conviction behavior. The December 7 breakout on **56.77M shares** was real, but the follow-through failed to attract volume. That matters.

The bull’s “VWMA defense” at **$136.48–$136.57** is also thinner than he admits. Price closed at **$138.17**, only **+1.17% above VWMA**. The January 2 low held, yes — but the session was down **-1.09%** on a range wider than ATR, closing below the 10-EMA. One intraday defense at the volume-weighted cost basis is not a trend. If **$136.48** breaks, the next levels are **$135.89**, then **$133.60**, then **$132.43**. The 50-day is only **3.4% below spot** — that’s not a safety net; that’s a short trip lower.

Then there’s the overhead supply the bull keeps glossing over. GOOGL is re-entering the **$138–$142** zone where October 24 closed at **$138.81** and then gapped down to **$128.16** on **84.37M shares**. That is a dense loss/breakeven shelf. The stock is not in open air. It is bumping its head into trapped supply, with the upper Bollinger band at **$143.95** and the December high at **$142.68**. The bull says by the time the setup is perfect, GOOGL will be through $143.95. I’d say by the time GOOGL proves it can clear **$139.72–$142.68 on real volume**, the risk/reward will be more honest — and right now it isn’t.

**The fundamental “inflection” is lower quality than the headline**

The bull’s strongest point is Q3 revenue **+11.0%** and operating income **+24.6%**. I’m not dismissing that. But look at the composition. Q3 operating margin was **27.8%**, up **303 bps** — yet on a nine-month basis, revenue and costs both grew **6.9%** and operating margin was **exactly flat at 27.4%**. The margin expansion is a Q3 phenomenon, not yet a full-year trend. The cost structure has been reset, but cost cuts are finite. Meanwhile, Q3 capex hit a record **$8.055B**, up **30% sequentially**. The FCF tailwind from capex discipline is already reversing.

That’s the critical point on cash flow. Nine-month FCF was **$61.599B, +40% YoY**, but the mechanism was capex falling **-11.1%** while CFO rose **+22.0%**. Capex intensity dropped to **9.6% of revenue** from **11.6%**. That is a one-time mix benefit. Q3’s capex slope tells you 2024 is going the other way. The fundamental report itself says sustainable annualized FCF is closer to **$65–70B**, not the **$82B** annualized run-rate. That’s a 15–20% haircut to the cash flow base the bull is implicitly capitalizing.

And the CFO number is not clean. **$10.392B** of the **$82.831B** CFO was tax working capital. Strip that out and underlying CFO growth is roughly **+7%**, in line with revenue and net income — not the **+22%** headline. The bull concedes this, but then still uses the FCF growth as a pillar of the thesis. You can’t have it both ways.

EPS quality has the same problem. Q3 EPS of **$1.55** was flattered by a **7.1% effective tax rate** versus **14.3%** a year earlier. Normalized at the prior-year tax rate, Q3 EPS is roughly **$1.43** — a **7.7% haircut**. The bull says normalized EPS still grew about **35% YoY**, and that’s true. But the forward risk is that consensus is anchored to the printed **$1.55** run-rate. If Q4 ETR normalizes to **14–16%**, that alone is a **$0.10–$0.15** EPS headwind. The bull is asking investors to pay for the printed number while assuming the tax benefit repeats. It probably doesn’t.

**SBC, legal, and balance-sheet overhangs are real, not narrative**

The bull calls SBC a “legitimate concern” and then moves on. But SBC was **$16.5B** in nine months — **7.5% of revenue** — and grew **+14.6%**, more than twice the **+6.9%** revenue growth. Buybacks are large, but they are partly neutralizing compensation, not magically creating value. Net share count fell **2.4%**, which is fine, but the effective capital return is less impressive once you adjust for SBC. And GOOGL paid **no dividend** in 9M 2023; total shareholder yield is buyback-only.

The legal and tax overhangs are quantified. The loss-contingency accrual is **$9.143B**. Accrued income taxes, current, jumped **+$11.896B** versus year-end 2022, and deferred tax assets rose **+$5.677B**. That combination points to a material tax/regulatory reassessment. The AFS unrealized loss is **$4.244B**, with **$45.495B** of loss-making securities now older than 12 months. Non-marketable equity write-downs increased by **$2.2B** in nine months on a **$28.821B** book. These are not thesis-killers for a company with **$119.9B** of cash and short-term investments, but they are real drags on book value and future earnings. The bull waves them away because the balance sheet is big. A big balance sheet absorbs mistakes; it doesn’t make them disappear.

**Competitive position: strong, but not invulnerable**

The bull says GOOGL is the global #1 in digital advertising and #3 in public cloud. Correct. But being #3 in cloud is not a free option — it is a capex arms race. Q3’s record capex is the cost of staying relevant against larger cloud competitors and the AI infrastructure buildout. The bull frames rising capex as “investment in AI/cloud capacity.” The bear framing is less romantic: it is a defensive spending cycle that compresses near-term FCF and may not earn an adequate return if AI monetization lags.

And the regulatory risk is not hypothetical. The report specifically cites a **$9.143B** loss-contingency accrual tied to legal/regulatory matters, including EU antitrust and ad-tech. The bull says it’s “already reserved.” Reserves are estimates. Adverse rulings can move the liability and the narrative. For a stock trading at roughly **25x** the annualized 9M EPS of **$5.55**, the market is not pricing in much regulatory downside.

**Directly engaging the bull’s counterpoints**

- **Bull: “The primary uptrend is intact.”** True, but the tactical report says the stock is stalling below the 10-EMA with MACD rolling and a flat 50-day. A primary uptrend can still deliver a 3–5% drawdown. At **$138.17**, the bull’s own preferred entry is **$136.50–$137.00** — below spot. That concession tells you current price is not the bargain he claims.

- **Bull: “The December high was just holiday volume.”** Maybe. But low-volume highs are still low-conviction highs. The breakout day was December 7; the follow-through to **$141.52** failed to attract participation. If institutions were accumulating, we’d expect volume expansion into strength, not contraction.

- **Bull: “VWMA held at $136.50.”** It held for one session, by **9 cents**. That is not a structural floor. A close below **$136.48** opens **$135.89**, then **$133.60**. The bull’s stop at **$133.40** is a 3.4% drop from spot — that’s the risk he’s asking you to underwrite for a first target only **2.4%** above spot.

- **Bull: “Capex is investment, not a problem.”** It’s both. It may strengthen the franchise long term, but it compresses FCF now. The market pays for FCF and EPS, not strategic optionality. The report’s own sustainable FCF estimate of **$65–70B** versus the **$82B** annualized run-rate is the number that matters for valuation.

- **Bull: “Normalized EPS still grew 35%.”** Yes, but the stock is not priced off Q3 2022. It’s priced off forward earnings, and the forward tax rate is a headwind, not a tailwind. The 9M normalized EPS adjustment is small, but the Q4 consensus risk is not.

**The bear recommendation on GOOGL**

I’m not shorting a net-cash franchise with a rising 200-day SMA. But I am saying **do not initiate new money at $138.17**. GOOGL is a **hold-at-best, avoid-new-buy** until one of two things happens: either it proves itself with a daily close above **$139.72** — ideally above **$142.68** — on **30M+ shares**, or it resets to the **$133.60–$132.43** area and forms a higher low. The middle of the range, below the 10-EMA, with MACD rolling and overhead supply at **$138–$142**, is where risk/reward is worst.

The bull’s case is a 12-month structural story. My case is that the evidence in front of us — a low-volume December high, a four-session pullback, a flat 50-day, decelerating momentum, tax-flattered EPS, non-recurring CFO benefits, and a capex inflection that kills the FCF tailwind — says the next move is more likely to be sideways-to-down than an immediate breakout. For GOOGL, that means **wait for confirmation or a better price. Don’t pay for the printed Q3 beat.**
