
Bull Analyst: **Bull case for AAPL — opening statement**

Let me start with the punchline: **AAPL at $294.38 is not a broken momentum trade. It is a high-quality compounder sitting inside an intact uptrend, with fundamentals that are accelerating while the chart resets.** The bear will likely come at this from three directions — memory-chip costs, an unconfirmed short-term technical picture, and mega-cap fatigue. Those are legitimate watch items, but none of them beat the evidence in front of us.

### 1. The “mature hardware” bear thesis is contradicted by the fundamentals

AAPL’s latest reported period is not a company struggling for growth. In FQ2 FY2026, AAPL posted:

- Revenue of **$111.18B, +16.6% YoY**
- Gross margin of **49.3%, +230 bps YoY**
- Operating margin of **32.3%, +130 bps**
- Diluted EPS of **$2.01, +21.8%**
- H1 revenue of **$254.94B, +16.1%**
- H1 EPS of **$4.85, +19.8%**

That is not a low-growth hardware story. That is a business reaccelerating with margin expansion. And AAPL is doing it while spending heavily on the future: **R&D rose 33.6% YoY in FQ2**, and H1 R&D intensity climbed to **8.75% of revenue** from **7.66%** a year ago. The bear may say Apple is underinvesting in AI or innovation. The financials say the opposite.

The balance sheet is also a weapon: **~$61.9B net cash**, total debt down **~$15.9B** from FY2025 year-end, current ratio improved to **1.07**, and H1 free cash flow of **$78.3B**. AAPL returned **$44.7B** in H1 through buybacks and dividends, and the share count is shrinking. That is a company with enormous financial flexibility to fund innovation, absorb input-cost shocks, and keep supporting EPS.

### 2. The competitive moat is still underpriced by the bears

AAPL’s advantage is not just the iPhone. It is the ecosystem: hardware, software, Services, payments, security, and an installed base that keeps monetizing.

The news flow in the window supports incremental strength: **American Express adding Apple Pay rewards redemption** strengthens Services engagement, and AAPL’s early AI-related cybersecurity software updates reinforce the trust/security narrative. The acquisition of the team behind the Play App is small financially, but it supports product design and talent depth. These are not thesis-changing events by themselves, but they are consistent with a company extending its ecosystem rather than defending a static franchise.

Most importantly, look at pricing power. Reports say AAPL is raising prices on “just about everything except iPhones — for now” due to memory costs. The bear will frame that as a cost problem. I frame it as evidence of **pricing power** — the ability to pass through input inflation while strategically holding iPhone pricing to protect demand. That is what a premium brand with a sticky ecosystem can do.

### 3. The memory-chip scare is real — but it is not a thesis-breaker

This is where I expect the bear to pound the table: Cook called the memory-chip shortage “extreme,” AAPL is reportedly seeking Chinese memory from CXMT, and that sourcing route is politically sensitive.

I’m not going to pretend that is irrelevant. It is a genuine near-term margin and geopolitical overhang. But the bear has to prove it breaks the AAPL investment case. So far, the evidence says it is manageable:

- AAPL has already shown **49.3% gross margin** in the latest reported quarter.
- The company is **passing through costs** outside the iPhone.
- Its balance sheet has **~$61.9B net cash** to absorb supply volatility.
- The CXMT route, if approved, could actually become a **cost-relief tailwind** — that is the optionality the bear is ignoring.
- If CXMT access is blocked, AAPL still has existing supplier relationships and pricing power to fall back on.

So yes, memory is a risk. But it is a **known, quantified-in-direction, multi-quarter input issue**, not an existential break in the franchise. The bear is treating a supply-chain headwind as if it were a demand collapse. The revenue and EPS data do not support that leap.

### 4. The technical picture is resetting inside a long-term uptrend

Let’s debate the chart directly, because this is where the bear will get loud.

AAPL is at **$294.38**, right at the resistance cluster: Bollinger mid-band **$294.88** and the 50-SMA **$292.67**. The short-term picture is not fully confirmed — the 10-EMA at **$290.25** is still below the 50-SMA, MACD is below zero, and the MACD line has not yet crossed back above its signal. The bear will say, “See? Momentum is still bearish.”

But that is only half the tape.

The dominant signal is the long-term trend: the **200-SMA is rising at $270.33**, and AAPL is about **9% above it**. The 50-SMA has risen from roughly **$261 in early May to $292.67 now**. This is still a bullish stack: price above the 50-SMA, 50-SMA above the rising 200-SMA.

And the June washout has the classic signatures of a defended low:

- Intraday low **$273.75** on 6/25, close **$275.15** on **107M shares**
- Next session close **$283.78** on **261.8M shares** — a massive volume reversal/absorption bar
- Price now above VWMA **$288.94**, meaning the recent recovery is volume-supported
- RSI has reset to **50.97** — neutral, not overbought
- MACD histogram has narrowed from about **−2.74** to **−1.25**, and the MACD line has turned up from **−2.92 → −2.79 → −2.26**

So the bear is right that confirmation is pending. But the bear is wrong to call this a broken chart. This is a **coiled setup at resistance after a climactic washout**, with the long-term trend still intact.

### 5. How I’d trade and invest in AAPL here

I would frame AAPL as **selectively bullish with confirmation** — and I think the risk/reward supports owning it.

- **Tactical trigger:** a daily close above **~$295** on above-average volume, with MACD crossing back above its signal and RSI pushing above **55**.
- **Upside targets:** **$302**, then **$312.68** Bollinger upper band, then the prior high zone **$315–$317**.
- **Risk management:** stops below **$288** VWMA for tighter tactical trades, or below **~$282** for wider ATR-adjusted positioning. With ATR at **$8.20**, position sizing should be smaller and stops wider.
- **Longer-term investors:** the current zone is a reasonable accumulation area because the 200-SMA is rising and the fundamental machine is still compounding.

The bear’s best argument is technical near-term uncertainty. But that is exactly what creates the opportunity: AAPL is not extended, RSI is neutral, the washout has already happened, and the stock is sitting just below a clear breakout level.

### Bottom line

AAPL is a **growth-and-quality franchise in an intact long-term uptrend**, trading at a decision point after a washout. The bear can point to memory costs, political risk, and an unconfirmed short-term MACD — and I’ll respect those points. But the bear still has to explain away **+16% revenue growth, 49.3% gross margins, +21.8% EPS growth, $78B of H1 free cash flow, $62B net cash, aggressive buybacks, rising R&D investment, and a rising 200-SMA**.

I don’t think the bear can do that convincingly.

**AAPL recommendation: Bullish / Buy on confirmation above $295; accumulate on strength toward $302, $312.68, and $315–$317. Risk below $288, with $282 as a wider ATR-adjusted stop.**
