
Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for AAPL — Opening Argument

Let me start where the bear is going to end up, because I want to save us both some time. The bear is going to walk in here with three talking points: the volume on the push to the highs was light, there's a little MACD divergence, and Samsung "took back the crown." I'm going to show you why each of those is either a non-event or actually an artifact of the *strongest fundamental setup AAPL has printed in years* — and why the weight of evidence says you own this name, not fade it.

Let's get into it.

---

## 1. The growth story just did something it almost never does: it accelerated

Here's the number that should end the debate before it starts. **Q3 FY26 diluted EPS grew 28.7% year-over-year. Q2 grew 21.8%. Q1 grew 18.3%.** Three consecutive quarters of *acceleration*. Revenue up 16.4% in Q3, 9M revenue up 16.2% — roughly **double the growth rate of FY25's trajectory.** This is not a company limping into a mature-hardware narrative. This is a $364B-revenue business growing mid-teens while its **gross margin hit 50.06% in Q3, up 357 basis points.**

Think about what that combination means. COGS grew 8.6% against revenue growth of 16.4%. Apple isn't buying growth with discounts — it's *expanding margin while accelerating revenue*. That's the signature of pricing power and mix shift, and it's exactly the engine that funds the next leg.

And before the bear says "that's all Services margin flattering the mix" — sure, Services helps, but the data here shows operating margin up 263 bps *while R&D grew 32.3%*. Apple is absorbing a massive investment step-up and still expanding operating margin. That's not a company running out of runway. That's a company flooring the accelerator.

---

## 2. The balance sheet is now a weapon, not a liability

The bear's old faithful — "Apple is over-levered, buybacks are financial engineering" — is dead on arrival.

- **Net cash $62.2B, up 84%** year-over-year.
- **Debt/equity 0.78x, down from 1.34x.**
- **Current ratio 1.00x, up from 0.89x** — crossed the line for the first time in years.
- **Total debt cut $14.3B; zero new long-term debt issued; commercial paper nearly eliminated.**
- **9M free cash flow $110.2B, up 52.5%.**

That's an Apple that is *deleveraging and hoarding optionality* — $62B net cash, $110B of nine-month FCF, buybacks deliberately trimmed 12%, and a mysterious **+$12.1B intangible build alongside +$13.5B in other non-current liabilities.** The bear will call that "unexplained." I call it the footprint of a company positioning for its next strategic move — likely AI capability. Either way, the downside tail risk here is earnings-driven, not solvency-driven, and the balance sheet gives management an enormous toolkit to defend the stock.

---

## 3. Competitive advantages: the moat is measured in margin points

AAPL's competitive position isn't a vibe — it's in the numbers:

- **50% gross margin** on a hardware-heavy mix is a structural advantage almost no competitor can touch at scale.
- **9.3% of revenue into R&D** funded entirely from gross-margin expansion.
- **Capex at just 1.9% of revenue** — an asset-light model throwing off $110B of FCF.
- **DSO collapsed to ~26 days from ~38.5 days** — receivables falling 21% while revenue rose 16%. That's collection power.

And the *ecosystem* is about to get a brand-new premium tier. The **iPhone Duo foldable at $1,999**, unveiled September 9 under new CEO John Ternus, is exactly the kind of ASP-expanding, halo-building product that lets Apple grow revenue *without* chasing unit volume. The bear will tell you $1,999 narrows the addressable market. Correct — and that's the point. Apple has never won by being cheap. It wins by capturing the top of the market and letting Services monetize the installed base.

## 4. The technical setup is structurally *better* than the July top — this is the key insight

Now let's talk tape, because this is where the bear thinks he has an edge. He doesn't.

AAPL closed at **$338.98** on September 21 — **0.32% below the record close** and **1.65% below the all-time high.** The bear wants to frame this as "resistance overhead, thin volume, don't chase." But look under the hood:

**Price is at the same absolute level as July, but with *more support underneath it*.** In July, price sat **+9.59% above the 50-day SMA and +21.99% above the 200-day SMA.** Today those premiums are **+5.73% and +18.28%.** Same price, *rising* moving averages that have come up to meet it. That's a materially stronger foundation — the market built a floor while it consolidated.

**Full bullish stack, all rising:** Price > 10 EMA ($331.57) > VWMA ($327.55) > 50 SMA ($320.62) > 200 SMA ($286.61). The 50 is climbing roughly twice as fast as the 200, and the **50/200 spread is widening at +11.87%.** That's a healthy golden-cross regime, not a late-cycle rollover.

**Volatility did the textbook thing:** band width compressed from 22.6% (July) to 9.07% (Sept 9), then began **expanding upward to 11.50%** — a squeeze releasing *in the direction of the trend*. ATR collapsed 26% from the gap session while price ground higher. Translation: the market **absorbed the July shock** and is now trending on lower noise.

**RSI at 66.32** — strong, with headroom to 70, and below the July reading of 68.9. **MACD histogram positive for 18 consecutive sessions**, and the histogram actually sits *above* its July peak. This is a maturing impulse, not a failing one.

## 5. Now let me take the bear's best shots head-on

**"The push to the highs came on 0.81× average volume."**
Fair observation — I'll take it. But context matters: the *medium-term recovery* has excellent volume pedigree — **65.6M on the Sept 9 absorption day, 70M on the Sept 10 +3.56% demand confirmation.** The final approach being thin is a reason to **buy the pullback into the 328–332 shelf or a confirmed breakout above $342.90 with volume** — both of which the data supports — not a reason to be bearish on the name. Thin approaches to resistance fail *or* resolve; the trend and momentum indicators lean toward resolve. And I'll happily note the 342.75 upper Bollinger band converging with the 342.89 July high — that's a high-confidence breakout trigger, not a wall.

**"There's a MACD histogram divergence — price up 3.28%, histogram down 20.5% since Sept 3."**
It's a *micro* divergence inside a primary uptrend, and the report itself says it's "not a sell signal inside a trend this strong." The histogram has **plateaued over the last five sessions** — it's consolidation, not distribution. Resolve above 1.70–2.03 and the divergence is dead. This argues for patience on entry timing, not for a bearish stance.

**"Samsung retook the smartphone throne."**
Share rankings are cycle-dependent, and Apple **routinely cedes unit share ahead of and around its own launch cadence** — which is precisely the window we're in. Meanwhile the *same Benzinga roundup* notes **iPhone demand "jumps"** per a consumer survey that drove a price-target hike, and **Musk dropped an antitrust claim.** I'll trade a noisy quarterly share print for ASP expansion and a bullish Street revision every day.

**"The $1,999 foldable is priced for enthusiasts only."**
The bear is confusing TAM with *profit pool*. Apple's model monetizes the top of the market through hardware ASPs *and* Services. A premium foldable is a margin and halo event, not a volume event — and the Street price-target hike on iPhone demand tells you the demand signal is already inflecting, foldable or not.

**"The AI-server/M8 Ultra story is just a rumor leaning on Nvidia."**
It's long-dated optionality — I'll grant the execution risk and the capex drag. But optionality on Apple re-rating as an AI-inference player is worth *something*, and the market pays for narrative inflection before EPS. Combined with the $62B net cash hoard and the unexplained $12B intangible build, you have a company clearly **building toward something.** You don't want to be on the sidelines when that "something" is confirmed.

**"Tax headwind and R&D step-up pressure EPS."**
The 219 bps effective-rate increase is real — but normalize for it and **underlying EPS growth is ~25–26%**, which makes the business look *better*, not worse. And R&D growing 32.5% is **self-funding** as long as gross margin holds ≥49% — which it did at 50.06%. This is investment, not deterioration. The bear is rooting for a margin mean-reversion that the data doesn't show.

**"Inventory +94%, raw materials +260% — demand is rolling over."**
Read the composition: **finished goods *fell* 4.1%.** This is a raw-material pre-build — consistent with locking in favorable component costs (hence the 50% gross margin) and buffering supply-chain/tariff risk ahead of the iPhone 18 Pro and Duo ramp. The bear's interpretation requires ignoring that finished goods didn't budge. The Q4 print is the tie-breaker, and three quarters of accelerating growth give management the benefit of the doubt.

## 6. The bottom line

The bear's case is a *timing* argument dressed up as a *thesis* argument. Yes — buying at $338.98 into stacked resistance at $340/$342.89 isn't the highest-expectancy entry. I'll concede the tactical point. But here's what the bear has to explain away: **accelerating EPS growth, 50% gross margins, $110B of nine-month FCF, an 84% jump in net cash, a full bullish moving-average stack, a squeeze releasing upward, RSI with headroom, a widening golden cross, an expanding product lineup, and a balance sheet that de-risks the whole story.**

That's an overwhelming weight of evidence for **higher-probability breakout than breakdown** — and the structural setup is *objectively better* than the one that produced the July high.

So my recommendation is not "sit out." It's: **own AAPL, and use the map.** Accumulate on the 328–332 shelf (stop below 323.50), or add on a confirmed close above $342.90 on volume above the 43M average (stop at 335.40), with the 50-SMA at $320.62 as the line that would actually invalidate the medium-term bull case. The trend, the fundamentals, and the competitive position are all aligned. The bear is arguing about the next five points; I'm arguing about the next leg. I'll take that trade every single time.
