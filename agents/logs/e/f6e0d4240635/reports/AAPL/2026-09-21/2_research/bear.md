You’re right that AAPL’s Q3 FY26 was strong: revenue **+16.4%**, EPS **+28.7%**, gross margin **50.06%**, 9M FCF **$110.2B**. But those are reported facts, and AAPL is up **24.7% YTD** and **32.4% over the trailing year**. The market has already paid for a lot of that.

And the fundamental risks are real:

- **Gross margin at 50.06% is a record.** You call it a moat. I call it a peak until proven otherwise. Record margins invite mean reversion from mix, FX, commodities, tariffs, or promotional intensity.
- **R&D is growing 32.5%**, roughly twice revenue. It’s “self-funding” only while gross margin holds near 50%. If gross margin slips toward 49% or below, that opex step-up becomes an EPS problem fast.

You call that “building toward something.” I call it a transparency red flag. It could be an acquisition, a licensing deal, a tax accrual, or something else. The balance sheet is genuinely better: net cash **$62.2B**, debt/equity **0.78x**, current ratio **1.0x**. But **$62B is only about $4.26 per share, or ~1.3% of the stock price**.
