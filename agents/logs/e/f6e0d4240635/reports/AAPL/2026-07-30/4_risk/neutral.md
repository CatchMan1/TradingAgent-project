The 2026-07-30 true range was about 8.60, slightly above the 8.06 ATR, and most of that came from a 5.09 gap. If AAPL gaps on the print, you fill at the print, not at your stop. But here's where you overreach. The claim that the cost of being light is "just one confirmation at 338.2, about 1.4 percent above spot" is only true if the tape is orderly, and you just told us a binary print gaps through levels. If FQ3 is a blowout, AAPL doesn't politely trade up to 338.2 and let you re-risk. It gaps to 345, 350, maybe 355, and you're rebuilding a third of the position six to eight percent higher, not 1.4. The honest statement is that the trim costs you something real in the 35 percent bull branch; the case for it is that it saves more in the 65 percent non-bull branch. That's a good case, but the insurance isn't free.

Second, calling this a "bold" or "risk-taking" strategy doesn't survive contact with what it is. A trim into a known binary is, by construction, defensive — it lowers beta to the event. You can be proud of it, but it isn't bold, and framing it that way is marketing. The rebuild capacity is real, but you don't get credit for having a documented way to buy back what you sold. What matters is whether the sizing and prices are right.
