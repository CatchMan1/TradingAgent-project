That is exactly where risk discipline needs to slow the desk down. A good company, a strong trend, and a real fundamental story do not automatically make $315 to $318 a good place to add risk.

On the “stretched above the 200 SMA is not a problem” point: I agree the primary trend is up, but that is not the issue. The issue is that AAPL is at $315.32, just under a proven $315 to $317 resistance shelf, still about 7.6% above VWMA at $293.13 and 5.9% above the 50 SMA at $297.77. The aggressive analyst says strength is confirmation. Fine, but strength at resistance is also where late buyers get penalized if the breakout fails. RSI at 62.93 is not overbought, but it is not a buy signal either. The prior RSI peak near 79.8 earlier this year did not prevent a correction. The conservative lesson from that is not “RSI has room, so chase”; it is “momentum can reverse quickly after a strong run.” We should not confuse a healthy trend with a favorable entry.

On volume: the aggressive analyst is leaning heavily on the June 26 reversal bar at 261.8 million shares. That bar tells us a bottom was defended. It does not tell us that the current push into $315 to $317 will succeed. The current session volume is 34.13 million versus a 50 to 75 million recent norm. That is light. The aggressive analyst calls it a compressed tape waiting for a trigger; I call it a tape that has not yet proven demand. That is a wide risk in a volatile name at resistance. A stop can also gap through on headlines. So the conservative response is not “wider stop, press harder.” The conservative response is “smaller size, demand more confirmation, and do not create a large overweight at an extended price.” If a breakout add is made, it should be a half or quarter tranche, sized so a stop-out costs a small fraction of portfolio risk, not a full +10 to 20% overweight without hesitation.
