## 1. Your “accelerating growth” point hides the deterioration underneath

But look at what happened *inside* the P&L in the most recent quarter:

- Operating margin fell from **36.1% in Q1 to 34.0% in Q2**—a **210bp sequential compression**.
- Total costs and expenses rose **+12.6% q/q** versus revenue **+9.0% q/q**.
- G&A rose **+50.6% q/q**.
- Legal loss-contingency accruals rose **$1.76B in Q2 alone**, to **$17.4B**.

So yes, the top line is accelerating. But the cost line is accelerating faster, and the normalized EPS trajectory in the report decelerated from roughly **+24% in Q1 to +18% in Q2**. That is not a clean “accelerating franchise” story. That is a company spending aggressively to buy growth while its operating margin starts to crack.

And on the bull’s preferred normalized EPS of **~$5.30 for H1**, annualized that is roughly **$10.60**. At **$342.48**, GOOGL is around **32x annualized normalized H1 EPS**—for a company that just printed **negative free cash flow**. That is not a washed-out multiple. That is a full multiple on a cash-flow story that is getting worse, not better.

## 4. The FCF scare is not a mask. It is the thesis.

This is where your argument is weakest.

You concede Q2 free cash flow was **−$5.9B**—the first negative FCF quarter in modern Alphabet history—and then call it “strength wearing a scary mask.” I disagree. It is a cash-conversion problem, and it is getting worse before it gets better.

Look at the actual numbers:

| Metric | H1 2026 | Signal |
|---|---:|---|
| Operating cash flow | $84.9B | +32.8% headline |
| OCF ex deferred-tax add-back | $57.3B | **−12.5% y/y** |
| Capex | $80.6B | **+103% y/y** |
| FCF | +$4.3B | **−82% y/y** |
| Q2 FCF | **−$5.9B** | First negative quarter |
| Capex / revenue | **35.1%** | From 21.2% |
| Accrued but unpaid capex | **$29.1B** | +174% y/y |

That **$29.1B of accrued but unpaid capex** is a Q3 FCF landmine. Q2 cash capex was already **$44.9B** and accelerating. The cash flow statement is going to feel the overhang.

Then there’s the depreciation catch-up. H1 depreciation was **$13.6B** against **$80.6B** of capex—only **16.9%** of capex. If annualized capex runs near **$180B** and useful lives are around six years, steady-state depreciation alone approaches **~$30B per quarter** versus roughly **~$7B per quarter today**. That is a multi-hundred-basis-point structural margin headwind that has not yet shown up in the P&L. You call it “known and modelable.” I call it “not yet in the numbers,” which is exactly when markets tend to underestimate it.

And the capital allocation regime change is not a minor detail:

- **Buybacks: $0** versus **$28.3B** in H1 2025.
- **Common stock issued: +$30.5B.**
- **Convertible preferred issued: +$19.1B.**
- **Total debt: $101.1B**, up **+106%** in six months.
- **Share count: +1.2% YTD.**

You say the balance sheet gives GOOGL “3+ years of runway.” I say runway is not return. A company can have $141B of net cash and still destroy equity value if the incremental capex earns below its cost of capital. Negative FCF removes the buyback support that historically underpinned the multiple. Management issued **$30.5B of common equity at ~$267/share** and stopped repurchasing stock. That is not a vote of confidence in the current price—it is a signal that the capital needs are large and the buyback floor is gone.
