  • Risk per share: ~$9 (tight) to ~$13.50 (structural).
  • Sizing: a 1% account risk with a $13.50 per-share stop implies ~0.074% of account equity per share, i.e., a notional exposure of roughly 74% of equity if you risk exactly 1%. Scale to your own tolerance — the point is that the compressed ATR currently permits larger size per unit of risk than it did on 2026-08-05, when ATR was 55% higher.
  • Caution: volatility contraction at multi-month lows is not stability — it is stored energy. Once the squeeze resolves, expect ATR to expand quickly, so do not leave stops at compressed-ATR distances after a breakout; trail them wider.

So let’s debate it properly.

1. Growth: this is acceleration, not deterioration

The bear wants to talk about the chart and the cash flow. Fine. But start with the operating engine, because it is the strongest part of the story.

  • Q2’26 revenue: $119.8B, +24.2% year over year.
  • Q1’26 revenue growth: +21.8%.
  • 1H’26 revenue: $229.7B, +23.1%.
  • 1H’26 operating income: $80.5B, +30.0%.
  • 1H’26 gross margin: 62.0%, up 240bp.
  • 1H’26 operating margin: 35.0%, up 190bp.

That is sequential acceleration in revenue growth and operating leverage on top of it. A company that is “broken” does not post 24% revenue growth with 35% operating margins and expanding gross margins. The bear can point to Q2’s sequential operating-margin slippage from 36.1% to 34.0%, and I’ll acknowledge it—but it was still up 160bp year over year. That is not margin collapse. That is a company investing heavily and still getting more efficient.

The bear will also say reported EPS is fake. I agree. Reported 1H’26 EPS of $14.24 is dominated by $135.8B of securities gains—62.8% of pre-tax income. I’m not defending that number. On an annualized normalized basis, that’s roughly $10.60 per share; at $346.96, GOOGL is around 33x normalized earnings. For a business growing revenue 24%, operating income 30%, with $141B of net cash, that is not a bubble multiple. The bear is attacking a straw man by using the noisy GAAP EPS; the operating earnings are high quality.

2. Competitive advantage: distribution plus a fortress balance sheet

GOOGL’s moat is not just Search or YouTube. It is the combination of distribution, enterprise cloud, and the ability to self-fund the most capital-intensive AI buildout in the industry.

The Google Cloud–Verizon strategic partnership announced August 24 is a perfect proof point. It’s not quantified, so I won’t pretend it moves near-term revenue. But it is a marquee enterprise AI win against AWS and Azure. Verizon is exactly the kind of large, demanding logo that validates Google Cloud’s enterprise credibility. That matters for the multi-year cloud/AI narrative.

And then there’s the balance sheet, which is a genuine competitive weapon in this cycle:

  • Cash + short-term investments: $242.5B.
  • Long-term debt: $101.1B.
  • Net cash: ~$141.4B.
  • Interest coverage: ~44x.
  • 1H’26 operating cash flow: $84.9B, +32.8%.
