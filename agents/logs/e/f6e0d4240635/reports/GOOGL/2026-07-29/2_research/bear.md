## 1. "The engine is accelerating" — then why is the margin rolling over?

The bull wants you to focus on +24.2% revenue and +30.4% operating income *year-over-year*. Fine. Now look at the *sequence*, which the bull conspicuously skipped:

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Δ QoQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $109.90B | $119.80B | **+9.0%** |
| Cost of revenue | $41.27B | $45.94B | **+11.3%** |
| Gross margin | 62.4% | **61.6%** | **−80bp** |
| Operating margin | 36.1% | **34.0%** | **−210bp** |

Costs are growing **faster** than revenue sequentially. The bull calls Q1's 36.1% "the peak of the cycle" — I agree. That's exactly my point. **Q1 2026 was the top tick on the margin curve, and it's already downhill.** The bull wants to hand-wave 210bp of sequential operating-margin compression as "the cost of building." I want you to notice that D&A was only **$13.6B against $80.6B of capex**. The depreciation curve hasn't even *started*. What we're seeing in Q2 is the **entrée**. The main course arrives over the next 4–8 quarters, exactly as the bull's own §2 admits.

The bull's "positive operating leverage" is real *year-over-year* — against an easy comp and before the depreciation ramp. The *trend* is negative. A trader doesn't get paid for the year-over-year; they get paid for what happens next.

And the "backlog building" point? Contract liabilities rose **$1.5B to $10.1B** — on **$480B of annualized revenue**. That's 2% of a year's sales in deferred revenue. Calling that "forward demand" is dressing up a rounding error as a thesis.

## 3. The bull's valuation math has a hole you could drive a data center through

Let me take the bull's "cheapest quality-growth setup in mega-cap tech" claim apart piece by piece.

**The bull's math:** operating EPS ~$2.68/qtr → ~$10.70 annualized → 31x trailing → back out net cash → **~24x EV/EBIT for 30% EBIT growth.**

Here's the problem. The bull is applying a **trailing EBIT multiple to a business whose depreciation is about to multiply.** $80.6B of capex in six months — call it $160B+ annualized — will translate into D&A far above the current $13.6B half-year run-rate over the next 2–3 years. The bull *acknowledges* this in §2 ("the D&A line multiplies") and then *ignores it* in §3 ("24x EBIT for 30% growth"). You cannot do both.

If D&A doubles from here — a conservative assumption given the capex base — that's roughly **$27B of annual depreciation flowing straight through gross profit**. On $480B of revenue, even a modest D&A step-up of $20–25B annually is a **500–600bp gross-margin headwind** over the next 2–3 years, before we even discuss the revenue the new capacity is supposed to earn.

So the honest multiple is **not 24x EV/EBIT**. It's 24x **pre-depreciation** EBIT, on a business whose depreciation is accelerating and whose *sequential* margin is already sliding. Adjust for the capex that generates that EBIT and the multiple is materially higher. The bull's "cheapest quality-growth setup" is a function of **where you start measuring the cost of the growth.**

And on earnings quality — the bull concedes 63% of H1 EPS is mark-to-market, then says the **$22.8B deferred tax liability** is a "conservative cushion." Let me be precise about what that $22.8B is: it's a **deferred tax liability on unrealized gains** — meaning the gains aren't the issue, the **cash tax is already being paid on them** (accrued income taxes current went from **$523M to $5,233M**). That's a real cash drain. And the *reversal risk* isn't hypothetical: the equity FV gains are **$99.0B in a single quarter** on a **$124.3B non-marketable book with $85.7B of cumulative upward marks**. Private AI valuations resetting is now a **swing factor larger than Alphabet's annual operating income**. The bull calls that "an opportunity to buy the operating business into a GAAP selloff." The market rarely gives you that gift cleanly — it re-rates the whole name.
