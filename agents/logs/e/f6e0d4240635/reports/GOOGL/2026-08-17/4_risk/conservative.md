The aggressive case keeps saying the 200 SMA is rising and GOOGL is still above it. That is true. But the 200 SMA is a lagging line, and price is only about 1.16 ATR above it. That is not a clean “primary trend is intact, buy the starter” setup. That is a corrective regime inside a longer uptrend, and the burden of proof should be on the bulls before new capital is committed.

The aggressive analyst says waiting for a close above 354.52 means paying 3% more and chasing. I disagree. A conservative process is allowed to miss the first 3% if it avoids a failing rally into the 353.33/354.52 confluence. On fundamentals, the aggressive analyst is right that GOOGL is not a solvency story. Revenue grew 24.2% in Q2, operating income grew 30.4%, gross margin expanded 215bp, operating margin expanded 160bp, operating cash flow rose 32.8%, and net cash is roughly $138.8 billion. Those are real strengths. But the conservative concern is not whether GOOGL can pay its bills. It is whether the equity is being asked to digest a capital-allocation regime change at the same time the tape is weak. H1 free cash flow collapsed to $4.26 billion from $24.25 billion, Q2 free cash flow was roughly negative $5.9 billion, capex was 37.5% of Q2 revenue, buybacks went to zero, debt doubled to $101.1 billion, interest expense rose 390% year over year in Q2, and Alphabet issued $18.0 billion of convertible preferred that sits ahead of common. Depreciation rose 43.2% in H1, and there is $29.1 billion of capex incurred but not yet paid. That means the depreciation wave is not finished. The operating business is excellent; the equity story now carries more capital intensity, more dilution risk, and less free-cash-flow support than it did a year ago. A conservative analyst does not ignore that just because the balance sheet is still net cash.

But normalization does not automatically mean “buy the operating line.” It means value GOOGL off roughly $10.60 annualized core EPS, which at $344.00 is about 32.5 times core earnings. That is not an obvious bargain for a company with negative Q2 free cash flow, a rising depreciation burden, and an unresolved roughly $32 billion investing outflow.
