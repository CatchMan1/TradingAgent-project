## 3. Let me take the bear's FCF argument apart, because it's the strongest one they have

Here's the number that matters: **Q2 2026 free cash flow was −$5.86B** and **H1 FCF was $4.26B, down 82.4%.** The bear will hang the whole case on this. Here's why it's a misread.

**Operating cash flow rose +32.8% YoY to $84.86B.** The cash engine didn't break — it got *stronger*, and it outpaced revenue growth of +23.1%. DSO improved (AR +10.0% against revenue +23.1%), and the allowance for doubtful accounts was flat. That's high-quality collection.

The FCF collapse is **entirely a discretionary capex decision**, and I can size it precisely. H1 2025 capex ran at 21.2% of revenue. Applied to H1 2026 revenue of $229.7B, that's $48.7B. Actual H1 capex was $80.6B — a $31.9B deliberate step-up. **Absent that choice, H1 FCF would have been ~$36B, not $4.26B.** Q2 tells the same story: at 21.2% of $119.8B, capex would have been $25.4B, and Q2 FCF would have been **+$13.6B instead of −$5.86B.**

So the bear's headline is not "the business stopped generating cash." It's "management chose to convert $32B of H1 cash into AI infrastructure." Those are not the same sentence, and the market is pricing the first one.

And the balance sheet means there is no forced-seller dynamic: **$242.5B of cash and short-term investments against $101.1B of debt — net cash of +$141.4B**, current ratio 1.68x. The leverage step-up (debt +105.9%, a $19.0B convertible preferred, $30.5B of equity issued) reads to me as *pre-funding an opportunity set management believes is underpriced*, not as distress. You don't raise equity when you think your stock is overvalued and your projects are marginal.

Two more pre-empts:

- **"The buyback is gone."** Yes — $0 vs $28.31B. But $28B of buybacks was a *capital-return* choice; the same dollars are now in a capex program with a 2027 AI TAM of $1.15 trillion behind it. If you want to argue management is misallocating, argue the ROI — but don't argue the cash disappeared.

My answer: that's precisely why the **24.2% revenue growth and 62% gross margin** matter. Growth is the only thing that dilutes a depreciation wave, and this company is growing faster this quarter than last.

And one free option the bear will never mention: **$124.3B of non-marketable equity securities, up 93.9% in six months, with $85.7B of cumulative upward marks.** Even haircut 25% for tax, that's ~$93B — call it 2%+ of the equity value — sitting in Alphabet's private AI portfolio.
