
Bear Analyst: **Bear rebuttal — GOOGL**

Bull, I’ll grant you the strongest part of your case up front: **GOOGL’s operating business is still growing well.** Revenue up 23–24%, operating income up 30%, operating margin at 35%, and core EPS up roughly 26.7% are real. I’m not going to pretend the franchise is broken.

But you’re making a classic jump: **a good business does not automatically make GOOGL a good investment at $344.98.** The equity story has changed underneath the operating story — negative Q2 free cash flow, a doubling of debt, a halted buyback, dilution, a massive mark-to-market exposure, and a chart that just failed its first test of the falling 50-day SMA. That is not a margin of safety. That is a lot of execution risk priced into a stock that still isn’t cheap on core earnings.

### 1. Your “core EPS” argument is a valuation trap, not a rescue

You concede reported EPS is inflated. Good. But then you pivot to core EPS as if that solves the problem. It doesn’t.

- H1 2026 reported EPS was **$14.24**.
- Your estimated core EPS was about **$5.31**.
- Q2 core EPS was about **$2.69** versus **$9.11** reported.

So roughly **63% of H1 EPS and about 70% of Q2 EPS came from non-operating marks**, not operations. If we annualize that ~$5.31 H1 core EPS, GOOGL is trading around **30–32x core earnings** at $344.98. That is not a broken-company multiple, but it is also not a deep-value setup. It requires the operating momentum to persist, capex to behave, marks not to reverse, and regulation to stay contained.

And the mark-to-market exposure is not trivial. GOOGL carries **$124.3B of non-marketable equity securities**, with **$85.7B of cumulative upward adjustments**. A 10% markdown is roughly **$12.4B pre-tax**; a 20% markdown is roughly **$24.9B**. That can swamp a quarter’s operating beat in headline terms. You call it accounting noise. I call it a source of violent EPS volatility and a valuation mirage that can unwind.

### 2. The cash-flow statement is the real bear case

You say free cash flow is being “deliberately converted into AI infrastructure.” Fine. But the numbers are severe:

- H1 2026 operating cash flow: **$84.9B**, up 32.8%.
- H1 2026 capex: **$80.6B**, up 103.3%.
- H1 free cash flow: **$4.3B**, down **82.4%**.
- Q2 free cash flow: **negative $5.9B**.
- Capex/revenue: **35.1%**.

That is not just a timing issue. Capex incurred but not yet paid rose from **$10.6B in H1 2025 to $29.1B in H1 2026**. And depreciation was only **$13.6B in H1 2026** against **$80.6B of capex**. The depreciation cliff into 2027 is mechanical: as that asset base enters service, D&A rises regardless of demand. You say if capex moderates, FCF snaps back. Maybe. But capex moderation could also signal an AI demand air pocket — and the depreciation bill arrives either way.

You call the capex a moat. I call it a toll. GOOGL is spending like a challenger to defend a franchise. That can be the right strategic move and still be a bad near-term equity setup if free cash flow stays thin and investors start demanding returns on that capital.

### 3. The balance sheet is strong, but the equity story is diluting

Yes, GOOGL has **$242.5B of cash plus short-term investments** against **$101.1B of debt**, for roughly **$141.4B of net cash** — about **$103B adjusted** for leases and preferred stock. That is a fortress. But fortresses don’t automatically protect per-share value.

Look at the direction of travel:

- Debt more than doubled, from **$49.1B to $101.1B**.
- Interest expense rose from **$295M to $1.8B** in H1 — about 6x.
- GOOGL issued **$18.0B of convertible preferred**.
- It raised roughly **$109.5B gross** in six months, including **$30.5B of common stock**.
- Buybacks went from **$28.3B in H1 2025 to zero**.
- Share count rose **1.2%** in six months.

If the stock were deeply undervalued, why did management stop buying it back and instead issue equity-linked capital? Because the AI build is consuming cash. That is a capital-allocation regime change, not a vote of confidence in the equity. The buyback halt removes both an EPS tailwind and a demand support. The preferred adds future dilution. The balance sheet can fund the build, but it does not make GOOGL cheap.

### 4. The chart is a range with a failing overhead trend — not a confirmed base

Bull, you’re right that the short-term tape has stabilized. The 10-day EMA has turned up, MACD’s histogram flipped positive, and the 200-day SMA has been reclaimed. But let’s not overstate it.

- GOOGL closed at **$344.98**, still **below the falling 50-day SMA at $346.54**.
- On Sep 14 it closed above the 50-day for the first time since Aug 10. On Sep 15 it failed back below it. That is the first test, and it failed.
- MACD is **−1.896 versus a signal of −2.811**. Both are still below zero. This is a mean-reversion signal, not a trend confirmation.
- RSI is **51.61** — neutral. There is no oversold cushion if it rolls over.
- VWMA is **$339.46**, below the 20-day SMA at **$341.28** and the 50-day SMA at **$346.54**. That means recent volume-weighted trading has occurred at lower prices than the simple averages imply — that is distribution on a multi-week view, not accumulation.
- The 50/200 spread has collapsed from **$48.93 in July to $9.47 now**. If the recent slopes persist, a death cross comes into view around mid-October. You call that lagging. True. But it still describes a deteriorating intermediate trend, and the falling 50-day is being dragged lower by the July highs rolling off the window.

The 200-day SMA at **$337.07** has held, but it has also been breached twice in two months — July and September — and each reclaim required a sharp bounce. A level tested repeatedly is not a fortress; it is a line the market is probing. If GOOGL closes below **$337.07**, the next zones are **$331.08**, then **$327.74**, then **$317.69**. From $344.98, that is a real downside air pocket.

Your own plan admits the issue: you want to buy **$341.3–$343.5** with a stop at **$335.5–$336.4**. That entry is below the current price and sits almost on top of the 200-day. If it gets there, it may be because the 200-day is failing. And GOOGL has gapped through stops before — **+9.96% on Apr 30** and **−7.1% on Jul 23**. A stop under $337 will not protect against a gap.

### 5. The news and regulatory overhang is sparse — which raises uncertainty, not confidence

You dismiss the headlines as “headline-level and unverified.” I agree they are thin. But that cuts against you too. The news sample is **20 of 188 eligible records**, all from Sept. 14 ET, with no full articles. That is not a basis for a confident bullish catalyst call.

And within that thin sample, the medium-term skew is negative:

- **EU reportedly moving to keep under-15s off social media and ChatGPT** — direct risk to YouTube engagement and ad reach, plus compliance costs.
- **Maryland appealing the ruling that struck down its digital-ad tax** — precedent risk for state-level ad taxes.
- **AI safety pact / slowdown commentary** — could reduce regulatory fragmentation, but could also imply slower frontier-model cadence and tighter constraints. No terms, so you can’t underwrite it as a positive.
- **~$220B of AI-related bond issuance across large tech and credit spreads no longer uniformly similar** — a cost-of-capital and credit-differentiation overhang.
- **YouTube TV subscriber sentiment** — minor, but another consumer-facing irritant.

GOOGL already carries **$17.4B of current loss-contingency accruals**. That is a reserve, not a cap. If EU child-safety rules or Maryland’s tax effort spread, the accrual can grow and the headline risk compounds.

### 6. Direct rebuttals to the bull case

**Bull: “The operating business is accelerating.”**  
Bear: Correct. But the stock pays for that acceleration at roughly **30–32x annualized core EPS**, while Q2 free cash flow is negative. Strong operations do not immunize the equity from multiple compression if cash conversion deteriorates.

**Bull: “Capex is a barrier to entry.”**  
Bear: It is also a capital sink until returns are proven. GOOGL is spending **35% of revenue** on capex. If competitors keep spending, GOOGL has to match. That is a competitive tax on free cash flow, not a clean moat.

**Bull: “Net cash is enormous.”**  
Bear: Yes, but debt doubled, interest expense rose 6x, preferred stock was issued, and buybacks stopped. The balance sheet can fund the build, but it does not make the equity a bargain — especially with **$22.8B of deferred tax liabilities** tied to unrealized gains.

**Bull: “The 200-day SMA is a tested floor.”**  
Bear: It has been breached twice in two months. Each reclaim required a sharp bounce. The first test of the falling 50-day failed immediately on Sep 15. A floor that keeps getting tested is weakening, not strengthening.

**Bull: “The death cross is lagging.”**  
Bear: True. But the 50-day is still falling, the 50/200 spread has compressed by roughly 81%, and the burden of proof is on the bulls. Compression does not tell direction, but the prevailing intermediate trend is down.

**Bull: “Regulatory risks are unverified.”**  
Bear: Exactly. Unknown timeline and unknown financial impact are reasons to demand a discount, not reasons to ignore them. The market has not priced details it doesn’t have.

### Bear stance on GOOGL

I’m not calling GOOGL a broken company. I’m saying the bull case requires too many things to go right at once: capex moderates, free cash flow snaps back, private marks don’t reverse, regulation stays contained, the 200-day holds, and the falling 50-day stops capping price. That is a lot of “ifs” for a stock at roughly **30–32x core EPS** with **negative Q2 FCF** and a failed 50-day test.

At **$344.98**, GOOGL is mid-range with resistance at **$349.1–$351.6** and structural support at **$337.07**. I would not initiate a new long here. Existing holders should consider trimming or hedging. The bear trigger is a daily close below **$337.07**, which opens **$327.74** and then **$317.69**. I would only flip constructive on GOOGL on a decisive close above **$351.60** with volume at least **1.3x average** and MACD pushing above zero — and even then, I’d want a retest, not a chase.

The bull is right that GOOGL is a high-quality franchise. But high quality is not the same as low risk. Right now, GOOGL is a high-quality franchise with a deteriorating cash-flow profile, a mark-to-market earnings distortion, a diluting capital structure, and a fragile technical base. That is enough to stay bearish.
