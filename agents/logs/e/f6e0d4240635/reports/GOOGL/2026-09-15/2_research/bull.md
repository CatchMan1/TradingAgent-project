
Bull Analyst: **Bull opening statement — GOOGL**

Bear, I know the case you’re about to make: headline EPS is inflated, free cash flow got crushed by AI capex, debt doubled, buybacks stopped, the stock is below a falling 50-day SMA, and the regulatory/AI headlines are ugly. Let me start with the strongest evidence: **the core business is getting stronger, not weaker.**

In H1 2026, GOOGL’s derived revenue was **$229.7B, up 23.1% YoY**. Q2 revenue was **$119.8B, up 24.2%** — the fastest growth in the visible dataset. Operating income rose **30.0% to $80.5B**, and operating margin expanded to **35.0%** from 33.1%. Gross margin improved **240 bps** to **62.0%** even while AI infrastructure spending was ramping. That is the opposite of a company losing pricing power.

And yes, reported EPS is distorted by **$135.8B of non-cash investment gains**. I’ll concede that immediately. But strip those out and tax operating income at the reported rate, and GOOGL still generated roughly **$5.31 of core H1 EPS, up about 26.7% YoY**. Q2 core EPS was roughly **$2.69 vs. $9.11 reported** — so the headline number is inflated, but the operating engine underneath is still growing at a mid-20s rate. The bear case needs you to believe the operating business is deteriorating; the data says the opposite.

Google Cloud gives it a direct enterprise AI channel. Gemini and TPUs give it proprietary model and silicon leverage. And the capex number that bears hate — **$80.6B in H1 2026** — is also the barrier to entry. Very few companies can spend at that scale, still generate **$84.9B of operating cash flow**, and hold **$242.5B of cash plus short-term investments** against **$101.1B of debt**. GOOGL ended the period with roughly **$141.4B of net cash**, or about **$103B adjusted** for leases and preferred stock. That is not a balance sheet under stress. That is a balance sheet funding a land grab.

The AI capex debate is real: H1 free cash flow collapsed to **$4.3B** from **$24.3B**, and Q2 FCF was negative **-$5.9B**. But the cash-flow problem is capital intensity, not profitability. Operating cash flow still rose **32.8%**. If capex growth moderates even modestly, FCF snaps back hard. And GOOGL is one of the few hyperscalers that can fund this build without financial distress. That matters when credit markets start differentiating AI spenders.
