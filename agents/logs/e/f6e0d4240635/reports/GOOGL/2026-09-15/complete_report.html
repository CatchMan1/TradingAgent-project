In this case the correct posture is small size, fade the extremes (buy 340–342, sell 349–351), with ATR-based stops.

10. Why These Eight Indicators
  • close_10_ema, close_50_sma, close_200_sma — three genuinely distinct time horizons. The 10 EMA is the live trend of the bounce, the 50 SMA is the falling overhead that defines the correction, and the 200 SMA is the structural floor that has actually been tested and reclaimed multiple times in this dataset. Using all three exposes the compression of the 50/200 spread (48.9 → 9.5 points), which a single average would hide.
  • macd + macdh — chosen instead of macd + macds because the histogram makes the rate of change of the crossover visible (it flipped positive only two sessions ago), which is precisely the information needed to judge whether this is an early reversal or a false start. The signal line value (−2.811) is available in the snapshot for reference without a separate series.
  • rsi — orthogonal confirmation of momentum at a swing juncture (39.4 → 51.6 recovery) and the only tool here that tells us there is no overbought constraint on upside, and no oversold cushion on downside.
  • atr — essential in a compressed-volatility regime both for stop distance and for position sizing. Substituted for the Bollinger trio because Bollinger width is a volatility measure and would have been redundant; the bands themselves are cited from the verified snapshot as price levels.
  • vwma — the only volume-aware tool in the set. The gap between VWMA (339.46) and the simple 50SMA (346.54) is a direct, quantified read on whether recent volume transacted at higher or lower prices, which is the accumulation/distribution question that price alone cannot answer.

Deliberately excluded: boll_ub/boll_lb/boll (duplicative of ATR for volatility), macds (duplicative of macd/macdh), and no stochastic oscillator (redundant with RSI).
