## 1. Executive summary

Alphabet's Q2 2026 10-Q reveals two very different stories that traders must separate:

1. **A strong, accelerating core business.** Derived revenue for H1 2026 was **$229.7B, +23.1% YoY**, with Q2 2026 revenue of **$119.8B, +24.2% YoY** — the fastest growth visible in this dataset. Operating income rose **+30.0%** to **$80.5B** in H1, and operating margin expanded to **35.0%** from 33.1%. Gross margin improved ~240 bps on lower cost of revenue as a share of sales, despite the AI infrastructure build-out.
2. **Headline earnings are massively inflated by non-cash investment gains.** Nonoperating income was **$135.7B in H1 2026**, driven by **$135.8B of debt/equity securities gains** (including $99.0B in Q2 alone). Reported diluted EPS of **$14.24** compares with an estimated **~$5.31 "core" EPS** excluding investment gains. About **63% of H1 EPS and ~70% of Q2 EPS came from non-operating marks**, not operations.

The cash-flow profile has also inflected sharply: **operating cash flow +32.8% to $84.9B**, but **capex doubled to $80.6B**, collapsing **free cash flow to $4.3B (-82.4%)** and turning Q2 FCF **negative (-$5.9B)**. Alphabet funded this with an unprecedented ~$110B of gross capital raising in six months (debt +$56.2B, common stock +$30.5B, convertible preferred +$19.1B, minority interests +$3.8B), while **suspending buybacks entirely** (zero vs. $28.3B in H1 2025).

**Fundamental stance:** constructive on operating momentum and margin execution, but cautious on earnings quality, free-cash-flow compression, dilution, and reversal risk in a $124.3B private-equity portfolio. Headline P/E screens will materially understate the true multiple.

## 2. Company profile

Alphabet Inc. is the parent of Google and one of the world's largest internet and technology companies. Its operating structure is conventionally organized into:

- **Google Services** — Search and other advertising, YouTube ads and subscriptions, Google Play, Android, Chrome, Maps, devices, and subscription platforms.
- **Google Cloud** — Google Cloud Platform infrastructure/data/AI services and Google Workspace productivity software.
- **Other Bets** — long-horizon ventures such as Waymo (autonomous driving), Wing, Verily and related investments.

The company generates the majority of revenue from advertising, with cloud and subscription/platform revenue as the principal growth diversifiers. It is a leading developer of AI models (Gemini family), custom silicon (TPUs) and large-scale data-center infrastructure. **Note:** the SEC fact excerpt returned for this analysis does **not** contain the segment revenue table, so segment-level growth and cloud margin cannot be independently verified here.

GOOGL represents Alphabet's Class A common stock. Class B shares carry disproportionate voting rights; Class C shares trade as GOOG.

The $135.8B securities gain is the single most important item in the release. Supporting balance-sheet evidence points to **remeasurement of non-marketable equity investments**:

- Equity securities **without** readily determinable fair value rose from **$64,094M (12/31/25)** to **$124,259M (6/30/26)**, +93.9%.
- Cumulative **upward** price adjustments on those securities rose from **$44,485M to $85,732M** (+$41.2B).
- Cumulative downward adjustments were roughly flat ($8.82B → $9.12B), indicating no offsetting write-down cycle in the period.
- Realized gains were small ($836M H1), so the bulk of the P&L gain is **unrealized/non-cash**.

**Earnings-quality math (est.):** Stripping all non-operating income and taxing operating income at the reported rate:
- H1 2026 core net income ≈ **$65.1B**, core diluted EPS ≈ **$5.31**.
- H1 2025 core net income ≈ **$51.3B**, core diluted EPS ≈ **$4.19**.
- **Core EPS growth ≈ +26.7% YoY** — still excellent, but roughly one-quarter of the reported +178% EPS growth.
- Q2 2026 core EPS ≈ **$2.69** vs. reported **$9.11**; ~70% of the quarter's EPS was non-operating.

**This is the key valuation trap in GOOGL today:** reported net margin was 76.1% in H1 2026, versus a ~28% core margin. Any screen based on reported P/E will make the stock look dramatically cheaper than its operating earnings justify.

- **Effective tax rate:** 19.1% vs. 17.1%. Deferred tax liabilities jumped from **$919M to $22,819M**, largely reflecting taxable temporary differences on the unrealized investment gains — a signal that cash taxes could rise if those gains are realized.

## 10. Key risks

1. **Earnings-quality/valuation risk (highest).** ~63% of H1 and ~70% of Q2 EPS came from non-cash investment marks. Reported P/E is not comparable to the operating business.
2. **Mark-to-market reversal risk.** $124.3B of non-marketable equity securities with $85.7B of cumulative upward adjustments; a private-market repricing could produce multi-billion-dollar losses with no cash impact but severe headline impact.
3. **FCF compression / capex sustainability.** H1 FCF of $4.3B (-82%) and negative Q2 FCF; if capex exceeds ~$170B annualized, FCF could be negative for the full year.
4. **Depreciation cliff.** $80.6B H1 capex vs. $13.6B H1 depreciation implies substantial future D&A growth and margin pressure.
5. **Rising leverage and funding dependence.** Debt doubled; interest expense up 6x; the company may need further debt/equity issuance, with dilution risk from $18.0B preferred.
6. **Buyback cessation.** Removes a historical EPS tailwind and demand support.
7. **Tax risk.** $22.8B deferred tax liability related to unrealized gains; realization would consume cash.
8. **Legal/regulatory.** $17.4B current loss-contingency accrual; antitrust and privacy exposure remains material.
9. **Rates/credit marks.** $53.2B of AFS securities sit in an unrealized-loss position (though accumulated loss is only $416M).
10. **Competitive/AI capex race.** Qualitative: hyperscaler AI competition could sustain elevated capex without commensurate near-term returns.

## 11. Actionable insights for traders

1. **Re-base valuation on core EPS.** Use ~$5.31 H1 2026 core EPS (~$10.6–11.5 annualized run-rate before seasonality), not the $14.24 reported figure. Reported EPS overstates run-rate earnings by roughly **2.7x**. If consensus or screeners pick up the headline number, expect a valuation "mirage" that can unwind.
2. **Treat the $135.8B gain as non-recurring.** It is non-cash, largely unrealized, and mark-driven. Do not extrapolate it into 2027 estimates. Conversely, do not dismiss the operating beat underneath it.
3. **Focus the next catalyst on three Q3 datapoints:** (a) capex trajectory and any upward guidance revision; (b) whether H1's FCF compression persists or turns negative for the full year; (c) any resumption of buybacks or further equity/preferred issuance.
4. **Watch gross margin as the AI-monetization scoreboard.** The 240 bp H1 gross-margin improvement despite soaring compute costs is a genuinely bullish signal; a reversal would indicate AI costs are outrunning monetization.
5. **Position sizing around mark-to-market volatility.** Reported EPS will swing violently with private valuations. Options-implied volatility around earnings may be mispriced if the market assumes operating-driven results.
6. **Monitor dilution.** Share count +1.2% in six months, $18.0B preferred, and no buyback. A continuation of this pattern is a structural headwind to per-share value.
7. **Balance-sheet optionality is real.** ~$141B net cash (≈$103B adjusted for leases/preferred) gives Alphabet the capacity to keep funding the build-out without distress — this limits downside tail risk even if FCF stays thin.
8. **Key thresholds to flag:** annualized capex >$170B; full-year FCF <$0; net cash <$100B; any markdown ≥10% of the $124B non-marketable portfolio; or a leverage ratio (debt/assets) above ~15%.

**Net:** The operating franchise is performing better than at any point in the visible history (+23% revenue, 35% operating margin, +33% OCF). But the equity story has become a financing-and-capex story, with reported earnings disconnected from cash economics and per-share returns now diluted rather than reduced. A constructive fundamental bias is warranted **only** with explicit adjustment for investment gains and close monitoring of free cash flow and dilution.
