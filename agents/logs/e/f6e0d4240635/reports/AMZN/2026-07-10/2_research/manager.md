**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Weighing the two sides, the bull won the argument about the business and the bear won the argument about the stock at $245.34 — and since the question is what to do with the position now, the bear's case carries the decision.

The bull's strongest ground is genuine and I'm not dismissing it: revenue +16.6% YoY to $181.52B, operating income +29.6% to $23.85B, operating margin up ~130 bps to 13.1%, gross margin +120 bps to 51.8%, and operating cash flow compounding $84.95B → $115.88B → $139.51B → $148.53B TTM. The bull also correctly pre-empted the earnings-quality attack by conceding the ~$12.3B private-equity mark-up and resting the case on operating income instead — that was intellectually honest and it landed. The balance sheet rebuttal also mostly holds: ~$144B of cash and securities against ~$121.9B of debt, a still-modest debt-to-assets ratio, and interest income ($1.14B) still exceeding interest expense ($0.80B).

But the bear took the three points that actually decide the trade. First, capital intensity is now outstripping the earnings engine: capex of $44.20B in Q1 against operating income of $23.85B — 1.85x — with TTM capex of $151.0B and TTM FCF of −$2.47B, and D&A already $70.44B TTM and rising. The bull asserted "high-ROIC AI infrastructure" but never produced ROIC; there is no segment-level return data in this debate, so that is faith, not evidence. Second, the financial-risk regime did change: debt from ~$68.4B to ~$121.9B in a single quarter, a record $53.4B long-term issuance, interest expense +48% YoY, plus $91.62B of operating and $13.33B of finance lease liabilities — the bull's "AAA-adjacent" label has no rating evidence behind it and the bear was right to call that out. Third, the technical picture is unconfirmed, not "screaming recovery": price is below a declining 50-SMA at $254.03, the July 10 session tagged the upper Bollinger band near $251.43 and closed at $245.34 near the low on only 31.65M shares versus 248M on the June 26 quad-witching reversal, and the MACD crossover occurred below the zero line (−1.523 vs. −3.276). Rising price on falling volume after a rejection candle is a divergence, not a basing pattern. The bull's own framing ("needs a close above ~$254") concedes the bear's point.

Valuation seals it. On the bear's core-EPS math (~$1.7–1.8, stripping the mark-up), AMZN is 34–36x annualized core earnings with negative FCF — and the private-equity book it is marking up ballooned from $16.2B to $48.1B, which is a reversible valuation, not a durable earnings stream. Layer on February 2026's earnings gap (222.69 → 202.70 open on 179M shares, low ~199.60), and the "loaded catalyst calendar" the bull cites is symmetric binary risk into an event, not an edge.

I stopped short of Sell because the bear never claimed the franchise is impaired — its own triggers ($237, $227) describe a stock that may just keep ranging, and the operating momentum genuinely argues against exiting entirely. What the evidence supports is reducing exposure and refusing to add at this price until the technicals and the cash-flow math confirm. That is Underweight, sized meaningfully but not maximally.

Caveats on the evidence: no segment-level ROIC or AWS/retail profitability split, no rating agency data, no insider-transaction data, and the BofA target contains an internally inconsistent level ($165 against a ~$247 stock) that should be treated as direction-only, not as a price anchor.

The trim should be executed into strength, not into a breakdown; use any push toward the $251–254 resistance band as the preferred exit zone for the trimmed shares.
