**As of 2026-09-14 | Latest filing: Q2 2026 Form 10-Q (period ended 2026-06-30, accepted 2026-07-31)**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Amazon's Q2 2026 filing shows a company in the middle of an **extraordinary capital-deployment cycle** funded increasingly with debt, while core operating momentum is simultaneously accelerating. Three things dominate the fundamental picture:

1. **Core operations are inflecting higher.** Q2 2026 revenue reached **$200.6B (+19.6% YoY)** and operating income **$27.5B (+43.2% YoY)**, lifting operating margin to **13.7% (+226bp)** — *despite* depreciation rising 31% YoY. Incremental operating margin was ~25%.
2. **Reported EPS is heavily distorted by a non-cash mark-up on private equity holdings.** Q2 diluted EPS of **$5.75** includes roughly **$3.59/share** of pre-tax mark-to-market gains on equity securities without a readily determinable fair value (a $50.5B upward adjustment in the quarter; $62.8B in H1). That asset line ballooned from **$16.2B to $122.3B (+655%)** in six months. Core, operations-only EPS is approximately **$1.95 (+26% YoY)**.
3. **Free cash flow has turned negative.** TTM operating cash flow of **$161.4B** was outrun by **$173.0B** of capex (Q2 capex alone: **$54.2B, 27.0% of revenue**), producing **TTM FCF of –$11.6B** versus **+$7.7B in FY2025 (derived)**. The gap is being closed with debt: long-term debt rose **+93% in six months, from $68.8B to $133.0B**, plus $13.5B of finance leases.

**Bottom line for traders:** the operating business is performing better than at any point in its history on margin, and that is the durable signal. But the headline EPS, the balance-sheet expansion, and the free-cash-flow profile are all being driven by items that are either non-cash (equity marks) or forward-dated (AI/data-center capex). Position sizing should reflect a company whose earnings quality is bifurcated and whose financial leverage is rising fast.

### 4.2 What is actually driving the P&L

**Cost discipline is real and broad-based.** Cost of goods grew 18.5% against 19.6% revenue growth; marketing grew only 2.5% and fell from 6.8% to 5.8% of revenue; G&A actually *declined* 6.0%. Gross margin expanded 45bp and operating margin 226bp **after** absorbing a 31% increase in depreciation. This is operating leverage, not accounting.

**Incremental margins are excellent.** H1 2026 incremental operating margin = Δ operating income $13.74B / Δ revenue $58.76B = **23.4%** (Q2 alone: **25.2%**). That is high-quality growth.

**Depreciation is the emerging headwind.** TTM D&A reached **$75.2B (+28.4% YoY)**; H1 2026 D&A of $38.9B grew 32.0%. Critically, **H1 capex of $98.4B is 2.53× H1 D&A**. When capex runs at more than twice depreciation, D&A growth of 25%+ annually persists for multiple years regardless of what happens to demand. Margin expansion must outrun this — so far it has, but the cushion is thinning.

**SBC is being managed down.** H1 SBC of $10.07B (–1.5% YoY) is now just **2.6% of revenue** vs 3.2% a year ago; unrecognized SBC is $24.8B. Dilution is modest (+0.5% shares in six months). This is shareholder-friendly relative to peers.

**Tax rate normalization is a headwind to EPS optics.** The Q2 ETR jumped from 12.8% to 22.5% (H1: 17.0% → 23.0%). Even on core earnings, this absorbs a meaningful slice of the operating improvement. Cash taxes were only **$3.98B in H1** against $27.8B of book tax expense, with **deferred tax expense of $30.5B** — evidence the equity mark-ups created a large deferred tax liability rather than a current cash obligation.

**Trading implication:** any screen or consensus figure using GAAP P/E on this print is measuring a private-markup event, not Amazon. Use the **$1.95 Q2 / $3.63 H1 core EPS** for run-rate earnings power, and treat the $122.3B stake as a separate, volatile asset value (with an associated deferred tax liability on eventual disposal).

### 7.2 Liquidity, leverage, and working capital

- **Liquidity:** $78.2B cash + $97.9B AFS debt securities = **~$176B of liquid resources**. The maturity ladder shows **$96.5B of AFS securities maturing within one year**, so most of that portfolio is short-dated and genuinely liquid.
- **Gross debt:** $133.0B long-term debt (incl. $3.3B current) + $13.5B finance leases = **~$146.4B**.
- **Net debt:** **–$29.7B net cash** if all investments are counted; **+$68.2B net debt** on a cash-only basis. Either way, leverage is rising fast from a previously net-cash position.
- **Debt / annualized H1 operating income:** ~146.4 / 102.6 = **~1.4×** — still comfortable, but the trajectory (+93% debt in six months) matters more than the level.
- **Interest coverage:** H1 operating income $51.3B / net interest expense $2.11B = **~24×**. Interest expense doubled YoY ($1.06B → $2.11B) and will keep climbing with the new debt.
- **Current ratio: 1.03×** (249.3/241.3), down from 1.05× at year-end. Working capital is thin — Amazon runs on negative-to-neutral working capital by design, but the cushion is narrowing.
- **Receivables are the one working-capital warning.** AR grew **+30.1%** while H1 revenue grew **+18.2%**. AR now equals **43.9 days of Q2 revenue**. Watch collections and the allowance ($2.80B, up from $2.40B, +16.7%).
- **Inventory is tightly managed** (–0.4% while revenue +18%) and inventory valuation reserves *fell* from $3.30B to $3.00B — no obsolescence build.
- **Deferred revenue is flat** ($25.0B → $24.9B). This is not where AWS backlog sits, but it provides no incremental evidence of accelerating committed demand.
- **Derivatives:** derivative assets jumped from $112M to $705M, including 270 GWh of energy contracts — consistent with power procurement for data centers.

## 10. Actionable Takeaways & What to Watch

1. **Re-base your earnings model to core EPS.** Use **$1.95 (Q2)** and **$3.63 (H1)** ex-non-operating, not $5.75/$8.53. Applying a normal multiple to core EPS produces a materially different valuation than headline GAAP numbers imply. Conversely, add the **$122.3B stake (~$11.3/share pre-tax, less deferred tax)** as a separate sum-of-the-parts asset rather than capitalizing it in earnings.
2. **The single variable that matters is capex efficiency.** Q2 capex of $54.2B against Q2 incremental operating income of $8.3B is the whole thesis in two numbers. Watch for the first quarter where capex/revenue stops rising (currently 27.0%). Stabilization would be a powerful positive catalyst; a further step-up with decelerating AWS growth would be the key short thesis.
3. **Monitor the funding mix quarterly.** Debt went from $68.8B to $133.0B in six months. A third consecutive quarter of large net issuance — especially if the equity-stake marks stop contributing to book equity — would signal rising refinancing and interest risk.
4. **Track receivables and deferred revenue.** AR at 43.9 days of revenue and deferred revenue flat at $25B are the two early-warning lines for demand quality.
5. **Treat the equity mark-up as a mark-to-market pair trade, not earnings.** A $50.5B quarterly swing in a private valuation is a both-directions risk; it inflates book value and the deferred tax balance.
6. **Catalyst calendar:** Q3 2026 results (quarter ends 2026-09-30, report expected late October 2026) — first read on whether the capex ramp is matched by revenue, and whether the equity stake is marked again.
7. **Insider signal is absent.** The vendor has no authorized insider-transaction archive for AMZN as of this date; do not assume neutral insider sentiment — it is simply unobserved.
