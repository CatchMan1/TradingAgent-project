
Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for AMZN — Opening Statement

Let me start where the bear will inevitably start, because I want to take the strongest punch off the table before I make my case: **yes, trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is negative — minus $11.6 billion.** I'm not going to hide from that. But if that single line is the bear's headline, then the bear is analyzing a company that stopped existing two years ago and ignoring the most important financial inflection AMZN has posted in a decade. Let me show you why.

## The operating business is accelerating — not decelerating

Here's what actually happened in the most recent reported quarter (Q2 2026, filed July 31):

- **Revenue: $200.6B, up 19.6% year-over-year** — and that's *acceleration* from Q1's +16.6%. On a ~$802B annualized run-rate.
- **Operating income: $27.5B, up 43.2%.**
- **Operating margin: 13.7%, up 230 basis points.**
- **Incremental operating margin: ~24%.** Every new dollar of revenue is dropping 24 cents to the operating line.

That is not a company in decline. That is a company that has crossed an efficiency threshold. And here's the part that separates real operating leverage from accounting flattery: **SBC fell 1.5% and G&A fell 3.9% while revenue grew 18%.** This margin expansion is not comp-driven, not one-time, and not a mix illusion. Stock comp as a percentage of revenue dropped from 3.16% to 2.64%. G&A dropped from 1.73% to 1.41%. Marketing dropped 79 basis points. The cost discipline is structural.

And the cash engine is roaring: **operating cash flow of $71.4B in H1, up 44% year-over-year**, at an 18.7% OCF margin. TTM OCF of $161.4B, up 33%. That's the number the bear conveniently skips past on the way to the FCF line.

## The "negative FCF" argument confuses investment with deterioration

Now let's do the FCF math honestly. TTM capex is $173B — 26% of revenue, up from ~18%. That's why FCF is negative. But **capex is a discretionary growth investment, not a cost of doing business.** And the return on it is visible in the incremental margin: 24 cents of operating income per new revenue dollar.

Here's the framing that matters. The bear says "AMZN is the weakest FCF converter among big tech" — that's a claim from a single comparative clause in an *Alphabet* article, with zero AMZN figures, no period definition, and no sourcing. It's a narrative datapoint, not analysis. And notice what it ignores: **a company deliberately converting cash into AI infrastructure at the exact moment the industry's demand signals are screaming.** In the same news sample: OpenAI raising $30B at a $1.4T valuation, Anthropic reportedly expanding infrastructure at massive scale. That's not a market where you want to be the player *under*-investing. AMZN is choosing to build. That's the right call, and the market has historically rewarded it.

The corollary the bear won't mention: if the data-center skeptics are right and half the planned capacity never gets built, **AMZN's capex discipline improves and FCF inflects positive faster.** The FCF bear case and the overbuild bear case are contradictory. You can't hold both.

## The balance sheet can fund this all day

The debt doubled — $133B, up 93% in six months. Scary headline. Now the context:

- **Cash + marketable securities: $176.1B.** Net cash position of ~$29.6B *excluding* operating leases.
- **Interest coverage: 24x** (H1 operating income $51.3B vs $2.1B interest expense).
- Even including $96B of operating leases, net obligations are under 0.5x annualized EBITDA.

This is a fortress balance sheet releveraging at investment-grade rates to fund a generational build-out. The interest expense doubling sounds dramatic in percentage terms, but at 24x coverage it's a rounding error against the operating profit trajectory. The bear is treating a deliberate capital structure decision as a solvency warning. It isn't one.

## The hidden asset the bear keeps calling a risk

Here's the part I find most compelling, and it's the piece the bear will try to spin as pure danger: **AMZN holds $122.3B of non-marketable equity — 11% of total assets, roughly equal to its entire long-term debt.** That's the Anthropic-type AI portfolio. The bear will say "mark-to-model risk, earnings quality, reversal danger." Fine — but consider the other side of that coin:

- That book is **latent, unrecognized optionality** the market is not pricing.
- It can be **monetized** through IPO/mark-up events to fund capex *without* more debt.
- It represents a **strategic stake in the AI ecosystem** that generates both returns and commercial alignment.

Yes, the $50.5B Q2 mark inflated GAAP EPS. That's why I don't underwrite GAAP. I underwrite **core EPS of ~$1.95 in Q2, up ~23% year-over-year** — and that's the *real* earnings engine, growing at 22–23% with expanding margins. The headline "+242% EPS" is noise; the "+23% core" is signal, and 23% is excellent. On core EPS, AMZN trades at roughly 32x a run-rate of ~$7.8 — for a business growing operating income at 43%. That is not an expensive stock. On GAAP TTM ($12.55), it's ~20x.
