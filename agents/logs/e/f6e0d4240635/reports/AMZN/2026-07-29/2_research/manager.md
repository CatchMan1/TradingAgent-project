**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: Let me walk through how I landed here, because this round was genuinely well-fought on both sides.

First, what is NOT in dispute: AMZN is a very good business right now. Both analysts accept revenue +16.6%, operating income +29.6%, gross margin +127 bp, operating margin +132 bp, SBC down 11.6% y/y, inventory down 4.7% sequentially. The bear conceded this outright. So the debate was never "is AMZN a good company." It was "what are you paying, and what's coming." That's where the bull lost the round.

The bull's case rested on two load-bearing pillars, and the bear demolished both on the merits.

Pillar one — the "$569.3B contracted revenue backlog." This was a factual inversion. The fundamentals report is explicit that the $569.3B (and the $57.1B due inside twelve months) is largely AWS compute/power/chip purchase commitments — it's money Amazon owes on a fixed schedule, not money customers owe Amazon. The bull relabeled the single biggest fixed-cost liability on the balance sheet as demand visibility. That's the error the whole "it's a choice, and a good one" section was built on. Correct it, and the FCF/leverage section turns from clever to dangerous.

Pillar two — "the depreciation wave already arrived because margins expanded anyway." The bear's arithmetic is decisive: TTM D&amp;A is ~$70.4B against a TTM capex run-rate of $151.0B — depreciation is running at less than half of capex. Q1 2026 capex of $44.2B, up 76.7% y/y, is still being built and enters the depreciation schedule when it's placed in service, quarters from now. The bull's own framing — "D&amp;A growing 32.8% vs revenue 16.6% cannot persist indefinitely" — confirmed the bear's timing: the squeeze is ahead in 2027, not behind in Q1 2026. That's a forward margin headwind sitting inside a stock already being re-rated for spending too much.

Then the balance-sheet turn, which I weighed heavily because it's the actual driver of the multiple: net cash fell from +$63.8B to +$22.1B in a single quarter, LT debt went $68.8B → $121.9B (nearly doubled), interest expense +47.9% y/y, and capex incurred but not yet paid rose to $27.0B from $16.8B — which means reported FCF of −$2.5B understates how bad real FCF is. And the trajectory matters more than the level: FCF +$32.9B → +$7.7B → −$2.5B while capex grows +82% versus OCF +28%. The decade-long "cash machine deleverages" identity just reversed in one quarter, and multiple repricing — not business deterioration — is what's moving this stock. The bear won that.

On technicals, the bear won the methodological point cleanly: the rising 200 SMA is the most backward-looking line on the page and will keep rising mechanically while price falls near a top. The leading evidence — MACD at −3.75 with a histogram expanding every session (−0.297 → −1.986), distribution volume heavy on declines and light on advances, price below the 10 EMA/50 SMA/200 SMA/VWMA and the lower Bollinger band, and explicitly no confirmed bullish divergence — all points the same way. The bull's own comparison to June actually illustrated the bear's point: June was a low-volatility base; this is accelerating downside into expanding distribution. And the bull's risk/reward was cherry-picked at both ends — the downside doesn't stop at 219.62, it runs 221.25 → 219.62 → 213.77 → 209.77 once 225.55 gives way, so a "3.1% stop" is really −6% to −9.6% risk; meanwhile the +7% to +12.5% reward has to claw through seven layers of overhead supply the bull himself just described.

Finally, the event. Q2 prints 2026-07-30. The dataset provides zero Q2 information — so the "binarily positive catalyst" framing is unsupported; binary is symmetric. The news flow into the print is specifically about Microsoft/Amazon possibly overspending on data centers, i.e. the bear's exact thesis, and with FY2026 capex tracking above $175B against negative FCF, a capex raise is a textbook sell-the-print setup. And the −8.98% single-day open precedent on 2026-02-06 means a 3% stop at 225.55 offers the illusion of defined risk, not defined risk — you get filled far below it.

So why Underweight rather than Sell? Because the franchise quality is real and largely conceded, the 17.6% de-rating has already happened, and RSI 33.31 with price sitting on tested support at 225.55 with a marginally higher low (226.16), plus this dataset's own June precedents of 8–13 point reflex bounces from RSI 32–37, mean an outright exit risks getting shaken out at the worst possible spot into a violently positive print. The 50 SMA at 246.97 is also still above the 200 SMA. A full Sell would require conviction that the business is impaired; it isn't — the balance sheet is levered, not distressed (net cash still positive, ~30x interest coverage, ROE ~21.4%).

Verdict: the bear wins the argument that matters over the next few months — asymmetric downside into a binary print, a forward depreciation ramp, a leverage reversal, and negative FCF — but not by enough to abandon a franchise this good after a 17.6% discount. Trim, don't exit, and let the two triggers decide.
