## 1. Executive Summary

- **Operating momentum is strong and accelerating.** Q2 2026 revenue reached **$200.6B, +19.6% YoY** (Q1 2026: +16.6%), while operating income rose **+43.2% to $27.5B** and operating margin expanded **+226 bp to 13.7%**. H1 2026 operating income was **$51.3B (+36.6%)** on revenue of **$382.1B (+18.2%)**.
- **Headline GAAP earnings are heavily distorted by a non-cash mark-up.** H1 2026 net income of **$92.9B ($8.53 diluted EPS)** includes **$62.8B of pre-tax "upward price adjustment" on equity securities without readily determinable fair value** ($50.5B in Q2 alone). Excluding that mark, adjusted H1 EPS is roughly **$4.09**, and "core" operating EPS (excluding all non-operating items) is roughly **$3.63** — still up ~26% YoY.
- **Cash generation is growing, but capex is outrunning it.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was **$71.4B (+44.2% YoY)**, but purchases of productive assets reached **$98.4B (+72.0%)**, producing **free cash flow of -$27.0B** vs -$7.7B in H1 2025. Trailing-twelve-month FCF is **-$11.6B**.
- **The balance sheet doubled down on AI-era investment.** Total assets jumped **+33.9% in six months to $1,095.7B**, driven by a **$106.1B increase in equity securities without readily determinable fair value** (to $122.3B), **+$52.1B in marketable securities**, and ~$59.5B of net capex. Long-term debt nearly doubled to **$133.0B**; interest expense rose **+100% YoY** in H1.
- **Liquidity remains solid.** Cash + marketable securities = **$176.1B**; net cash excluding operating leases is approximately **$29.7B**. Insider-transaction data is **not available** in the authorized archive and is not inferred here.

## 2. Company Profile

**AMZN (Amazon.com, Inc.)** is a global technology and e-commerce conglomerate organized around **three reportable operating segments** (confirmed by the 10-Q segment count of 3):

1. **North America** — online and physical retail, third-party marketplace, Prime subscriptions, advertising, and logistics.
2. **International** — the same commerce and advertising model across non-U.S. geographies.
3. **Amazon Web Services (AWS)** — cloud compute, storage, database, networking, analytics, and AI/ML infrastructure and services.

Supporting businesses include advertising (a high-margin profit engine), subscription services, devices (Echo, Kindle, Fire), logistics/fulfillment, healthcare, and a large portfolio of equity investments — the latter now a material balance-sheet item. The company is headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and is one of the largest employers and logistics operators in the world.

**Business-model strengths visible in the data:** negative cash-conversion cycle (~-67 days), dominant cloud franchise, advertising mix shift, and scale-driven operating leverage.

## 5. Earnings Quality: The $62.8B Mark-to-Market Is the Headline

This is the single most important analytical adjustment for AMZN right now.

| Mark-up per diluted share (pre-tax) | ~$4.63 | — | ~$5.77 | — |
| Mark-up per diluted share (after ~23% tax) | ~$3.59 | — | ~$4.44 | — |
| **Adjusted EPS ex-mark** | **~$2.16** | ~$1.68 | **~$4.09** | ~$3.26 |
| **Core operating EPS** (op income × (1−tax rate) ÷ shares) | **~$1.95** | ~$1.55 | **~$3.63** | ~$2.89 |

**Key takeaways:**

1. **~62% of Q2 reported EPS and ~52% of H1 reported EPS came from a non-cash mark-up**, not operations.
2. On a like-for-like basis, adjusted EPS grew **~29% in Q2** and **~26% in H1** — strong, but less than half the headline growth rate.
3. **The mark-up is non-cash and reversible.** It added to book equity but generated **deferred tax expense** ($30.5B in H1), not cash taxes (cash taxes paid were only $4.0B in H1).
4. **Sensitivity:** A 20% decline in the $122.3B equity holding implies a ~$24.5B pre-tax hit (~$18.8B after tax, ~**$1.73/share**); a 50% decline implies ~$61.2B pre-tax (~**$4.32/share**) — more than a full half-year of core operating EPS. This is a genuine GAAP-earnings risk unrelated to the retail/AWS business.

## 10. Catalysts, Risks, and Actionable Insights

### Catalysts to Watch
1. **Q3 2026 earnings (late October 2026)** — first read on whether the 13%+ operating margin holds as depreciation ramps; watch AWS growth, advertising, and holiday-quarter guidance.
2. **FY2026/FY2027 capex guidance** — the single biggest swing factor for FCF and sentiment.
3. **Any valuation event or IPO involving the large equity holding** — could crystallize or reverse the $62.8B H1 mark.
4. **Debt issuance/refinancing** — $133B LTD and rising interest expense; watch credit spreads and maturity ladder.
5. **Holiday retail season and consumer macro** — tariffs, FX, and demand elasticity.

### Key Risks
1. **Earnings-quality reversal:** A markdown of the $122.3B equity stake could produce a large GAAP miss with no operating cause. A 50% markdown ≈ **$4.32/share**, larger than a full half-year of core operating EPS.
2. **Capex/FCF risk:** TTM capex $173B (2.3x D&A); FCF is already negative. If AI/cloud demand disappoints, the depreciation tail becomes a multi-year margin drag.
3. **Rising leverage:** LTD +93% in six months; interest expense +100% YoY. Net cash ex-leases has shrunk to ~$30B.
4. **Tax normalization:** ETR rose from 17% to 23%; cash taxes (14% of book tax) will eventually catch up, especially if gains are realized.
5. **Concentration risk:** A single illiquid equity position is ~11% of assets and ~22% of estimated book equity.
6. **Competition/regulatory:** Cloud competition (Microsoft, Google), antitrust scrutiny in the U.S. and EU, and labor/regulatory issues.
7. **Contractual obligations:** $650B total, $78B due within 12 months.

### Actionable Insights for Traders

- **Anchor valuation on operating earnings, not GAAP EPS.** Use ~**$3.63 H1 core operating EPS** (~$7.3 annualized at the H1 run-rate, with H2 seasonality typically stronger) as the earnings base. Reported $8.53 is not a clean representation of the business.
- **The bull case is intact operationally:** revenue +19.6%, op income +43%, op margin +226 bp, SBC and marketing disciplined. If Q3 op margin holds ≥13%, the operating story is stronger than the headline adjustment suggests.
- **The bear case is cash flow and capex:** FCF -$27B H1, -$11.6B TTM, capex 27% of revenue. The stock's next leg likely depends on **capex growth decelerating below OCF growth** — watch the 2027 capex guide for the inflection.
- **Balance-sheet capacity is adequate but no longer ample:** $176B liquidity and ~$30B net cash ex-leases support continued investment, but gross debt of $146B (incl. finance leases) and $243B including operating leases means less room for error.
- **Treat book value and ROE as inflated.** The ~$560B estimated equity includes a $122.3B illiquid mark. Price-to-book and ROE comparisons to history are misleading this year.
- **Position-sizing implication:** The distribution of GAAP outcomes is unusually wide because of the mark-to-market stake. Consider sizing for a potential headline EPS miss driven by a stake write-down even if operating results beat.

## 12. Bottom Line

**AMZN's operating business is performing very well:** revenue growth reaccelerated to ~20%, operating income grew 43% in Q2, and margins expanded meaningfully on advertising/AWS mix and cost discipline. However, the **reported earnings are not the operating earnings** — a $62.8B non-cash mark-up on an illiquid equity stake accounts for roughly half of H1 EPS, and the same stake now represents ~$122B of balance-sheet value subject to reversal.

At the same time, Amazon has entered its heaviest investment cycle ever: **$98.4B of H1 capex, TTM capex of $173B, negative FCF, and long-term debt that nearly doubled to $133B.** The balance sheet can support this, but the margin for error has narrowed. The key forward debate is whether AI/cloud capex converts into sufficiently high-return revenue before depreciation and interest costs compress margins and free cash flow.

**For traders:** separate the operating signal (strong, improving) from the accounting noise (large, reversible mark-up) and the cash-flow signal (deteriorating on capex). The highest-conviction actionable view is that **Q3 operating margin and 2027 capex guidance matter far more than the next GAAP EPS headline** — and that any stake-related write-down could create a headline-driven dislocation in an otherwise healthy operating business.
