
Bear Analyst: **Bear Analyst — Rebuttal on AMZN**

### 1. You’re right that operating income is the cleanest line. That’s exactly why it worries me.

Q2 operating income of **$27.46B, +43.2% y/y**, with margin at **13.69%**, is genuinely good. I’m not going to pretend otherwise. Marketing +2.5%, G&A −6%, SBC −1.5% against revenue +19.6% is real operating leverage.

But here’s the problem: **that operating line is being purchased with enormous capital intensity.**

- LTM capex: **$173.0B, +60.7% y/y**
- Capex as % of operating cash flow: **107%**
- LTM free cash flow: **−$11.6B**
- H1-2026 FCF: **−$27.0B**
- Q2-2026 FCF: **−$8.82B**
- Capex/revenue: **25.8%** in H1-2026 vs **17.7%** in H1-2025
- Depreciation: **+44.1% y/y** in Q2, and capex is running at **2.3x D&A**

You call 2.3x D&A “building a second business.” I call it **a locked-in depreciation headwind**. Maintenance capex roughly tracks D&A. When you spend 230% of depreciation, you are making a forward bet that AI compute demand will earn a return above the cost of capital. That may prove right — but it is not in the current financials. What *is* in the financials is negative FCF, rising depreciation, and a funding gap being closed with debt.

And your “$650B contracted backlog” is not a backlog. It is **total contractual obligations** — $650.0B, or 4.0x LTM operating cash flow — including purchase commitments, leases, and capacity contracts. Those are obligations AMZN has taken on, not necessarily customer revenue already committed to AMZN. You’re reading a liability note as a demand backlog. That’s a meaningful analytical leap.

### 3. The balance sheet is still fine — but the direction is wrong.

You cite **+$29.6B net cash ex-leases** and **20.9x interest coverage**. Fair. AMZN is not distressed.

But look at the trend:

- Gross long-term debt: **$68.8B → $133.0B, +93.2%** in six months
- Interest expense: **+155% y/y** in Q2
- Current ratio: **1.033**, down from 1.051
- Cash: down **$23.8B** in Q2 alone
- Acquisitions: **$41.96B LTM**, versus $1.65B in the prior H1
- Receivables: **+30.1% YTD** vs revenue +19.6%, with allowance coverage falling from **3.54% to 3.18%**

Including operating leases, the “net cash” comfort flips to net debt. None of this is fatal. But the bull case treats the balance sheet as static strength when the actual data shows **rising leverage, rising interest, rising depreciation, and thinning liquidity**. That is a deteriorating funding profile being masked by a still-strong operating engine.

### 6. Your strongest point is operating leverage. It is not enough to justify the current price.

I’ll grant you the core of the bull case: AMZN’s operating trajectory is strong. Revenue +19.6%, operating margin +226 bps, core EPS growth ~+25.6%, SBC discipline, negative cash conversion cycle. That is a good business.

But the investment case at **$272.26** requires more than “good business.” It requires believing that:

1. The AI capex cycle earns attractive returns;
2. Depreciation and interest do not compress margins more than expected;
3. FCF turns positive before the market loses patience;
4. The optical EPS flattery does not reverse sentiment;
5. The unconfirmed breakout confirms rather than fails.

That is a lot of “ifs” stacked on a stock that is **37x annualized core EPS**, **10% above its 50-SMA**, **15% above its 200-SMA**, with **negative FCF**, **+93% debt growth**, and an **unfilled gap** below it.

### Bottom line: avoid new money in AMZN here. The bull is early, not wrong on the business.

My recommendation is **do not initiate or add to AMZN at $272.26**. If already long, this is a place to tighten risk, reduce size, or hedge — not to chase. A tactical short is defensible only with strict risk control, because the primary trend is still up; but the near-term setup is poor.
