
Bull Analyst: # AMZN — Bull Case: The Market Is Pricing a Capex Bust That the Numbers Say Isn't Happening

The bear hasn't taken the podium yet, so let me do him a favor and state his case for him — because it's a case I've heard before, and it's the wrong read of this company.

The bear's brief writes itself from the tape: AMZN is 11.3% off its August 3 high, it just closed below its 50-day for the first time since July, MACD crossed below zero, RSI has no oversold cushion, there's a 1.7%-wide resistance wall from 254.95 to 259.38, and the fundamental backdrop shows negative free cash flow, debt up 93% in six months, and $650 billion of contractual obligations. Add in oil above $101, 10-year yields at multi-year highs, a reported Ohio data-center incentive review, and Ray Dalio muttering "bubble." That's a tidy bearish checklist.

Here's why it doesn't hold up.

---

## 1. The long-term trend is intact — and this is a pullback, not a reversal

Let's start with the structural facts the bear has to explain away. The 200-day SMA is **239.62 and rising every session since late June** (232.51 on June 12 → 239.62 today, +3.1%). The 50/200 spread is **+15.33, or +6.4%** — the golden-cross configuration is fully in force. The higher-low sequence is unbroken: **196.00 → 199.14 → 226.16**. The stock sits **+5.1% above its 200-day** and at the **61st percentile of its 52-week range**. Nothing in the long-term data says "trend over."

What the bear is pointing at — two closes below the 50-day — is real but small. The prior close below that line was **July 30, the session before the earnings gap**. The 50-day is still *rising* at roughly +0.43/session. Two closes beneath a rising medium-term average is a test, not a failure. The bear is extrapolating a two-day event into a trend change. I'll take the six-month structure over the six-session noise.

And here's the asymmetry the bear keeps skipping: from 251.89, the **prior high is 287.20 — +14.0%**. The **200-day structural floor is 239.62 — –4.9%**. That's roughly a **3:1 payoff** if the primary trend simply reasserts. You don't need to be heroic on the near-term direction to like that skew.

---

## 2. The operating business is accelerating — this is the part the bear can't wave away

Show me a cyclical peak that looks like this:

- **Q2-2026 revenue $200.6B, +19.6% y/y** — accelerating from Q1's +16.6%.
- **Q2 operating income $27.46B, +43.2% y/y** — operating income growing more than twice as fast as revenue.
- **Operating margin 13.7% vs 11.4%** a year ago, +230 bp.
- **H1-2026 operating income $51.31B, +36.6%** on revenue +18.2%.
- Gross margin **52.3%**, up. Marketing fell to **5.8% of revenue** from 6.8%. G&A fell to **1.4%** from 1.8%. SBC fell **in absolute dollars** (–7.6% y/y in Q2).

And critically: **this happened while D&A rose 31% y/y.** That's the whole ballgame. The bear's thesis is that AI capex destroys returns through depreciation. But depreciation is already running at **$20.0B in Q2, 10.0% of revenue**, and margins *still* expanded 230 bp. The gross profit and AWS/ads mix is absorbing the capex digestion today — not in some hoped-for 2028.

The bear will say: "Sure, but the reported EPS of $5.75 is fake." **I agree — and I'm not using it.** Strip the $50.5B pre-tax non-cash mark and Q2 adjusted EPS is **~$2.16–2.40, up 29–43% y/y**. H1 adjusted is **~$4.09–4.40, up 25%+**. That's the number to underwrite, it's clean, and it's excellent. The earnings-quality critique is a critique of the *headline*, not of the business. Point it at the mark, not at me.

---

## 3. "Negative free cash flow" is a choice, not a distress signal

This is the bear's loudest line, and it's the most misleading.

TTM operating cash flow is a **record $161.4B, +33.2% y/y**. Capex is **$173.0B, +61%**. FCF is **–$11.6B**. What the bear calls a crisis, I call **27% of revenue being reinvested into revenue-generating AI infrastructure** by a company that generates $161B of operating cash before it even starts.

Look at what that capital buys: **$650B of contractual obligations**, of which **$78.1B is due within 12 months**. The bear reads $650B as a fixed-cost time bomb. I read it as **contracted forward demand** — capacity commitments, content, energy, logistics — the visible backlog of an AI utility build. In a demand shock it cuts the other way; nobody disputes that. But the base case isn't an AI winter, it's AI scarcity, and those commitments are why AWS can monetize capacity as fast as it comes online.

And the debt number needs context, not alarm: **gross debt $133.0B + $13.5B finance leases = $146.4B against $176.1B cash + AFS → still ~$29.7B net cash**, with **~21x interest coverage** in Q2. Amazon levered up to build assets that depreciate over 5–6 years and generate revenue immediately — that is the opposite of a leveraged retailer borrowing to plug a hole. If capex needs to flex down, it can; this is discretionary acceleration, not a solvency spiral.

## 4. The technical setup is a coiled spring at support, with sellers losing conviction

I'll grant the near-term tape is soft — the technical work calls it "short-term bearish, long-term bullish," and I won't pretend otherwise. But look at *how* it's soft:

- **Bollinger bandwidth has collapsed from 29.68% (Aug 10) to 6.25% — a 79% compression.** ATR is down 31% from Aug 10 and 37% from Aug 3. Volatility contraction this extreme resolves, and it resolves *from* the current level.
- **Price is pinned to the lower band at 251.89 vs 251.28 (%B ≈ 3.8%)**, after the **Sept 10 intraday low of 249.58 undercut the band and closed back above it.** That is an undercut-and-recover — the classic short-term stabilization tell.
- **Sept 10's decline came on 25.48M shares — the lightest volume since Aug 26.** Price made a new closing low for the leg on *contracting* volume. That's effort-vs-result divergence: sellers aren't pressing. Downside momentum without downside volume is how legs exhaust.
- **RSI 43.62 is mid-range** — which the bear calls "no cushion," but the flip side is **no overbought exhaustion to work off either**. There's plenty of room to mean-revert upward.

Now look at the upside map. The resistance wall is **254.95–259.38** — and yes, four independent measures cluster there. But a **close above ~259.4 on volume >40M is a genuine trend repair** with the VWMA (258.16) and 20-day (259.38) both reclaimed. From there, the path is **267.45 (38.2% Fib, which tagged the Aug 28 high at 267.56) → 270.7 → 280.1 → 287.20**. The bear wants to short into a rising 200-day with a squeeze at the lower band — that's a tactical trade, not an investment thesis, and he should size it accordingly.

## 5. The news flow the bear will cite is thin, second-hand, and macro — not idiosyncratic

Let's grade the bear's ammunition honestly:

- **Ohio data-center incentive review:** "reportedly," "might," no Amazon-specific dollar figure, no timeline, unclear whether prospective or retroactive — and it's **medium-term and contingent**. That's a *risk signal, not a quantified liability*. If it clips 0.2–1.0% on a quiet tape, that's noise, and AWS is simultaneously signing constructive data-center principles in Canada. Net policy signal: mixed.
- **NTSB/Prime Air headline:** low direct financial materiality at Amazon's scale; ownership and fault unestablished. Sentiment, not math.
- **Dalio "bubble" warning:** commentary with **no AMZN-specific content**. If I'm not allowed to use an unquantified bullish op-ed, the bear isn't allowed to use an unquantified bearish one.
- **The –1.78% on Sept 9:** explicitly attributed to **oil >$101 and 10-year yields at multi-year highs** — a rates/duration and consumer-cost beta move, not an Amazon catalyst.

And the biggest caveat of all: the news sample is **20 of 177 records, all dated Sept 9**. The bear is trying to build a fundamental narrative off 11% coverage with zero records from Sept 3–8. I won't overclaim from it either — but I *will* note that the "no positive catalyst" argument is an artifact of the sample, not a finding about AMZN.

The real catalyst is **Q3-2026 results in late October** — and the setup into it is *better* because an 11.3% drawdown has reset expectations while the operating trajectory (op income +36.6% H1) hasn't blinked.
