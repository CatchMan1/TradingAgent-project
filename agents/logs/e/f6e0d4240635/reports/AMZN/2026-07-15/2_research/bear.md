## 2. The Debt "78% Jump" — The Bull Measured the Wrong Thing

The bull's defense is "0.5x EBITDA, net cash +$8B, highest-quality credit." Three problems:

**First, the direction and rate of change are the story.** Debt went **$68.8B → $122.6B in a single quarter.** Q1 alone issued **$53.4B of LT debt.** Interest expense jumped **+48% YoY to $800M/quarter** *before* the $25B bond sale even lands. This isn't a steady-state credit; it's a balance sheet in its steepest re-leveraging in years. "0.5x EBITDA" is a snapshot of a moving object.

**Second, the bull's "net cash +$8B" excludes the $91.6B of operating lease liabilities.** Include them — as any analyst pricing the full obligation stack should — and AMZN is at roughly **−$84B of net obligations**, not net cash. Add the **$569.3B of contractual obligations with $57.1B due inside 12 months** (his "demand signal") and you have a hard, contractual forward claim on cash flow that gets *more* expensive if AI demand softens.

**Third — and this is the part the bull actually inverted — those $569B of commitments are a LIABILITY, not demand.** They are **purchase obligations** — commitments to *buy* compute, chips, and content. The bull is literally taking a fixed-cost overhang and presenting it to you as a revenue backlog. That's backwards. A company signs forward *purchase* commitments because it *has* to secure capacity, not because it's guaranteed the end-demand. If the AI revenue arrives slower than the capacity does, those commitments are a margin trap, not a moat. He flipped the sign on the risk.
