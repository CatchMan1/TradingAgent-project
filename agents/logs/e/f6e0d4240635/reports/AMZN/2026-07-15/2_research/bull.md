## 4. Taking the Bear's Three Knives Head-On

Let me preempt the objections, because I know exactly where the bear is going.

Yes. And here's why that's **the wrong lens for this company at this moment.** AMZN is in a *deliberate* AI infrastructure supercycle: capex went $52.7B (FY23) → $83B (FY24) → $131.8B (FY25) → annualizing ~$177B. Here's the empirical precedent the bear ignores: **Amazon did exactly this in 2022 and came out the other side with explosive FCF.** Capex is a leading indicator of future revenue, not a sign of distress. And critically — the operating cash flow funding it is *strong*: Q1 OCF was **$26B, up +53% YoY.** This isn't a company burning cash it can't generate; it's a company *choosing* to reinvest at high returns. That's what a ~25–27% ROE business should do.

### "Debt exploded 78% in one quarter to $122.6B!"

Let me put that in perspective, because the absolute number is scary and the *ratio* is not. Net obligations including leases are roughly **0.5x EBITDA.** Amazon has **$144B of cash and marketable securities** against that debt — a strict net-cash position of ~+$8B. This is one of the highest-quality credits on earth borrowing at investment-grade rates to fund the single largest secular growth opportunity of the decade (AI compute). 

That $25B bond sale the news flagged? It's **smart financing** — locking in capital to build AWS capacity while the AI demand is red-hot. If you're a bear arguing Amazon shouldn't borrow at ~5% to fund AI infrastructure generating 25%+ returns on equity, you're arguing against the best capital allocation in the market. The leverage *direction* is worth watching, yes — I'll grant that. But 0.5x EBITDA is not a red flag; it's dry powder being deployed.

### "The earnings are distorted by a $12.3B mark-up!"

*Already addressed — and I'll say it again for the cheap seats:* core EPS was ~$1.92, +21% YoY. The operating income growth of +29.6% is real cash-generating operating profit. The mark-up is noise on the headline; the engine underneath is pristine. ### "New York's data-center moratorium and 14 states considering caps!"

Fair to flag, and I'll own it as a real medium-term thematic risk. But let's be precise: **NY is not a core AWS data-center state**, and AWS runs a *global* footprint. The direct near-term P&L impact is small. The diffusion risk (14 states "considering") is the thing to monitor over *quarters*, not a July-15 price catalyst.
