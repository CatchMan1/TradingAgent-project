
Neutral Analyst: I’ll take the middle here, but not because the middle is comfortable. The aggressive analyst is right about the most important thing: holding a full AMZN position through a 15.4% bandwidth coil into NVDA earnings and an inflation print is not a passive, neutral choice. It is an active full-size bet. He is also right that the operating business is genuinely inflecting: operating income up 43.2% on revenue up 19.6%, margin from 11.4% to 13.7%, SBC down in dollars, and marketing growing far slower than revenue. That is real, and it is why I would not abandon AMZN entirely.

But the aggressive analyst then overplays his hand. He uses a bearish asymmetry argument to defend keeping 50–60% of a standard weight, and then adds a tactical short below 257.04. You cannot simultaneously say the payoff is skewed down and that the right answer is to keep a half-weight long plus a new short. The short is not a hedge; it is a second directional bet in a name that went from 226.65 to 284.02 in three sessions on 129 million and 91 million shares. The nominal risk looks like five points, but the realized risk in an AI-complex melt-up is whatever the gap opens at. And his claim that a descending triangle “resolves in the direction of the pressure” is pattern folklore, not a base rate. The lower highs are real; the inference that AMZN must break down is not. His limit at 265.53 has the same flaw the conservative correctly identifies: in the scenario we are worried about, that order never fills, so the caller ends up holding more AMZN than the plan intended through the exact event that motivated the trim. And 266.10 is not a stop-loss for a residual long position. It is a rebuild or invalidation level. A “stop” above spot is not risk control; it is a trigger.

The conservative analyst is right on execution and right to reject the tactical short. If you have decided to reduce AMZN, do not leave a meaningful tranche hostage to a 5.25-point bounce. Complete the de-risk at market. He is also right that 266.10 is noise-tight as a stop in a name with a 6.82 ATR, and that position size, not a tight stop, is the primary risk control. But he overcorrects. He treats the $122.3B non-marketable equity stake as almost pure accounting artifact and uses that to argue for a 40–50% standard-weight residual. The marks do cut both ways. A 25% markdown is a real $30B pre-tax hit and a $2.20-a-share GAAP swing, but an up-round is the same in reverse, and the operating business is not an artifact. The 50 SMA at 250.88 has risen eighteen straight sessions, the 200 SMA at 238.53 has risen every session in the dataset, and the 50/200 spread is widening. Record-low volume at 21.53M is not automatically bullish, but it does mean the pullback is not confirmed distribution. A 40–50% standard-weight residual is defensible, but it is more defense than the evidence requires. His hard exit on the first daily close below 257.04 is also too rigid. That floor is only 1.2% away, it has held at 257.04, 259.05 and 258.24, and with event gaps in the window, one close below it is a noisy signal, not a clean trend break.

So my neutral adjustment to the AMZN plan is this. If the caller is at a standard weight, trim 45–50% of the current holding at market now, targeting roughly 50–55% of a standard AMZN weight. Complete at least 80% of the intended reduction at market before the NVDA and inflation prints. If you want to leave a sell-limit at 265.53, cap it at 10–15% of the intended sale and treat it as opportunistic extra trimming into strength, not as a required part of the de-risk. No tactical short. Keep the residual long because the trend structure and operating inflection are intact, but size it so a gap does not force action. Treat 266.10 as a rebuild trigger, not a stop-loss. If AMZN closes below 257.04, cut the residual again by half or to a token/underweight position, and use 250.88 as the hard risk limit for what remains. Rebuild only on a daily close above 266.10 with volume above 40M, and do it in thirds rather than all at once. If the caller is already at or below half weight, do not add. If the caller is flat, this is not an instruction to initiate a short.

That is the sustainable middle for AMZN: more de-risking than the aggressive plan, less forced liquidation than the conservative plan, no new short, and a residual position that still respects the genuine operating improvement and the rising medium- and long-term trend. It protects against the downside resolution without pretending the upside resolution is impossible.
