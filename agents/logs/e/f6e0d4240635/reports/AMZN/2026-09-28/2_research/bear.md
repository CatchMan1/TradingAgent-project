
Bear Analyst: **Bear rebuttal: AMZN — good company, bad setup at $246.15**

Bull, I’ll grant you the operating business is not broken. AMZN’s Q2 revenue growth of **+19.6%** and operating income growth of **+43.2%** are genuinely impressive. But you’re making a classic equity mistake: **you’re giving AMZN credit for being a great business while asking investors to ignore the cash-flow, balance-sheet, and technical evidence that says the stock is not a good risk here.**

You framed this as “a timing problem, not a thesis problem.” For AMZN at **$246.15**, timing and thesis are now the same problem. The stock is below the **50-SMA at $256.15**, below the **10-EMA at $250.94**, below the **VWMA at $251.72**, below the **20-SMA at $253.29**, with a failed bounce, a failed MACD reset, and negative trailing free cash flow. That is not a coiled spring by default; it is a market that has repeatedly rejected higher prices.

### 1) Your FCF defense is the weakest part of the bull case

You say AMZN’s **TTM FCF of −$11.6B** is just an investment-cycle issue, and that “if capex merely stops accelerating, FCF snaps positive quickly.”

Let’s test that with the actual numbers.

- TTM operating cash flow: **$161.4B**
- TTM capex: **$173.0B**
- Q2 capex: **$54.2B**, or **27% of revenue**
- Capex/D&A: **2.30x**
- H1 FCF: **−$27.0B**

If Q2’s **$54.2B** capex simply stops accelerating and stays at that quarterly run-rate, annualized capex is roughly **$217B** — far above TTM OCF of **$161.4B**. That is not a path to “FCF snaps positive quickly.” That is a path to a **~$55B annual FCF hole** before finance leases, unless OCF accelerates dramatically or capex actually falls.

And even if capex flattens, the depreciation bill from past spending keeps arriving. D&A already grew **+31.3% YoY** in Q2 and is now **10.0% of revenue**, up from 9.1%. With capex/D&A at **2.30x**, future depreciation is effectively pre-loaded. So the bull case requires not just “capex stops accelerating,” but **capex decelerates materially while AWS/AI revenue converts into high returns**. That is an assumption, not a reported fact.

That is why the ROIC trend matters: estimated ROIC is around **12.3%**, down from roughly **15%** a year earlier. AMZN is getting bigger and more capital-intensive while returns on that capital are diluting. That is a real thesis issue, not a timing nuisance.

### 2) “The balance sheet can fund it” is true — but the margin for error is shrinking

You cite **$176.1B** of cash and securities, total debt/OCF of **0.91x**, and interest coverage of **24.3x**. Fine. AMZN is not in distress. But look at the direction:

- Long-term debt: **+93.2%** to **$133.0B** in six months
- Total debt including finance leases: **+80.5%** to **$146.4B**
- Q2 interest expense: **+155% YoY**
- Interest coverage: down from **35.5x** to **24.3x**
- Net cash cushion: roughly halved
- Adjusted net debt including operating leases: **+$28.9B** to about **$66.7B**
- Total contractual obligations: **$650B**, with **$78.1B due within 12 months**
- Current ratio: **1.03x**, working capital only **$8.0B**

You say the balance sheet “can carry it.” I agree it can carry a lot. But the bull thesis needs AMZN to carry this capex cycle **without a stumble**. The data shows less slack, rising interest cost, rising D&A, and **$650B** of forward commitments. That is not a crisis, but it is exactly the kind of setup where a demand disappointment or a private-mark reversal hits both earnings and sentiment at the same time.

### 3) The GAAP EPS issue cuts against you more than you admit

You correctly note that reported Q2 EPS of **$5.75** was inflated by a roughly **$50.5B** non-cash equity remeasurement, with core EPS closer to **$2.15**. I’ll give you that. But the bear case is not “AMZN’s EPS is fake.” The bear case is that AMZN’s **reported earnings and book value are becoming more sensitive to private-market marks and accounting noise**, while its cash flow is going the wrong way.

Look at the balance sheet:

- Non-marketable equity securities: **$122.3B**, up from **$16.2B** at year-end
- That is roughly **11% of total assets**
- Deferred tax expense jumped to **$30.5B** in H1 2026
- Effective tax rate normalized to **23.0%** from **17.0%** a year ago

So yes, headline EPS is noisy. But that noise can reverse. A private-mark write-down would hit reported earnings and book value. Meanwhile, the tax rate normalization is a multi-billion-dollar headwind that the bull case cannot wave away.

You also cite core EPS growth of **+28%** in Q2 and **+25%** in H1. That is solid. But it is not enough by itself. The stock needs the cash-flow inflection and the return-on-capital stabilization. Right now, AMZN is producing good core operating growth while **burning free cash flow and adding debt**. That is the definition of a capex supercycle that has not yet proven its payoff.

### My bear conclusion on AMZN

AMZN is a high-quality company. I am not arguing otherwise. But the equity at **$246.15** is not offering a bear-resistant setup. The bull case requires three optimistic assumptions to break in its favor:

1. The **$244.30** support shelf holds.
2. The capex cycle peaks soon enough to turn FCF positive.
3. AWS/AI demand converts into returns before depreciation and interest pressure bite.

The reported fundamentals show only the first is testable in real time, and the technicals show the market has repeatedly rejected higher prices. Until AMZN reclaims **$253.29** and then **$256.15** on convincing volume, or until capex/FCF actually inflects in the reported numbers, I would **avoid or underweight AMZN**. A confirmed close below **$244.30** on volume above **45M** would validate the next leg lower, with targets at **$240.94**, **$235.50**, and then the **$226–234** volume shelf.

The bull is right that AMZN is not broken. But “not broken” is not the same as “buy.” Right now, the burden of proof sits with the bulls, and AMZN has not cleared it.
