Bull Analyst: Bull opening statement — AMZN: own the operating engine, not the accounting noise

I’ll start with the punchline: AMZN is a dominant, accelerating operating franchise trading inside an intact primary uptrend, and the bear case is focused on timing and accounting distortions rather than business deterioration. At $258.90, I’m not pounding the table for a chase—but I am firmly on the bull side of the investment debate.

Bear, if your case is going to be “negative free cash flow, debt is up, GAAP EPS is inflated, and the chart is below the VWMA,” I’ve heard it—and I think it misses the forest for the trees.

1. Growth: AMZN is accelerating, not maturing

The latest 10-Q shows something rare for a company this size: top-line acceleration with simultaneous margin expansion.

  • Q2 2026 revenue: ~$200.6B, +19.6% y/y (derived from costs + operating income)
  • H1 2026 revenue: ~$382.1B, +18.2% y/y
  • Q2 operating income: $27.46B, +43.2% y/y
  • Q2 operating margin: 13.7%, +226 bps y/y
  • H1 operating income: $51.31B, +36.6% y/y
  • H1 operating margin: 13.4%, +181 bps y/y
  • Q2 gross margin: 52.3%, +45 bps y/y
  • H1 gross margin: 52.1%, +84 bps y/y

That is not a company struggling to scale. That is a company whose retail logistics, advertising, and AWS mix are all feeding operating leverage.

And look at cost discipline, which is where the bear argument usually goes quiet:

  • Marketing expense: +2.5% y/y against revenue +19.6%
  • G&A: –6.0% y/y in absolute dollars
  • SBC: –7.6% y/y in Q2, falling from 3.90% to 3.01% of revenue
  • Unrecognized SBC: $24.8B, already known and declining as a percentage of revenue

AMZN is not buying growth with runaway expenses. It is squeezing controllable costs while growing revenue high-teens. That is the definition of operating leverage.

2. The “bad earnings quality” bear point actually supports the bull case—if you adjust correctly

Yes, the bear is right that reported EPS is heavily inflated by non-cash marks:

  • Q2 2026 non-operating income: $53.4B
  • Of that, $50.5B was a non-cash upward price adjustment on non-marketable equity securities
  • H1 2026 equity mark-ups: $62.8B, about 52% of pre-tax income
  • Reported Q2 diluted EPS: $5.75
  • My adjusted “core” Q2 EPS: ~$2.1
  • Reported H1 diluted EPS: $8.53
  • Core H1 EPS: ~$4.0

So I accept the criticism: GAAP EPS is optically inflated. But that cuts against the bear, not for it. The bear wants to say the earnings aren’t real. Fine—underwrite the core. Core EPS was still up roughly +24% y/y in Q2 and +22% in H1. The operating business is growing even after removing the mark-to-market candy.

And critically, those equity marks are non-cash and do not flow into operating cash flow. They can reverse, but they do not impair AMZN’s ability to generate cash from selling goods, ads, cloud capacity, and Prime subscriptions.
