**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: I went into this expecting the bear to have to beat a genuinely strong operating quarter, and the bull does deserve credit on that ground: Q2 FY2026 revenue of $200.6B at +19.6% YoY (accelerating from +16.6%), operating income $27.5B at +43.2%, operating margin 13.69% up 226 bps, and gross margin 52.26% up 45 bps — all while D&A rose ~31%. That is real, and it is why this is an Underweight and not a Sell. The bull is also right that reported TTM EPS of $12.42 is inflated by a ~$4.44/share non-cash PE revaluation and that normalized TTM EPS near $7.98 still implies core EPS growth of roughly +25–29%. So the business is not deteriorating.

But on the exchanges that actually decide the call, the bear won, and won on specifics rather than tone.

First, the bull's central growth pillar collapsed under cross-examination. The bull called the $650B contractual-obligation book "committed demand and a visible buildout runway." The bear correctly identified these as purchase obligations — money AMZN has promised to pay suppliers for chips, content and compute — and pointed out that the actual customer-commitment line, deferred revenue, is flat at ~$25.0B. That is the opposite of demand, and the bull never recovered the point. With $78.1B of those obligations due inside 12 months against Q2 FCF of −$8.8B, the "runway" is a fixed claim on a currently negative cash generator.

Second, the bull conceded the cash-flow and leverage facts and then argued around them. "Guilty" on negative FCF, "yes" on debt nearly doubling to $133B — then back to "capex is the input, not a leak." The bear's arithmetic is the one I'll underwrite: capex at 101% of adjusted EBITDA ($54.2B vs $53.5B in Q2), capex at 27% of revenue versus 19.2% a year ago, H1 capex +72%, $64.2B of debt added in six months, long-term debt +93%, interest expense +155% in Q2. Adjusted OCF of ~$120B against $173B of capex is roughly −$53B of adjusted FCF, and the funding mechanism is borrowing. The bull's defence — "the core business already covers the investment" — is contradicted by capex exceeding the entire adjusted EBITDA line.

Third, valuation was asked and never answered. The bear put the honest multiple at ~32x trailing normalized EPS ($258.45 / $7.98) for a negative-FCF, relevering, 27%-of-revenue-capex business. The bull's entire rebuttal consisted of price levels — 259.77, 265.9–267.6, 271.9 — with no target multiple, no forward EPS, no return-on-capex estimate. When one side names a price and the other only names levels, the price side carries.

Fourth, the technical reading favours the bear, and it matters because the bull's "favorable risk/reward" was a load-bearing claim. Price at 258.45 is inside the unfilled 254.83–259.77 gap, not above it. MACD is below zero and below signal, which the report itself classifies as mean-reversion rather than trend. The 10-EMA (253.92) sits below the 50-SMA (256.10); VWMA (253.75) is below the 50-SMA; the 50-SMA is flattening into a "weak support shelf." The structure is lower highs (284.02 → 266.43 → 258.90 → 256.78) and lower lows, with no bullish divergence at the 9/16 low. And the arithmetic on the bull's own trade is damning: buy 259.77, stop 253.60 = 6.17 points of risk against 6.13 points to the first target — roughly 1:1 on a range that has already failed repeatedly. The report's actual repair threshold is 266.4–267.6, roughly 3% above the bull's trigger.

Fifth, quality of earnings cuts against the bull in a way he underweighted. The $122.3B non-marketable equity stake (up from $16.2B, $62.8B of mark-to-model revaluation in H1, ~11% of total assets) and the AOCI build of +$38.1B against only −$1.56B of FX mean both the EPS and the book value anchors the bull leans on are propped by the same non-cash mark. That is correlated accounting risk, not optionality.

What keeps this at Underweight rather than Sell: the operating momentum is genuinely accelerating, the −64 day cash conversion cycle and $176.1B of cash and securities give real flexibility, price is still 7.5% above a rising 200-SMA with the 50 above the 200, and a mark-to-model headline-EPS air pocket is a multiple event, not a solvency event — which makes an outright short a poor risk shape against a $122B stake portfolio and earnings-gap risk. So: reduce exposure and sell strength, don't press a short.

Evidence I could not verify and would flag to the trader: AWS segment revenue and AWS operating income are absent, so the bull's own "AWS re-accelerating above 25%" test cannot be measured, and neither can capex return on investment; there is no capex guidance figure, no forward consensus EPS, and no quantification of the pregnancy-litigation, cargo-crash or minimum-wage items. Anyone upgrading this call needs those numbers.

**Strategic Actions**: Sizing frame: standard allocation = benchmark weight for AMZN. Target ~50–60% of that weight (i.e., trim roughly 40–50% of a full allocation), retaining a residual core position rather than exiting.

1) Reduce now, at market. Sell down to ~50–60% of standard weight at the current ~258.45. Do not wait for the 259.77 gap-fill to start trimming — that level is a gap-fill, not a trend confirmation, and the setup pays roughly 1:1 from there.

2) Sell strength into 265.9–267.6. This is the upper-band / August-high confluence and where the bear's risk/reward inverts. Use any push into that zone to take the residual back toward ~40% of standard weight. Only reverse that instruction if price closes decisively above 267.6 on expanding volume with VWMA turning up through the 50-SMA.

3) No new longs above 259.77 on the bull's trigger. If the desk wants tactical long exposure, cap it at a small trading sleeve (no more than ~25% of standard weight), require volume ≥40M and an actual MACD/signal cross above zero, and use 253.60 as a hard stop. Treat it as a trade, not a position.

4) Downside ladder for further de-risking: a close below 253.60 (breaks the 10-EMA/VWMA/50-SMA cluster) → trim another ~10% of standard weight. Below 250.5–251.9 → move toward ~30%. Below 244.30 (swing low) → move to minimum residual (~20%) and reassess; a close below 240.4 (200-SMA) would be the first primary-trend breach since spring and argues for flat.

5) Upgrade conditions — require all of these before rebuilding toward or above benchmark weight: (a) decisive close above 267.6 on expanding volume with VWMA back above the 50-SMA; (b) evidence of a capex plateau or decelerating capex growth with D&A growth falling below gross-profit growth for two consecutive quarters; (c) growth in deferred revenue or a disclosed customer backlog line — not purchase obligations — demonstrating demand rather than obligations; (d) AWS segment revenue and operating income disclosed and re-accelerating. Items (b) and (d) cannot be measured on the current evidence set — flag that explicitly to the PM.

6) Risk management on the residual: no naked short. Express the view through underweighting and trimming, not inverse exposure — a $122B mark-to-model equity stake plus Q3 earnings-gap risk creates squeeze and gap asymmetry against shorts. If hedging the residual, prefer defined-risk put spreads and size for overnight event risk rather than relying on ATR (~1.5x ATR ≈ 249.4 understates gap risk). Also flag headline-EPS volatility: a markdown of the private-stake revaluation would be a non-cash air pocket, and should not be read as a fundamental deterioration — or as a reason to add.

7) Watch items to report back: any new debt issuance and its spread/coverage versus hyperscaler comps; whether the $78.1B of near-term purchase obligations gets funded from operating cash flow or incremental borrowings; any quantified outcome on the pregnancy-accommodation litigation or the minimum-wage decision.
