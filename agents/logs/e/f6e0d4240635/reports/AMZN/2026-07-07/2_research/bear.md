
Bear Analyst: **Bear Analyst — Rebuttal on AMZN**

Bull, you gave a polished pitch, but you’re asking people to buy **AMZN at $245.98** — right underneath a falling 50-day, with negative TTM free cash flow, a $53.8B one-quarter debt raise, and headline EPS that is flattered by a non-cash private-stake mark. That’s not a “coiled spring.” That’s a stock where the burden of proof has not been met.

Let me be clear: **AMZN is not a broken company.** The operating numbers are good. But the bear case isn’t “Amazon is dying.” The bear case is: **at this price, with this cash-flow profile and this technical setup, the risk/reward is poor, and the bull is extrapolating optionality into earnings that haven’t arrived.**

### 1. “Operating engine accelerating” — yes, but operating income is not free cash flow

You led with revenue +16.6% and operating income +29.6%. Fine. That is genuinely strong. But you skipped the line that matters for equity value: **cash conversion.**

- TTM operating cash flow: **$148.5B**
- TTM capex: **$151.0B**
- TTM free cash flow: **≈ -$2.47B**

That’s negative. And it’s not a one-quarter blip. FY2025 FCF collapsed to **$7.7B** from **$32.9B** in FY2024. Q1 capex was **$44.2B, +77% YoY** — higher than Q1 operating cash flow of $26.0B. The capex line is growing faster than the cash generation line.

You call that “deliberate high-return capital deployment.” Maybe. But “deliberate” is not the same as “proven.” There is no evidence in this dataset that the AI/data-center capex is earning an adequate return yet. What we *do* see is the cost side arriving: TTM depreciation and amortization is already **$70.4B**, and Q1 D&A rose to **$18.9B from $14.3B** a year ago. That’s a future earnings drag baked in.

So when you say “negative FCF because they’re building the future,” my response is: **great — show me the return on that build.** Until then, investors are underwriting a capex super-cycle with a negative FCF print.

### 2. The “fortress balance sheet” is getting more leveraged, not less

You waved off the debt raise because AMZN has ~$144B of cash and securities. True — liquidity is strong. But look at the direction:

- Long-term debt: **$68.8B → $122.6B in one quarter**, a **$53.8B increase**
- Interest expense: **$800M in Q1, +48% YoY**
- Operating + finance lease liabilities: **~$105B**
- Total contracted obligations: **$569.3B**, with **$57.1B due within 12 months**

That is not distress — but it is **less room for error**. You’re framing a $53B debt raise in a single quarter as “funded from strength.” I’d frame it as **capital-markets dependency** for a capex program that is already consuming all of operating cash flow and then some. If AI demand disappoints or rates stay higher for longer, the fixed-cost stack — depreciation, interest, leases — becomes a real drag on EPS and FCF.

### 3. Earnings quality and valuation: the bull is using the Anthropic stake both ways

You correctly noted that headline EPS of **$2.78** includes a **~$12.3B pre-tax unrealized gain** on the Anthropic stake. Adjusted EPS is roughly **$1.92**. You say that’s still +21% YoY — fair. But let’s put that in context.

Using the report’s illustrative adjusted TTM EPS of roughly **$6.60**, **AMZN at $245.98 trades at about 37x adjusted earnings.** That is not cheap for a company with **negative TTM FCF** and a capex program that is still accelerating. On headline EPS it looks cheaper, but headline EPS is exactly the number you told us to strip out.

And on the Anthropic stake: you want to count the **$48.1B** book value as strategic optionality, but you can’t also ignore that it is a **private, non-marketable, mark-to-market asset** that can reverse. The news report itself flags that the Q1 outperformance was tied to an “unusual driver” — a non-operating item. That cuts both ways. If private AI valuations cool, that stake becomes an accounting headwind, not a moat.

If you buy now and use the 200 SMA as your invalidation, you’re risking more to the downside than the first leg of upside offers. That’s not a coiled spring; that’s a **show-me setup where the easy money has already been made off the $227 low.**

### 5. Competition is not “background” when AWS is the profit engine

You dismissed Meta’s cloud ambitions, Azure share gains, and SpaceX’s Starmind as long-horizon risks. But AWS is the profit engine of AMZN. If Azure is gaining share per Jefferies, and Meta is entering the cloud conversation with a $9% single-day move on the report, that is not irrelevant. Cloud is an expanding pie, yes — but **share and pricing power matter** when the market is paying a premium multiple for the profit pool.

Same with Kuiper. Crossing **390 LEO satellites** is a real milestone, but it is capex-heavy and facing a well-funded SpaceX “Starmind” constellation. That adds to the FCF pressure rather than relieving it. Trainium third-party sales are a “could” — speculative, unconfirmed, and not in the numbers. The $1B forward-deployed-engineer buildout is strategically interesting, but it’s also an **opex drag** with multi-quarter payoff.

And the news tape itself is thin: only **20 of 172 records**, all timestamped **July 6**, with no coverage from June 30 to July 5, summary-only evidence, and no social sentiment report available. The “Goldman endorsement” is an opinion piece. The “narrative shift” you’re leaning on is not robustly evidenced.

AMZN may well be a great company. But at **$245.98**, the stock is:

- Below a falling 50 SMA at **$255**
- Capped by the **$248.9–$250.6** confluence
- Rebounding on **light volume**
- Carrying **negative TTM FCF**

So my recommendation is: **avoid/underweight AMZN at current levels until the burden of proof is met.** I want to see a **volume-confirmed daily close above $250.6**, then a reclaim of **$255**, with evidence that the capex cycle is translating into FCF rather than just depreciation and interest. If AMZN loses **$233**, the uptrend structure is broken and the June low at **$225.55–$227.01** comes back into play.
